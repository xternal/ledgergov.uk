Serious offenders detained in hospital will no longer receive benefits
Serious offenders detained for treatment under a Hospital Order will no longer receive taxpayer-funded benefits worth hundreds of pounds a month.
Change ends “double provision,” whereby offenders have their living costs met by the NHS while also claiming benefits at the taxpayer’s expense.
Follows direct engagement with victims’ families who campaigned to close the loophole and a range of experts.
Under the current system, an offender can be paid over £800 a month in Universal Credit – amounting to thousands in taxpayer cash over several years. The proposed change is expected to save tens of millions of pounds each year.
As part of ongoing action to put fairness back at the heart of the welfare system, this intervention will stop hundreds of offenders receiving benefits from early next year – those already in hospital and future cases.
Those transferred to mental health hospitals from prison are already banned from claiming. The regulations laid today will extend this to mental health hospital patients that are guilty of a dozen serious offences – focusing on violent and sexual offences.
The move will end the so-called “double provision” where people detained under a hospital order can receive benefits despite already having their bed and board fully funded by the taxpayer through the NHS.
Families of victims in high-profile cases - including the loved ones of Kennedi Westcarr-Sabaroche and the victims of the Nottingham attacks, Grace O’Malley-Kumar, Barnaby Webber, and Ian Coates - have bravely highlighted the loophole which sees offenders continuing to receive benefit payments.
The change comes after extensive engagement between the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions and a range of experts and victims directly affected.
Secretary of State for Work and Pensions, Pat McFadden said:
We said we’d act to tackle this gross unfairness and we’re delivering on that promise.
For too long these violent criminals have had their living costs paid by the taxpayer twice over, while their victims and families paid the price.
That ends today because victims and taxpayers deserve a system that is fair and supports only those who genuinely need it.
The decision to remove benefits from serious offenders will not affect people currently detained under the Mental Health Act 1983, who have not committed any offence.
Anyone detained under a hospital order for any reason will continue to receive appropriate mental health support.
The regulations deliver on the promise made earlier this year to close the loophole, marking the latest step in reforming the welfare system to put fairness at its core, while ensuring it works for the people who need it most.

Additional Information:
The Regulations will apply to individuals subject to a Section 37 order under the Mental Health Act 1983 and a Section 57A order under the Criminal Procedure (Scotland) Act 1995
The offences under scope for England and Wales (not including the Scots Law equivalents) are:
Manslaughter (whether voluntary or involuntary)
Attempted murder
Wounding (etc) with intent to cause grievous bodily harm (GBH)
Rape
Assault by penetration
Causing a person to engage in sexual activity without consent (aggravated – with penetration)
Rape of a child under 13
Sexual assault of a child under 13 (aggravated – with penetration)
Causing or inciting a child under 13 to engage in sexual activity (aggravated – with penetration)
Sexual activity with a child
Familial sexual offences (sexual activity with a child family member) (offender 18 or over)
Familial sexual offences (inciting a child family member to engage in sexual activity) (offender 18 or over)
Murder is not included because a Hospital Order is not an available disposal for the offence of murder which attracts a mandatory life sentence.
The affirmative regulations will be laid in Parliament on 20 October and remain subject to parliamentary approval.
Without these regulations, an offender on Universal Credit, in a single household, aged over 25 and receiving the LCWRA rate, could receive over £800 a month.
The change is expected affect some 2,500 offenders already in hospital and approximately 200 a year in future.
Removal of these benefits will save in the low tens of millions of pounds each year with full financial impacts to be published as part of the 2026 budget.
Individuals in scope will no longer be entitled to either Universal Credit, Pension Credit, State Pension and Employment and Support Allowance while they remain detained in hospital.
The NHS will continue to provide appropriate mental health support.
The policy follows engagement with clinicians, victims’ groups, mental health experts and the devolved governments and the decision to remove benefits from serious offenders will not affect anyone currently detained under the Mental Health Act 1983, who has not committed an offence.
The change applies only to those convicted of a serious offence as defined in the regulations; it does not affect anyone on remand, unfit to plead, awaiting trial or those convicted of offences that are not detailed in the prescribed list.
While the change only comes into effect early next year, it will apply to both existing and future cases from the point that the regulations come into force, regardless of when their conviction was made.
