VAT (Drugs and Medicines) (Health and Welfare) Order 2026
Who is likely to be affected
Community pharmacies, registered pharmacists and registered pharmacy technicians in England, Scotland and Wales.
General description of the measure
This measure amends the VAT zero rate for medicines to include qualifying medicines dispensed by a registered pharmacy technician or by other pharmacy staff under their supervision. In both cases, the registered pharmacy technician must be acting under the authorisation of a registered pharmacist.
This measure means that qualifying medicines dispensed under these arrangements receive the same VAT treatment as medicines dispensed by a registered pharmacist.
Policy objective
The policy objective is to make sure that the VAT treatment of prescribed medicines keeps pace with Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC) pharmacy reforms by providing for the same VAT treatment for medicines dispensed by a registered pharmacist to those dispensed by a registered pharmacy technician or someone they supervise under the authorisation of a registered pharmacist.
This will remove a potential VAT barrier to the expansion of community-based pharmacy services, helping to reduce pressure on GP services and the wider NHS.
Background to the measure
The VAT zero rate for medicines is set out in Group 12 to Schedule 8 Value Added Tax Act 1994. Item 1 provides for the zero rating of qualifying goods dispensed to an individual by a registered pharmacist for that individual’s personal use on a prescription of an appropriate practitioner. A temporary zero rate applies under item 1A to qualifying goods supplied to an individual for their personal use in accordance with a patient group direction.
On 10 December 2026, new legislation will come into effect to expand the role of registered pharmacy technicians, allowing them to dispense medicines and supervise dispensing under the authorisation of a registered pharmacist.
The government is amending VAT legislation to make sure that medicines dispensed under these new arrangements continue to qualify for the zero rate. Without this amendment, these supplies would fall outside the scope of the existing VAT zero rate.
The measure applies to England, Wales and Scotland, in light of legislation introduced by the DHSC. The measure will not apply in Northern Ireland until ‘pharmacy technician’ becomes a registered profession in Northern Ireland and further legislation is made to bring the measure into effect.
The measure maintains the current policy intent and does not extend the scope of zero rating. The measure is a consequential change, so no formal consultation was undertaken.
Detailed proposal
Operative date
The measure will have effect for supplies made on or after 10 December 2026.
Current law
The measure will amend the VAT zero rate for medicines. Current law is contained in items 1 and 1A of Group 12 to Schedule 8 of the VAT Act 1994.
Proposed revisions
Provision will be made by statutory instrument under section 30(4) and 96(9) of the Value Added Tax Act 1994 to amend Group 12 of Schedule 8 Value Added Tax Act 1994. The measure will amend Note 2D of Group 12 to Schedule 8 to the Value Added Tax Act 1994 so that the zero rate applies to the supply of qualifying goods dispensed to an individual for that individual’s personal use on the prescription of an appropriate practitioner or on a patient group direction, where the dispensing is carried out either by:
a registered pharmacy technician acting under the authorisation of a registered pharmacist
other pharmacy staff acting under the supervision of a registered pharmacy technician who is authorised by a registered pharmacist

This measure is not expected to have an Exchequer impact.
Economic impact
The measure is not expected to have any significant macroeconomic impacts.
Impact on individuals, households and families
This measure is expected to have no impact on individuals, households or families as it does not change the provision of or access to pharmacy services and maintains consistent prescription charges between medicines dispensed by registered pharmacists and those dispensed by or under the supervision of a registered pharmacy technician. There are no changes to individuals tax obligations or processes.
Equalities impacts
Updating the scope of the VAT zero rate to include medicines dispensed by or under the supervision of registered pharmacy technicians is an enabling measure to support DHSC reform and expansion of community-based pharmacy services, providing VAT parity with medicines dispensed by a registered pharmacist.
The measure will not otherwise affect provision of or access to pharmacy services, independent of the DHSC reform. It is therefore not anticipated that there will be impacts on those groups sharing protected characteristics.
Administrative impact on business including civil society organisations
This measure is expected to have a negligible impact on administrative burden costs for approximately 10,000 pharmacies to ensure that medicines dispensed by or under the supervision of registered pharmacy technicians are treated the same as those dispensed by registered pharmacists.
This measure will support DHSC reform and expansion of community-based pharmacy services and will not affect the provision of or access to pharmacy services. One-off costs will include familiarisation with the change and updating processes and systems relating to VAT accounting and coding. There are expected to be no ongoing costs.
This measure will have no impact on businesses’ experience of dealing with HMRC as it does not change any obligations or processes. This measure will have no impact on civil society organisations, as almost all community pharmacies are privately owned businesses.
Operational impact
HMRC will not incur any extra costs implementing this change.
Other impacts
Other impacts have been considered, and none have been identified.
Monitoring and evaluation
Consideration will be given to monitoring this measure through information collected from existing HMRC compliance processes.
