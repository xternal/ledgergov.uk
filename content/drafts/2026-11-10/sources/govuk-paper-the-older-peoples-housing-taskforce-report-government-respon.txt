The Older People's Housing Taskforce Report: government response
Ministerial forewords
Housing shapes every stage of our lives. As our population ages, it becomes even more important that people live in homes that support their health, their wellbeing and their independence.
England’s population is changing. More people are living longer, and many of us will require different types of homes as we age. Yet there are many older people who live in homes that no longer meet their needs, while others struggle to access the housing options that could help them live more comfortably, safely and independently. Meeting this challenge is not only important for older people and their families – it is also essential to creating a housing system that works for everyone.
I thank the Older People’s Housing Taskforce and its chair, Professor Julienne Meyer, for their valuable work and thoughtful recommendations. Bringing together expertise from across housing, health and social care, their report has made an important contribution to the conversation about how we can best respond to the housing needs of older people.
I am delighted to present the government’s formal response to the Taskforce, jointly with the Minister of State for Social Care, Alison McGovern MP. We are already taking forward significant reforms that will help foster a better housing system for an ageing society. Through planning reform, investment in social and affordable housing, leasehold and commonhold reform and support for home adaptations, we are laying the groundwork for change. But we know that these measures alone will not be enough.
That is why our response also looks ahead. It will be followed by a wider roadmap, setting out how the whole of government intends to take forward this work in the years to come – working with partners across local government, housing providers, developers, the health and care sectors, and crucially, older people themselves.
This response marks an important first step towards a housing system that better supports people throughout later life, and genuinely reflects the needs and aspirations of our ageing population. Together, we will deliver that change.
Baroness Taylor of Stevenage OBE
Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State (Lords Minister for Housing and Local Government)
Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government
There is no place like home. It provides the safety and comfort we all need to be able to live long, fulfilling lives.
For older people, getting the home environment right is more than a matter of safety and comfort. Housing is a wider determinant of health. High-quality, safe and suitable homes help people stay independent and healthy for longer. Suitable housing helps reduce the need to draw on health and social care services. The right housing is beneficial to both the individual and to society as a whole.
We know that unsuitable housing negatively impacts people’s health and can limit independent living, creating pressures and incurring costs to the NHS and social care. This must stop. In line with the commitments in the 10 Year Health Plan, we are radically transforming the NHS, including major shifts from hospital to community and from treatment to prevention, with more proactive and preventative services helping people to stay independent for longer. Housing needs to be an integral part of these changes, and our work to transform both the NHS and social care will not work if we do not get housing arrangements right for older people.
In the first days of his premiership, the Prime Minister set out his vision for rapid progress on reform of adult social care – which is about building a social care system that is person-centred, highly integrated with health services and preventative in its character. Baroness Casey’s independent commission into adult social care is leading work to develop recommendations for the long-term reform of adult social care and the future National Care Service.
I would like to take this opportunity to thank members of the independent Older People’s Housing Taskforce for contributing their unrivalled expertise and unremitted effort in preparing their report and its recommendations. My Department is working closely with the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government to consider how we can use the findings from this report to inform our plans to transform health and social care in this country. This response outlines the actions we are already taking, together with the sector, to realise the government’s vision – for every older person to live in a home that suits their needs, their health and their independence, supporting them to age in their community.

Government’s vision
Housing and opportunity are deeply entwined. But just as it is essential for providing people with the best start in life, it is equally as important as people grow older, helping them to age with dignity and in good health.
The government’s vision is for every older person to live in a home that suits their needs, their health and their independence, supporting them to age well in their community. We want a housing market that embraces ageing and offers choice for people, providing a wide range of different types and tenures of homes that reflects the diversity of our ageing population. We want older people to feel connected to their communities and the local services that matter to them, living side by side with people of all different ages rather than cut off or left on the fringes. Importantly, we want older people and their families to have access to the right resources that support them to plan and make informed decisions on where they want to live.
It is important that we get this right. Living in a home that meets people’s needs can improve their health – reducing the likelihood of falls and hospital admissions and delaying the point at which they may need to enter residential care. The right home in the right place can help older people to maintain their independence, reducing feelings of loneliness and social isolation that are all too common as people age. All of this can fundamentally enhance quality of life, supporting older people to live longer, fulfilling lives.
We cannot achieve our vision alone. To fulfil this ambition, we need a housing system that puts the needs of our ageing population at the heart of its decision-making. The older people’s housing sector already plays a critical role in supporting older people’s independence, and the government wants to see the sector continue to grow and thrive. But we need the wider system to be planning for the needs of older people, too – such as our housebuilders, and our town and transport planners – working together to offer older people more choice, more security and more routes into homes that meet their needs.
We also need a clear plan – one that supports the growth of the number of good-quality, age-friendly homes, equips local areas to plan for their ageing populations and improves public understanding. Building on the work of the Taskforce and the steps we have already taken, we intend to publish a roadmap in the next year. This will set out the actions we will take across government to achieve our vision, working with the sector to drive the change we want to see.
Core recommendations
The government welcomes the Taskforce’s report, and agrees with its analysis of the main issues impacting older people in the housing market. We have responded to the Taskforce’s 10 overarching recommendations below, and set out next steps on specific areas of policy, subject to further work and engagement.
1. Standardise definitions of older people’s housing
The government agrees that it is important to ensure there is a shared understanding across policy and regulatory frameworks – and most importantly with the public – of the variety of housing options for older people, and what these options offer.
This will require join-up across government and beyond; however, as a first step, we have acted to ensure the planning system effectively supports the provision of older people’s housing across a range of needs.
The new National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), published in August 2026, includes a range of reforms intended to support local authorities to plan for the needs of the ageing population (which we have detailed in response to Recommendation 6, ‘Strengthen planning policies’). In the response to the NPPF consultation, the government committed to publishing updated guidance in due course to assist local authorities in assessing the housing needs of older people. As part of this work, we will consider what additional clarity we can provide to local authorities on planning for specialist older persons’ housing need, including the definitions used.
As set out in our response to Recommendations 7 and 8, we are also looking at wider reforms to improve the information provided in the homebuying and selling process and enhance consumer protections. We will consider further work, alongside the Department of Health and Social Care, to understand what definitions are needed and align these across government, as well as encourage their adoption by the sector.
2. Incentivise a wide range of older people’s housing options
The government agrees that it is important to ensure we provide a wide variety of accessible and affordable housing options for older people, alongside support to adapt existing homes for those that cannot afford to make improvements themselves.
The Social and Affordable Homes Programme, with over £40 billion in funding, has been designed to be flexible to support a greater diversity of schemes to come forward, including supported housing that can include homes for older people, across a range of tenures. Beyond this, the government recognises that community-led housing can offer social benefits, such as social support and alleviating loneliness, at a lower cost to both residents and the taxpayer and provide people with the opportunity to directly shape development in their area. Co-housing schemes also provide opportunities for mutual support and intergenerational living.
To help the community-led housing sector grow, we have invested £20 million in a social finance fund that will provide capital finance to accelerate community-led housebuilding across England over the next decade. The Social and Affordable Homes Programme is also designed to support a greater diversity of supply, with flexibility in grant rates to help community-led schemes achieve viability. In addition, our reforms to the National Planning Policy Framework since 2024 will provide a further boost to the sector. These include broadening the definitions of organisations able to deliver community-led housing and providing greater clarity on how community-led exception sites should be supported. To go further, as part of our housing for an ageing population roadmap, we are exploring whether underused models, including community-led housing and co-housing, could help meet older people’s needs.
To improve the accessibility of existing homes of all tenures, the £723 million Disabled Facilities Grant for 2026-27 enables local authorities to support eligible people to fund adaptations up to the value of £30,000 to their homes, to make them safe and suitable for their needs – including in older age. A means test ensures this funding is focused on those who can least afford to adapt their home, although local authorities can agree a more flexible local policy on means testing and funding depending on local need. Following consultation in 2025, a new approach to allocating Disabled Facilities Grant funding to local authorities in England from 2026-27 will ensure the grant is better aligned with local needs.
To help households check whether they are eligible and find out how to apply, the government funds a national body – currently Foundations – which provides a free-to-use website for members of the public, ‘Adapt My Home’. This also has information for everyone who is considering adapting their home, including a decision tool to help work through the pros and cons of adapting or moving, and links to help find trusted builders and local sources of advice.
3. Ensure more housing is designed for later life
The government recognises the need to ensure that homes are designed to be age-friendly at the outset. We agree with the Taskforce’s assessment that by ensuring more homes are built to be accessible and age-inclusive, this can reduce the need for more costly retrofit later. This can also promote healthy ageing through maximising independence and minimising time in ill health, in line with the ambitions that we set out in our 10 Year Health Plan.
The updated National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) includes new requirements for plans to set out the proportion of new homes that should be delivered to M4(2) and M4(3) accessibility standards, in order to meet or exceed identified need. In relation to M4(2) housing, we have set a clear minimum whereby at least 40% of housing on major developments should be built to M4(2) or higher, where necessary to meet need. This seeks to formalise best practice and drive up provision in areas without clear requirements.
Our approach acknowledges that local authorities are best placed to assess accessible housing need in their areas and set out appropriate exemptions based on local circumstances. We are clear that this is a minimum floor, and not a target; many local plans already do, and should continue to, set higher targets where necessary to meet or exceed need.
The government also consulted on updated Design and Placemaking Planning Practice Guidance from 21 January to 10 March 2026, which embeds inclusive design and the needs of the ageing population in housebuilding and placemaking. We will publish our response later this year.
Model Design Codes put into practice the placemaking principles set out in the Design and Placemaking Planning Practice Guidance. They are standard design requirements that aim to improve design quality, reduce duplication and speed up decision making by providing clarity on what will be accepted. The government intends to publish a small number of Model Design Codes for development types where quality and consistency are most at risk. The first Model Design Codes will be for small urban sites and edge-of-settlement sites, and we intend to publish these early next year.
4. Create age-friendly, dementia-inclusive, faith and culture-sensitive communities
The government acknowledges the importance of ensuring that older people have access to local facilities, their families and their communities. This includes making sure that neighbourhoods are designed to be inclusive of changing needs as people age.
Ensuring the needs of older people are considered in planning and design decisions – around housing, transport, outdoor spaces and social inclusion – can also have wider benefits. Understanding population needs at an early stage, and designing liveable places that prioritise health and the needs of older people, can help people retain their independence as they age.
As above, we are taking forward updates to Design and Placemaking Planning Practice Guidance which embeds inclusive, age-friendly placemaking. Separately, subject to the outcomes of the consultation on the government’s New Towns Programme, New Towns will be consistent with the ambitious placemaking principles set out in the programme’s objectives and draft New Towns Planning Policy. This includes providing a diverse range of high-quality housing, with a mix of housing types and tenures to suit the needs of a balanced community. In the consultation, we also proposed to publish further detail on the New Towns placemaking principles alongside the programme, to support delivery bodies in meeting the programme’s placemaking objectives.
More broadly, neighbourhood health is a primary delivery vehicle for the government’s 10 Year Health Plan, bringing the 3 shifts – from hospital to community, analogue to digital, and treatment to prevention – into one coherent care model. This shift towards prevention and community-based care is central to helping older people stay well and live independently for longer, in their own homes and communities. It will ensure that services are offered as locally as possible – digitally by default; in a person’s home, if possible; in a neighbourhood health centre, when needed; and only in a hospital if necessary.
Neighbourhood health will be locally determined, with services shaped around what best serves local people, ensuring that areas are equipped to provide for the needs of their ageing population. It will also bring together integrated neighbourhood teams of professionals and partners closer to people’s homes – nurses, doctors, social care workers, pharmacists, health visitors and more – to work together to support people and places.
This sits alongside the government’s plans to develop a Modern Service Framework for Frailty and Dementia, which will seek to reduce geographic variation and inequalities by setting national standards for frailty and dementia care and support. While focused on health and adult social care, the Framework is relevant to older people’s housing, as dementia-inclusive homes, neighbourhoods and local services are vital to helping people live well, maintain independence and stay connected to their communities.
5. Expand older people’s housing at scale and ensure it is affordable to live in, and viable to finance, build and operate
The government acknowledges that more can be done to grow the purpose-built older people’s housing sector, and notes the Taskforce’s assessment of how we might learn from international comparators – such as New Zealand – to do so.
The Social and Affordable Homes Programme can be used to support the supply of social and affordable homes for older people, including housing with care, Older Persons Shared Ownership and supported housing. Beyond this, the government is considering how we may further support the sector to grow and increase supply as part of our housing for an ageing population roadmap.
Further to this, the government’s programme of leasehold and commonhold reform will support the growth of high‑quality older people’s housing. The Taskforce highlighted concerns relating to leasehold arrangements in the sector, and emphasised the importance of ensuring arrangements are fair and transparent for residents, while enabling sustainable funding and operation over the long term. As we take reforms forward, we will continue to consider how different tenure models can operate effectively in this sector, drawing on international experience where relevant. This work will be informed by ongoing engagement with industry.
6. Strengthen planning policies
The government agrees that planning policy can play an important role in encouraging a diverse range of homes to be built, including the development of purpose-built homes for older people and more accessible mainstream options.
The revised National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) goes further to set new requirements for local authorities to deliver more housing for older people. This includes requirements for authorities to identify sites for older persons’ housing, clearer weight for older persons’ housing need within planning policy, and new accessibility requirements for new homes (as outlined under Recommendation 3). It also introduces clearer national requirements for the delivery of specialist forms of accommodation, including specialist older persons’ housing, ensuring that this comes forward in appropriate locations and with the right access to services.
More widely, the updates we have made to transport planning policy through the NPPF place a greater emphasis on accessibility in the design of proposals, through meeting the needs of older people (as well as children and people with disabilities).
As set out in response to Recommendation 1, we have also committed to publishing updated guidance to assist local authorities in assessing the housing needs of different groups, including older people. We will publish the guidance in due course. Alongside this, the Planning Advisory Service will in due course be publishing information for Local Planning Authorities on planning use classes for older people’s housing.
7. Establish a national information platform and local hubs
The government notes the recommendation to provide older people with access to dedicated advice on housing options in their area. It is important that older people and their families understand the housing options available to them as they age, enabling them to make informed decisions for later life.
Our intention is that reforms to the homebuying and selling process will help older households looking to move to a home that suits their needs. These reforms include improving information provided in property listings and requiring sellers to prepare upfront sales packs so that prospective buyers have early access to comprehensive property information. This could support older buyers in assessing whether a property is accessible for them; and help tackle the lack of transparency sometimes seen around hidden costs that can be associated with purpose-built homes for older people.
We recognise that gaps remain in the availability and accessibility of advice at the local and national level, which is why we are exploring how government can best support the provision of advice as part of our upcoming roadmap on housing for an ageing population. This includes considering the role that neighbourhood health could play in bringing together health and care services with wider public services – such as housing support – for individuals, families and communities.
At Autumn Budget 2025, the government announced its commitment to deliver 250 neighbourhood health centres, with 120 delivered by 2030. We have now announced the first wave of schemes, with 27 sites across England that combine GP practices with a mix of community, local authority and voluntary sector services, which may include housing-related services.
8. Build consumer confidence
The government acknowledges that issues in the private, for-sale older people’s housing sector – including poor resale values and opaque event fees – have led to poor outcomes for residents and reduced both consumer and investor confidence.
In response, the government will take forward research into resale values of private older people’s housing, to deepen our understanding of the drivers behind resale values and why some models retain their value, while others do not.
More broadly, we recognise the Taskforce’s emphasis on the importance of transparency, fairness and consumer confidence across the full range of older people’s housing tenures, including rental and affordable housing.
Enhancing consumer protections in the for-sale market is a central objective of our programme of leasehold and commonhold reform, alongside supporting the continued growth of the sector and maintaining investor confidence. We are committed to ensuring that homeowners benefit from arrangements that are fair, transparent and offer appropriate protections, including in relation to fees, service charges and obligations at the point of purchase and resale. As we take reforms forward, we will consider how best to deliver a consistent baseline of consumer protection across the for-sale market. In doing so, we will continue to recognise the need for funding and operating arrangements that support the long-term sustainability of the full range of older people’s housing.
The government has also tightened expectations on landlords for new Shared Ownership homes, including Older Persons Shared Ownership. This includes expectations for landlords to have greater consideration of long-term affordability; to set fair and transparent fees that are not used for profit; and to provide customers with the ability to opt out of optional services. This will be applied to all new Older Persons Shared Ownership homes built through the Social and Affordable Homes Programme.
9. Enhance innovation, research and professional development
The government notes the recommendation to drive forward research and development to better support our ageing population and learn ‘what works’, including creating and funding a long-term research programme.
However, the government believes that this recommendation is best led by the sector. We are aware of numerous industry-led efforts to trial novel approaches and invest in new research, working across local and national networks. This includes programmes such as the Suitable Housing for an Ageing Population Evidence (SHAPE) Exchange, bringing partners together from housing, health and social care with older people and community organisations. Further local initiatives are currently underway to improve local join-up across housing, health and care more broadly, including co-location of services, which we are learning from. We encourage the sector to continue collaborating and sharing best practice.
As government, the Department of Health and Social Care (in collaboration with the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government) is commissioning research to explore how the Disabled Facilities Grant is delivered and how it is experienced by recipients. This will be used to help local authorities to improve delivery of home adaptations funded by the scheme, and enable us to understand how we can maximise the impact of this funding. Going further, we are considering how we might best support innovative approaches through development of our forthcoming roadmap – for example, exploring underused models to boost housing supply.
10. Create collective leadership to drive change
The government agrees with the value of setting out a clear plan to address the housing needs of our ageing population that is taken forward jointly across government and the sector. As part of this, we recognise the vital role that local authorities and mayors play in planning for the needs of their communities – not just in housing, but in health, social care and transport – working closely with local stakeholders.
The government notes the specific recommendation to establish a joint unit between the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government and the Department of Health and Social Care to take this work forward. Officials from both departments are already working closely together on this agenda, and so we do not feel this needs to be formalised through the creation of a joint unit. We will continue to work closely on development of our roadmap and provide the leadership needed to deliver the change we need to see. This will touch on a range of areas, including exploring what further support government can provide to help areas plan for their ageing population in a joined-up way.
Achieving our vision for neighbourhood health will equally rely on strong collaboration across organisational boundaries – between health and social care providers and local government, working alongside a full range of public services, civil society and wider partners.
More broadly, the government remains committed to reform and local government reorganisation, and achieving good growth requires a fundamental rewiring of the way our country works. A review of plans to streamline local government has been announced to ensure the process is robust and aligns with the new government’s plans to rewire the state, including opportunities to bring public health, adult social care and housing services under one roof.
Next steps
Achieving our vision will be a cross-government and cross-sector effort. The Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government and the Department of Health and Social Care will continue to spearhead this work jointly, working closely with wider government departments as we develop our roadmap. We will also collaborate with stakeholders across the sector, building on the expertise of the Taskforce to support a housing system that enables people to age with dignity, now and in the future.
