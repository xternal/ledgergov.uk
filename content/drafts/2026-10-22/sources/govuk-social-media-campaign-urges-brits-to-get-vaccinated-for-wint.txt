Social media campaign urges Brits to get vaccinated for winter
Flu vaccination drive a key part of NHS winter preparations
Social media campaigns and NHS App make booking easier than ever
10 million vaccinated and millions more encouraged to come forward for their free NHS flu vaccination
The NHS is urging millions of people eligible for a free flu vaccine to help protect themselves, their families and ease the pressure on England’s health services this winter.
Data released today shows more than 10 million flu vaccinations have been delivered since bookings first opened in August and vaccinations began in September - including 2.7 million this week.
Appointments are available for the millions more who are still eligible for their free NHS flu vaccine as the government, NHS and the UKHSA work together on new and exciting ways to encourage more people to come forward.
The government has launched a social media campaign running across Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, and - for the first time – TikTok and is also promoting a new feature on the NHS App which helps people check their eligibility and book their flu vaccination at a community pharmacy.
The ‘Manage a Vaccination in School’ (MAVIS) programme – which streamlines school-age immunisation programmes - has also been accelerated to cover 14,000 schools this year.
In a further effort to see more kids vaccinated against the flu, later this year the government will launch the first ever ‘catch-up’ programme from community pharmacies for school children who missed their vaccinations earlier in the year.
Making sure all those eligible, especially the most vulnerable, take up their flu vaccine is a key part of ensuring the health service prepares for one of our most challenging winters.
Health Minister James Frith said:
Vaccines save lives. It has never been easier to find out if you are eligible and get vaccinated, helping protect yourself and reduce pressure on the NHS during the winter season.
Eligibility is focused on those most vulnerable - from young children to older adults and people with long-term health conditions - so if you are eligible, please come forward this winter.
Flu is not just a bad cold. It can cause serious complications, hospital treatment and death, particularly for those most at risk including young children, pregnant women, older people, and people with certain long-term conditions.
Vaccination remains one of the best defences, helping prevent serious illness and avoidable admissions.

The NHS app is a fantastic and quick way to book, or you can contact your GP or find a pharmacy near you.

UKHSA estimates that flu vaccination prevented around 100,000 hospitalisations and 7,000 deaths in England in the last year.

Dr Agostinho Sousa, Head of Extreme Events and Health Protection at UKHSA, said:

While this estimate of cold-associated deaths is lower than we saw during the previous winter, these figures show that even short periods of cold weather can have serious effects on health and can lead to increased deaths in the days and weeks that follow.
As we head into winter, it is important that everyone takes simple steps to protect themselves and others. To help drive uptake for those eligible, there are more opportunities to get a vaccine than ever before. These include:
School teams have been asked to visit every school by the end of November, with second visits completed by 11 December. Parents can help by returning consent forms promptly and vaccinating children helps protect them, reduce flu spreading to others and limit disruption to learning.
Participating pharmacies offer vaccination to two- and three-year-olds and children aged two to 17 in clinical risk groups. School-age pharmacy catch-up starts on 1 December.
NHS teams are taking vaccines into pharmacies, schools, maternity services, care homes, pop-up clinics and mobile vans. Outreach teams and charity partners are reaching people sleeping rough or using homeless hostels and night shelters, so they are not left behind. Pregnant women can ask their maternity team, GP or pharmacist, following earlier pharmacy booking access this year.
Adults aged 65 and older, those with long term conditions, pregnant women and those with caring roles are just some of the people who are eligible for a free NHS flu vaccination. Those eligible can book through the NHS website or App, 119, a person’s GP or a participating walk-in pharmacy.
