The Government is introducing a Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) from 1 January 2027. It will ensure that highly traded, carbon-intensive goods imported into the UK face a carbon price comparable to the price that would apply under the UK Emissions Trading Scheme (ETS) if those goods were produced in the UK. At the UK-EU Summit, the UK and the EU have agreed to link their respective ETSs. As set out in the UK-EU political declaration on 20 November 2026 ETS linking has created the conditions for goods originating in the UK and the EU to benefit from mutual exemptions from the UK and EU CBAMs, in accordance with the relevant legislation in each jurisdiction. [1]
Today, the Government is confirming that it will introduce an amendment to the Finance Bill to exempt goods originating in the EU, EEA, and Switzerland from the UK CBAM. The amendment will be introduced early next year but will apply retrospectively to CBAM goods imported from 1 January 2027, subject to parliamentary approval.
Exempting these goods will avoid unnecessary administrative burdens for UK importers complying with the UK CBAM. This means that UK businesses importing CBAM goods will not incur a UK CBAM liability in relation to goods originating in these jurisdictions. It also means the value of those goods will not count towards the £50,000 registration threshold or need to be included on any CBAM returns.
The application of the exemption will be determined by the origin of the goods, rather than the country from which they are imported into the UK. Goods exported from the EU will not qualify solely because they were exported from the EU. Goods originating in other jurisdictions will remain in scope of CBAM but could be eligible for carbon price relief where a good has already been subject to a qualifying carbon price.
Businesses importing CBAM goods should continue to keep import records required for UK CBAM, including evidence demonstrating origin. HMRC will publish further guidance on how the exemption will operate in due course.
The UK and EU are taking forward the legislative and technical work required to operationalise ETS linking without delay. Once the link is operational, in line with the commitments made as part of the Summit, the EU has agreed to apply the reciprocal exemption to exports of UK-originating goods to the EU, in accordance with each party’s domestic legislation.
The UK CBAM will continue to apply to CBAM goods originating from other jurisdictions not covered by the linking agreement. CBAM is an important part of the government’s approach to reducing carbon leakage, ensuring that UK decarbonisation leads to a genuine reduction in global emissions and supporting British businesses by ensuring differing levels of decarbonisation do not push production overseas.
