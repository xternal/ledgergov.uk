Government backs local journalism with £6 million funding while careers campaign reaches thousands of youngsters
The ‘Inspiring the Future’ campaign, which encourages children to consider careers in journalism, has reached more than 11,000 students and engaged with more than 100 schools
This all forms part of the Government’s Local Media Action Plan, backing the vital role of local media in communities across the country
Fifty organisations are set to benefit from the government’s £6 million Local News Fund, helping to ensure people can continue to benefit from trusted, high-quality local journalism.
With audiences moving online and consuming their news through an increasingly diverse range of sources, the role of local journalism has never been more important, especially as the Government seeks to devolve power to a local level , given its integral role in holding power to account and tackling mis- and disinformation online.
Central to the Government’s ‘Amplify: The Local Media Action Plan’, the Local News Fund aims to strengthen communities and social cohesion, rebuild social trust, and support local growth by supporting local journalism.
The majority of funding has been awarded to local print and online news outlets and independent local TV and radio stations, who received grants of up to £125,000. The money will be used to invest in community outreach projects, digital tools, apps and new multimedia services that can help these organisations reach new and younger audiences while developing more sustainable sources of income. These grants have been evenly distributed across England and Wales, providing funding support for local journalism in every region.
The remainder of the Fund has been awarded to seven organisations, including software developers and major industry players, to develop infrastructure that can benefit the sector as a whole, with grants of up to £275,000 awarded.
Media Minister Ian Murray said:
For decades our local media has remained among the most trusted sources of news. But as the media landscape has become more complex, many outlets have been forced to reduce their numbers or even close. I am determined not to leave local journalism to collapse. This government knows it is critical to communities and fosters a self-confident nation that everyone feels part of.
Through the Local News Fund we are taking action to help newsrooms innovate and adapt their business models for the online world, while incentivising and encouraging the production of high-quality, trustworthy news that Britain’s towns and villages deserve and rely on.
Another key measure in the Local Media Action Plan is the ‘Inspiring the Future’ careers campaign, joint-funded by the News Media Association (NMA), which connects local media employers and journalists with schools to inspire the next generation of reporters. This initial six-month campaign focused on the North West, targeting schools in more deprived areas, with 79% having above national average free school meal eligibility, and the Government will seek to roll it out nationally subject to evaluation and funding.
Theo Bamber, Chief Executive, News Media Association, said:
Alongside DCMS and Education and Employers, we are very pleased to have supported the ‘Inspiring the Future’ pilot connecting local media professionals with the next generation of talent.
As power is devolved out of Westminster, the role of local news media holding power to account will become even more important. This initiative will support that by helping young people understand our industry, the benefits of a career in local media, and the vital role it plays in local communities across the country.
This Journalism Matters Week, an annual campaign run by the NMA that showcases the industry as a key part of the UK’s national infrastructure, the National Committee for the Safety of Journalists will meet to agree on a refreshed National Action Plan, reaffirming this Government’s commitment to journalist safety and tackling obstacles to news gathering which is another pillar of the Local Media Action Plan. Bringing together representatives from government, journalism, policing, prosecution services and civil society, the aim of the National Action Plan is to set out how the safety of journalists can be protected in the UK.

On the Local News Fund:
Applicants were assessed by a Local News Fund Steering Board, composed of independent industry experts, and were required to demonstrate an established track record of providing local news in England or Wales.
The Local News Fund is competitive, with all bids assessed according to technical and strategic criteria set out in the Fund advert and application form. A technical assessment of bids was conducted by the Government Grants Managed Service, and a strategic assessment conducted by the Local News Fund Steering Board composed of independent industry experts to guide final decisions on awards by the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport. Bids were assessed according to factors including their overall quality, deliverability and likely impact over the short and long term.
Bidders also had to meet eligibility criteria, by demonstrating a sustained track record in the provision of local journalism over at least the past 12 months, and by demonstrating that their content is editorially accountable.
Next year, £1 million from the Fund will be dedicated to tackling local ‘news deserts’, areas that have no dedicated local print, online, TV or radio outlet, in collaboration with key community stakeholders and the Fund’s Steering Board. Up to a further £5 million will also be made available next year to continue supporting financial sustainability and innovation in the sector.
On the Local News Fund Steering Board:
The government appointed the members of a Local News Fund Steering Board on 15 June following a fair and open recruitment process. The purpose of the steering board is to play an advisory role in providing independent expertise and objective advice to the government in the decision-making process for distribution of funding under the Local News Fund.
This includes a role in setting the bidding and eligibility criteria for the Local News Fund. In convening this board, the government aims to benefit from a wide range of experience of the local media sector and related industries. This will help to ensure value for money in the distribution of funding and that the best bids are selected for Local News Fund grants, for the benefit of local media and the communities that they serve.
The steering board comprises eight members, including two co-chairs. The DCMS Senior Responsible Owner for the Fund also attends meetings. The Board may also agree to invite others to attend on an ad hoc basis to provide specialist knowledge if and when required. The membership is:
