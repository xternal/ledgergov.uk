Capital gains — preventing protected cell company avoidance
Who is likely to be affected
UK resident individuals, trustees and companies holding assets through a non-UK resident protected cell company.
General description of the measure
This measure makes changes to the capital gains rules that apply to gains made by non-resident close companies that are attributed to UK resident individuals, trusts and companies that are ‘participators’. These rules provide that gains made by such non-resident companies should be apportioned to its participators. The rules apply to participators with an interest of more than 25% in a non-resident close company.
This measure amends the participator test, so that in the case of protected cell companies (PCC) it can apply to the level of interest in an individual cell within a PCC as well as that in the PCC as a whole. PCC are a type of company made up of a number of separate cells where the assets and liabilities of one cell are segregated and protected from those of the other cells.
Policy objective
This measure will increase trust in the tax system by clarifying the participator rules as they apply to PCC.
Background to the measure
This measure was announced at Budget 2026.
Detailed proposal
Operative date
This measure will have effect in relation to gains accruing on or after 28 October 2026.
Current law
Current law can be found in sections 3 to 3G Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992. These rules are targeted at UK-resident individual, trust or company ‘participators’ (defined by section 454 Corporation Tax Act 2010) who, to avoid a liability to Capital Gains Tax or Corporation Tax on gains, hold assets through a non-resident close company.
These rules provide that gains made by such companies should be attributed to its UK resident ‘participators’ holding an interest of more than 25% in a non-resident close company. The attributed gains are then subject to Capital Gains Tax or Corporation Tax.
Proposed revisions
Legislation will be introduced in Finance Bill 2026 providing that the 25% participator test can apply by reference to an individual cell of a PCC.

This measure is not expected to have an Exchequer impact. This measure supports the Exchequer in its commitment to protect revenue.
Economic impact
This measure is not expected to have any significant macroeconomic impacts.
Impact on individuals, households and families
This measure primarily impacts on UK resident individuals who are sheltering assets in non-resident PCC in order to avoid Capital Gains Tax. It prevents the use of PCC to avoid tax.
As this is a technical change, individuals will not be required to do anything differently.
This measure is expected overall to have no impact on individuals’ experience of dealing with HMRC.
This measure is not expected to impact on family formation, stability or breakdown.
Equalities impacts
This measure may apply to individuals regardless of their protected characteristics. The population impacted is expected to be a small subset of the Capital Gains Tax population. HMRC does not hold data that it can use to assess whether there are any disproportionate impacts to protected groups.
Administrative impact on business including civil society organisations
The measure will have an impact on those businesses that enter into avoidance schemes using PCC to avoid Capital Gains Tax or Corporation Tax.
One-off administrative costs could include businesses familiarising themselves with the change. There are not expected to be any further one-off or continuing costs.
This measure is not expected to disproportionately impact civil society organisations.
This measure is expected overall to have no impact on business experience of dealing with HMRC.
Operational impact (£million) (HMRC or other)
This measure will not require changes to HMRC’s processes or resources.
Other impacts
Other impacts have been considered and none have been identified.
Monitoring and evaluation
The measure will be kept under review through regular communication with affected taxpayer groups.
