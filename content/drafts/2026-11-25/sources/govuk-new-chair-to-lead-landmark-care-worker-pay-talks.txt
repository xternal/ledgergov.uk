New chair to lead landmark care worker pay talks
Baroness Christina McAnea will chair negotiations between employers and trade unions on pay and conditions for care workers in England
First fair pay agreement is backed by £500 million and will work alongside forthcoming adult social care workforce plan
Negotiations to begin in April 2027, with the first settlement introduced by April 2028
Work to agree a landmark fair pay agreement for care workers in England will be led by Baroness Christina McAnea, who has been newly appointed as chair of the negotiating body over the next 3 years.
Baroness McAnea of Kelvingrove will lead a new independent public body, made up of unions and employers, to negotiate a proposed agreement on:
pay
terms and conditions
wider employment matters
She will support the Adult Social Care Negotiating Body to present that fair pay agreement to the government.
Baroness McAnea’s appointment marks a major milestone in delivering the government’s manifesto commitment to introduce a fair pay agreement for care workers, and also represents important progress towards the creation of a national care service.
The first ever adult social care fair pay agreement will be backed by £500 million and will deliver for the 1.5 million people currently working in the sector, boosting domestic recruitment and retention of carers, while growing the economy, raising living standards and creating opportunities for all.
The first round of negotiations will commence in April 2027, with the first settlement introduced by April 2028.
Minister for Social Care and Mental Health, Alison McGovern, said:
I’m immensely proud of our work to deliver this landmark fair pay agreement for the millions of care workers across the country.
Baroness McAnea’s experience and expertise will be invaluable in this role, as she leads the negotiations and advocates for our care workforce.
This agreement will give care workers a voice and a fair deal in their vital contribution to society - not just for today but for every day - and will underpin our national care service.
Baroness Christina McAnea said:
I’m delighted to be appointed to this important role and to be part of getting the negotiating body off the ground.
Raising the wages and status of care workers are crucial steps towards establishing a national care service.
Better pay will mean a boost to many thousands of workers in the sector, help employers both attract and hold on to more staff and lead to improved support for all those in need of care.
The new independent public body is being set up by the Department of Health and Social Care. The body will negotiate the first ever fair pay agreement for the adult social care workforce and agreements thereafter on an annual basis.
Through the body, care providers and unions will negotiate on important issues including pay, terms and conditions, and wider employment matters through a regular negotiation process.
Professor Oonagh Smyth CBE, Chief Executive of Skills for Care, said:
Everyone who works in social care deserves to be recognised and rewarded for the vital, skilled work they do. We welcome the appointment of Baroness McAnea and this important next step towards the first fair pay agreement.
Improving pay and employment conditions is one of the most significant recommendations in the sector-led workforce strategy for adult social care. The fair pay agreement will be strongest when it works alongside wider investment in skills and career development, with clear routes for people to develop and progress through the care workforce pathway, and support for employers to implement change.
Together, these measures have the potential to make a lasting difference for our workforce and for everyone who draws on care and support.
Professor Nicola Ranger, Royal College of Nursing General Secretary and Chief Executive, said:

Work must now urgently start to address the poor pay, terms and conditions endured by the tens of thousands of nursing staff working in adult social care. This is crucial to bolstering the sector’s workforce and easing pressure on the NHS. We stand ready to support the new body in turning things around and redressing the balance for all those who work in this central part of our health and care system.
Gary Smith, GMB General Secretary, said:
This is a welcome appointment. Christina will bring a wealth of experience and knowledge to this crucial role. GMB looks forward to working with her in tackling the scandals of low pay and poor conditions experienced by too many of our hard pressed care workers.
The fair pay agreement will work in tandem with the forthcoming adult social care workforce plan, due to be published by the end of the year, which will support recruitment, retention and career development.
In September, the Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced that a fully funded national care service will be established in the next Parliament, providing free personal care for older people, based on need rather than their ability to pay. The national care service will be founded on the same principles as the NHS and will be funded by changes to the triple lock pension.
This will be informed by Baroness Louise Casey’s commission into adult social care, which the government has brought forward by a full year, and is set to report in 2027.
In the short term, the government:
is making over £4.6 billion of additional funding available for adult social care in 2028 to 2029 compared with 2025 to 2026
has published an unpaid carers action plan led by a new unpaid carers champion
has delivered an increase in the carers’ allowance, provided home improvement funding for people with disabilities and increased social care allowances
