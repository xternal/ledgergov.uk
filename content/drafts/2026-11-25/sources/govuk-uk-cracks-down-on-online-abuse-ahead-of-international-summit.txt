UK cracks down on online abuse ahead of international summit seeking to end violence against women and girls
New UK programme launched to tackle online abuse of women and girls, including the growing threat from AI
Programme will work with technology companies to make online services in the UK and across the world safer
UK also confirms major international summit on ending violence against women and girls set to be in London on 27-28 April 2027
Women and girls in the UK and around the world will be better protected from online abuse by a new UK-funded programme.
Supported by £27m of UK funding, the Thrive programme will tackle the growing use of AI and online platforms to stalk and harass women, and to create or share intimate images without their consent.
Under the programme, the UK will work with technology companies and start-ups to make products safer from the outset, including measures such as automatic image blurring and age restrictions. UK-funded experts will also provide legal and technical expertise to countries to strengthen online safety laws and close loopholes that allow abusers to operate across borders.
Foreign Secretary, Ed Miliband said:
Online abuse does not stop at national borders. Within seconds, a real or AI-generated image can be shared around the world.
The UK is leading international action to strengthen online safety laws, and stopping criminals from exploiting loopholes in emerging technology, protecting women and girls from appalling abuse.
The funding forms part of a series of measures set out in a UK pledge to tackle violence against women and girls at home and abroad ahead of a major international summit, hosted in London. The UK has today confirmed this summit will be held on 27-28 April 2027.
At the Summit, governments will come together with NGOs, the private sector, philanthropy and multilateral institutions – as well as survivors and influencers. Governments will make concrete commitments, share what works and collaborate to end violence against women and girls. This follows the UK’s domestic mission to halve violence against women and girls in a decade, announced in December 2025.
Online abuse is a growing global threat. Around 85% of women worldwide have witnessed or experienced online violence, while advances in AI are creating new risks. Reports of online image abuse to the UK’s Revenge Porn Helpline rose 1,463% between 2019 and 2025.
Foreign Office Minister for tackling Violence Against Women and Girls, Uma Kumaran said:
Violence against women and girls is one of the greatest injustices in our world today. One in three women will experience abuse in their lifetime.
That’s why we are cracking down on online abuse around the world and strengthening protections for survivors. And we’re not stopping there - next April the United Kingdom will bring governments, survivors and partners from around the world together in London for a global summit to drive action and speed up progress towards ending violence against women and girls.
Zara McDermott, documentary maker and campaigner said:
Having shared my own story of experiencing intimate image abuse at just 14 years old, I know how important it is for survivors to feel heard and supported.
Through my work as a documentary maker, I have had the privilege of listening to so many survivors share their experiences of violence and abuse. Time and time again, I hear stories of women and girls whose lives have been profoundly affected, often leaving them feeling silenced, isolated or unfairly burdened with shame.
What has always struck me is the extraordinary courage it takes to speak out, and how much more we need to do to ensure survivors feel heard, believed and protected.
Online abuse is everywhere; it travels borders and the globe. I am pleased to see the UK government taking action on a global scale to strengthen protections for women and girls, hold perpetrators accountable and create a safer digital world for future generations.
In line with the government’s announcement this week on bolstering protection for women facing domestic abuse, the UK has also initiated international work to explore stronger cross-border protections for victims of domestic abuse and other harmful behaviours.
The UK government is also putting together a global advisory board of survivors, to enable people with lived experience to influence decisions on how we tackle violence against women and girls.
Notes to editors
The International Coalition to End Violence Against Women and Girls was launched by the UK in 2026. The coalition members are: UK, South Africa, Brazil, Morocco, Spain, Jamaica, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Australia and France.
The Global Summit on Violence Against Women and Girls will take place in London on 27 and 28 April 2027. Countries are expected to make commitments in time for the summit and report progress against them.
Thrive will scale up the global Non-Consensual Intimate Image (NCII) abuse clearing centre, enabling more countries, platforms and support organisations to work together to identify, report and remove harmful content before it spreads further online. The programme will also develop a Model National Framework on Addressing NCII to help governments strengthen legislation, policy, regulation and enforcement responses. This will help countries strengthen their own national responses while promoting greater international consistency in tackling the issue.
The programme is funding experts to work directly with technology companies, including AI developers and digital start-ups in emerging markets, to build safety into products from the outset. Companies will receive expert support to identify and address risks such as image-based abuse, online harassment and harms enabled by artificial intelligence, helping make online services safer for women and girls.
The International Survivor Advisory Board will be established in partnership with the Brave Movement. The Brave Movement, launched in 2022, is a survivor-centered global advocacy movement dedicated to ensuring that all children and adolescents can live free from sexual violence.
Last year, the UK unveiled its national VAWG strategy, setting out how the UK will meet its ambition to halve VAWG in a decade.
The UK has made intimate image abuse, cyberflashing and choking priority offences under the Online Safety Act – treating this material with the same seriousness as child abuse or terrorism and placing legal duties on platforms to stop this content before it reaches users. The UK has also introduced legal requirements that mean tech firms must now remove intimate images shared without consent within 48 hours of being flagged, shifting the burden from victims to platforms. An amendment to the Crime and Policing Bill also created a new offence criminalising so‑called “nudification apps”, AI tools that generate synthetic sexualised images of women and girls.
