VAT treatment of medicines dispensed by registered pharmacy technicians
Purpose of this brief
This brief explains changes to the VAT treatment of medicines dispensed by registered pharmacy technicians, or by other pharmacy staff they supervise, in England, Scotland and Wales.
Who should read this brief
You should read this brief if you are a:
community pharmacy or other pharmacy business
registered pharmacist
registered pharmacy technician
VAT adviser
Background
UK VAT law allows certain drugs and medicines to be zero-rated for VAT, including medicines dispensed to an individual for their personal use on a prescription from an appropriate practitioner. The zero rate is set out in Group 12 of Schedule 8 of the Value Added Tax Act 1994.
The Department of Health and Social Care has changed medicines legislation.
From 10 December 2026, registered pharmacists will be able to authorise registered pharmacy technicians in England, Scotland and Wales to:
dispense medicines
supervise dispensing by other pharmacy staff
HMRC policy
VAT legislation is being amended to reflect the new pharmacy supervision arrangements.
From 10 December 2026, the zero rate will also apply to medicines dispensed by:
a registered pharmacy technician authorised by a registered pharmacist
other pharmacy staff supervised by a registered pharmacy technician (who has been authorised by a registered pharmacist)
This means medicines dispensed under the new arrangements will receive the same VAT treatment as medicines dispensed under the existing arrangements.
The change does not affect which drugs and medicines can be zero-rated.
The change does not apply in Northern Ireland because pharmacy technicians are not currently a registered healthcare profession there.
More information
HMRC will update its VAT guidance and public notice when the legislation comes into force on 10 December 2026.
This will include the VAT Health Manual and Health professionals and pharmaceutical products (VAT Notice 701/57).
