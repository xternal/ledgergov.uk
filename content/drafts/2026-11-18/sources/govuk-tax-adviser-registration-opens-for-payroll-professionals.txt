Tax adviser registration opens for payroll professionals
Phase three of the new online registration requirement for tax advisers is now open.
Payroll professionals have until 17 February 2027 to register.
Mandatory tax adviser registration will help protect customers and raise standards in tax advice.
Registration is now open for payroll professionals as phase three of the new Modernising and Mandating Tax Adviser Registration (MMTAR) rolls out.
Advisers who solely provide professional third-party payroll services and interact with HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) on behalf of their clients and do not already have an agent services account (ASA) have until midnight on 17 February 2027 to register.
Some payroll professionals may not consider themselves as tax advisers, but the registration requirements apply to anyone who is paid to interact with HMRC on behalf of their clients’ tax affairs. HMRC is encouraging them to check the guidance now to understand if they need to register and, if so, to submit their application as soon as possible.
These mandatory changes are designed to raise standards in the tax advice market, protect taxpayers and support those who play by the rules. More than 5,000 new ASA have been created since the first agent registration window opened on 18 May 2026.
Robert Jones, HMRC’s Director of Intermediaries, said:
Payroll service providers play an important role in helping employers to meet their employees’ tax obligations. They should check the guidance on GOV.UK and apply as soon as possible to continue supporting their clients without disruption.
Mandatory registration will help raise standards across the tax advice market and strengthen trust in professional tax services.
MMTAR is a single, streamlined digital registration system that replaces a range of previous processes, making it easier for tax advisers to interact with HMRC.
Registration is free and online. Step-by-step guidance and an interactive checker tool are available on GOV.UK to help advisers understand if they need to register and what they need to do. Eligible tax advisers must meet HMRC’s registration conditions to apply for an ASA.
Advisers who submit an application and receive a registration number can continue to engage with HMRC while their registration is being processed.
Those who missed an earlier registration window, or are new to the tax advice market, should register as soon as possible.
HMRC may limit an adviser’s ability to act on behalf of clients if they fail to register when required. Advisers who continue to operate without completing the registration requirement could also face enforcement action, including financial penalties.
Further Information
More information about Modernising and Mandating Tax Adviser Registration (MMTAR) is available on GOV.UK.
The government is investing £36 million to modernise HMRC’s tax adviser services.
MMTAR registration is being introduced in three-month phases from:
18 May 2026 - Unregistered tax advisers without an ASA who interact with HMRC on behalf of clients.
18 August 2026 - Advisers with a Self Assessment or Corporation Tax account who do not have an ASA.
18 November 2026 - Advisers who solely provide professional payroll services on behalf of clients and who do not have an ASA.
31 December 2026 – Financial services organisations without an ASA
Anyone paid to interact with HMRC on behalf of someone else about their tax affairs is generally considered to be a tax adviser unless an exemption applies.
Tax advisers who already have an ASA do not need to register again. They will be moved to the new digital service in the financial year 2027 to 2028. HMRC will contact them directly if any additional information is required.
Advisers can continue to interact with HMRC on behalf of their clients while HMRC processes their application.
