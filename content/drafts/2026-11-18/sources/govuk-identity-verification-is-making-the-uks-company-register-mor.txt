Identity verification is making the UK's company register more trustworthy
Companies House introduced mandatory identity verification on 18 November 2025 to give more assurance about who is setting up, running and controlling companies in the UK.
Improving the quality of information on the register and making it harder for people to abuse the system helps create an environment where legitimate businesses can thrive.
Identity verification was phased in over 12 months to minimise the impact on business and help Companies House maintain a high level of service to customers.
Around 90% of live companies have complied with identity verification requirements and more than 12 million appointments have been verified.
In addition, over 75,000 personal data corrections have been made following identity verification activity and 14,000 person with significant control (PSC) address inaccuracies have been identified, improving the quality of the data on the register.
Minister for Regulatory Reform, Liz Lloyd said:
These simple changes are improving confidence in the register, supporting businesses to make better, informed decisions through increased transparency.
That’s why it’s crucial all companies comply with identity verification, so everyone continues to feel the benefits of it.
The identity verification process
Since 18 November 2025, identity verification is required by Companies House for:
new directors and people with significant control (PSCs)
existing directors and PSCs
members of a limited liability partnership (LLP)
Individuals can verify their identity with Companies House through GOV.UK One Login, or through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP).
In a Companies House Perceptions Survey, 86% of customers found the identity verification process straightforward.
Andy King, Companies House chief executive said:
We’re really pleased that the business community has supported the process and over 12 million appointments have been verified.
Identity verification is helping to create a more trusted business environment for everyone by improving the quality and integrity of information on the register. This helps ensure that legitimate businesses can start, grow and operate with confidence.
Compliance and enforcement
By the end of March 2027, all companies will have met identity verification requirements linked to their confirmation statement or be on an appropriate pathway to compliance or enforcement action.
Companies House has begun targeted enforcement activity for those not complying with identity verification requirements, as we focus on maintaining compliance and taking action.
The proportionate approach starts with reminders and opportunities to make representations. Where people continue to fail to comply with identity verification requirements, we take enforcement action, which has included financial penalties, prosecutions, and referrals to the Insolvency Service that have led to court-imposed fines and director disqualifications.
In future, we will put notes on the public register where identity verification requirements have not been completed by the legal deadline. Further information will be published ahead of this measure being introduced.
The value of identity verification
Jonathan Geldart, Director General of the Institute of Directors said:
Mandatory identity verification improves the quality and trustworthiness of the register, protects legitimate directors, and strengthens the UK’s business environment while supporting the broader objectives of the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act. Effective corporate governance depends on transparency and accountability. Director identity verification should therefore not be viewed merely as a compliance exercise.
The high completion rate at the end of the implementation phase demonstrates directors’ strong recognition of the importance of meeting their responsibilities, ensuring company information is accurate and up to date, and helping to combat fraud, impersonation, and the misuse of corporate structures. This ultimately benefits both responsible businesses and public trust in the UK’s corporate framework.
Dan Jobson, Chair of the Business Information Providers Association (BIPA) said:
This will help build greater levels of trust and transparency in conducting business with UK registered companies, which will help the UK growth agenda.
Having all identities verified on the register has been estimated to be worth £210 million to £400 million of additional annual value for enhanced corporate transparency alone.

New powers under ECCTA
The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 (ECCTA) gave Companies House new and improved powers to prevent fraudulent company incorporations and to query, remove and share suspicious information.
Using the Registrar’s powers, we took over 138,000 actions to tackle abuse and improve the integrity of the register between 1 April and 31 October 2026. These actions affected 73,000 corporate bodies.
We aim to take 225,000 actions by the end of the financial year (31 March 2027).
From 4 March 2024 to 31 October 2026, Companies House has:
defaulted 185,900 registered office addresses
defaulted 147,800 officer addresses
defaulted 117,500 PSC addresses
redacted 113,800 pieces of information from incorporation documents, to remove personal data used without consent
This has affected 253,500 total corporate bodies.
We have used enhanced detection techniques and system improvements to prevent more than 4,200 suspicious filings, including over 2,700 suspicious incorporations.
Identity verification compliance and enforcement
We have begun targeted and proportionate enforcement activity for identity verification non-compliance.
We began issuing financial penalty warning letters in July.
We’ve successfully prosecuted directors for failure to file confirmation statements, where failure to verify their identity is an aggravating factor (131 convictions and an aggravated fine increase of £32,745).
Our partners at the Insolvency Service have prosecuted people for acting as a director with an unverified identity, securing the first convictions and disqualifications in September.
