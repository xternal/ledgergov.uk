New champion to give unpaid carers a stronger voice
Millions of unpaid carers will have a stronger voice in government as Anna Dixon MP is appointed the new Champion for Unpaid Carers on Carers Rights Day (19 November 2026).
She will work directly with unpaid carers to ensure their voices and experiences help shape the national care service and build a care system that better supports them and their families.
She brings established credibility across the unpaid carers and social care sector and will:
listen to carers
work with their organisations
engage across government
The appointment builds on the unpaid carers action plan, announced in the summer to help millions of unpaid carers access the right support at the right time.
Yvette Cooper, Secretary of State for Health and Social Care, said:
Care starts with people, with unpaid carers and overstretched care workers who care for us when we can no longer care for ourselves.
That is why Anna Dixon’s appointment as our new Champion for Unpaid Carers is so important, making sure their voices are always heard in government.
Her wealth of experience in the sector will prove invaluable as we develop a national care service that provides dignity and decency for all.
Anna Dixon MP, Champion for Unpaid Carers, said:
There are millions of unpaid carers across the country who work tirelessly every single day to support their loved ones and families. They do this often at high personal, physical and financial cost. After decades working in health and social care, it is an honour to have the responsibility of ensuring their voices are heard directly at the heart of government.
Throughout my career and time in Parliament, I have met so many extraordinary carers, including young people, who don’t even think of themselves as carers. Unpaid carers’ contributions are absolutely vital, yet too often they go unrecognised until they reach a crisis point.
Having served as the chair of the All-Party Parliamentary Group on Carers, I know the importance of joined-up action across health, social care, welfare and employment, and as Champion for Unpaid Carers, I can assure you that carers’ voices will be key to shaping government policy from the ground up.
Having seen first-hand the care my mum gave my grandma, my sister has given as a parent to my niece with autism, and my husband and brother-in-law gave to my mother-in-law at the end of her life, I look forward to working closely with carers, carers organisations and ministers across government to ensure rights are protected, crucial support is available and easier to access, and make certain that our millions of unpaid carers get the recognition they deserve.
Nearly one in 10 people in England is an unpaid carer - providing an invaluable service in society looking after family members, friends or loved ones.
However, unpaid carers can experience challenges with keeping up careers or education, looking after their own health - including loneliness and isolation - and difficulties in taking breaks away from caring.
The government’s Unpaid carers action plan: recognise, refer, reach contains 42 actions and sets out practical steps across health, social care, education, employment and social security to improve support for unpaid carers.
Measures include new support through the NHS App, clearer information on carers’ rights and better recognition across health and care, alongside stronger workplace support and action to identify and support young carers earlier.

In September, the Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced that a fully funded national care service will be established in the next Parliament, providing free personal care for older people, based on need rather than their ability to pay. The national care service will be founded on the same principles as the NHS and will be funded by changes to the triple lock pension.
It follows the steps the government has already taken to support unpaid carers, including raising the Carer’s Allowance earnings limit by more than £2,750 in 2 years, the largest increase since the 1970s.
Alongside this, Universal Credit and Pension Credit provide an additional £2,500 a year to 1.1 million unpaid carers through the carer element and carer addition.
The government is also reviewing the implementation of carer’s leave and looking at where any improvements may be needed to ensure that employment rights for unpaid carers are fit for the modern world of work. This includes a public consultation on employment rights for unpaid carers and parents of seriously ill children, which closed on 1 September 2026.
Following the review’s conclusion, the government will publish a response to the consultation, findings from the review and any next steps needed to ensure that unpaid carers can balance work and care. The government intends to publish the findings of the review in early 2027.
