I am today announcing the establishment of new Neighbourhood Support Teams delivering a care management service, to transform the support available to people with the greatest health and care needs.
Too many older and vulnerable people are let down by a health and care system that is fragmented and too often waits for a crisis before it acts. People and their families are left to navigate multiple services and to join up their own care. A small group bears the heaviest cost: around 3 to 5 per cent of the population account for approximately 70 per cent of emergency bed days, and many of their admissions could have been prevented with earlier, coordinated support at home.
Neighbourhood Support Teams will change this. In every neighbourhood, the service will provide dedicated and coordinated support to those at highest risk, drawing together general practice, community health, social care and the voluntary sector. It will begin with people living with frailty, those approaching the end of life, care home residents and the housebound. Each person will have a dedicated team that knows them, with a named lead responsible for coordinating their care, a shared care plan, proactive support to stay well, and, in time, a single route to call at any hour. The teams will coordinate support across health and care, respond when people’s needs change and help them return home safely when hospital treatment is necessary. The teams will complement, not replace, people’s relationship with their general practitioner, and will work alongside a strengthened acute frailty service at the hospital front door.
The service will begin next year and grow over time, reaching around half a million people initially. It will be funded through existing NHS allocations, including by prioritising investment in care outside hospital and, over time, reinvesting the benefits generated by reducing avoidable hospital use. Integrated care boards will be required to provide the service to a growing proportion of their population, working to nationally defined outcomes and supported by a model service specification.
Alongside Neighbourhood Health Centres, improvements to primary care access, and community Mental Health Centres, this service will form core part of the Government’s reforms to strengthen health and care in the community, as set out in the 10 Year Health Plan
Further detail will be set out in the forthcoming Urgent, Emergency and Planned Care Strategy, and I will keep the House informed as the service is implemented.
