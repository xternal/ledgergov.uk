My hon. Friend the Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Victims, Violence Against Women and Girls and International Justice (Alex Davies-Jones) and I are today pleased to announce the rollout of Domestic Abuse Protection Notices (DAPNs) and Domestic Abuse Protection Orders (DAPOs) from 25 November across England and Wales.
The Domestic Abuse Act 2021 legislated for DAPNs and DAPOs to provide stronger and more flexible protection for victims and survivors of domestic abuse. DAPOs are the first cross jurisdictional protective order that can be made in the criminal, civil, and family courts. They can be accessed through a range of routes, with applications being made by the police, victims and survivors themselves, and third parties, including family members, local authorities and the domestic abuse sector, helping to ensure that victims and survivors can access protection in the way that best meets their needs. In certain family and civil proceedings, courts can also make a DAPO of their own initiative, and DAPOs can be made following either conviction or acquittal where the court considers that protection is necessary. DAPOs can also protect victims from all forms of domestic abuse, including controlling or coercive behaviour and economic abuse. They can impose both prohibitions, such as restrictions on contact, and positive requirements, such as participation in a behaviour change programme. Breach of a DAPO is a criminal offence, ensuring that perpetrators are held to account for their actions.
Since November 2024, DAPNs and DAPOs have been piloted in selected areas of England and Wales. The pilot has demonstrated the value of a coordinated multi-agency response, with police, courts, local authorities, specialist domestic abuse services and other partners working together to support victims and survivors and manage perpetrators. More than 3,000 DAPOs have been made during the pilot, providing vital protection for victims and survivors while enabling government and operational partners to test these new orders in practice and inform preparations for rollout.
To support implementation, tomorrow I am publishing updated police statutory guidance on DAPNs and DAPOs under section 50 of the Domestic Abuse Act 2021. This updated guidance incorporates learning from the pilot and is intended to provide police forces with clear and practical information to support their use of these new notices and orders and the delivery of a victim-centred response to domestic abuse. Alongside this, we are publishing a suite of wider supporting guidance for victims, local authorities, legal practitioners, and the domestic abuse sector.
We will continue to work closely with operational partners to support effective implementation across England and Wales and to ensure that victims and survivors benefit from the strengthened protections that DAPNs and DAPOs provide. I would like to thank all those who have contributed to the development, testing, and implementation of DAPNs and DAPOs, their expertise and commitment have been instrumental in reaching this important milestone.
As one of the final measures of the Domestic Abuse Act 2021 to be implemented, the DAPO rollout marks an important milestone. It will give police and courts stronger tools to protect victims and survivors and to relentlessly pursue perpetrators, holding them to account for their abuse, supporting the Government's mission to halve violence against women and girls within a decade and delivering the ambition set out in the Violence Against Women and Girls Strategy.
Together, we will ensure that DAPNs and DAPOs fulfil their potential to strengthen protection for victims and survivors, prevent further harm, and help build a safer future for all.
