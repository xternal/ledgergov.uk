Better regulation chief appointed to cut unnecessary bureaucracy
William Sargent appointed Chair of the Better Regulation Executive, restored by the Chancellor at the Budget to drive a more proportionate, predictable regulatory system
New Executive will tackle delays and pointless paperwork holding British businesses and public sector workers back to clear the way for growth and investment
That includes delivering target to cut regulatory admin burdens by 25%, equivalent to £5.6 billion, by the end of Parliament
British businesses and public servants facing time-consuming admin and delays in decision-making will get a powerful new champion at the heart of government to make regulation more proportionate and predictable – unlocking growth and investment and delivery across the country.
William Sargent has today (24th November 2026) been appointed Chair of the Better Regulation Executive, which the Chancellor John Healey restored at the Budget to drive improvements in the regulatory system from the heart of government.
Strong regulation protects workers, consumers and communities and gives businesses the certainty they need to invest. The Better Regulation Executive will protect those benefits while stripping away unnecessary bureaucracy and delays that waste British businesses’ time, money and prevent the public and private sectors from innovating.
Sargent will attend Cabinet today to rally departments as Ministers step up a cross-government drive to make regulation simpler and more effective, with stronger public leadership and control to remove barriers holding British business and public sector workers.
Working from the heart of government, Sargent will find outdated and duplicative rules and challenge departments and regulators to ensure regulation is proportionate and encourages growth and investment, and frees public servants to focus on delivering for citizens.
This builds on the Chancellor’s drive to cut admin burdens by 25%, equivalent to £5.6 billion or 280 million hours work hours per year, by the end of Parliament.
So far, the Government has made £2 billion of net progress against the target for delivering administrative savings.
These are changes that businesses will feel. Under changes made this year to corporate reporting requirements, any business employing around 100 people could save up to £20,000 per year.
Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey MP said:
“Regulation has a critical role to plan to protecting consumers, workers and the environment. Businesses are fed up with pointless paperwork eating up the time and money they should be using to invest and grow.
“With my backing, William Sargent will cut unnecessary bureaucracy for businesses and frontline workers, speed up decision-making and make sure regulation works for businesses and public servants rather than getting in their way.”
Chair of the Better Regulation Executive William Sargent said:
“I am delighted to be appointed to Chair the new Better Regulation Executive. A proportionate and streamlined regulatory system is essential to growth and investment, and to delivering the world-class public services people expect.
“My focus will be to confront where the system is falling short and deliver practical change.”
As well as working with departments to ensure regulation is proportionate, Sargent will work closely with the Chancellor, First Secretary of State and Business Secretary to drive action across Whitehall and make sure regulators are effective, efficient and improve the experience for businesses dealing with them every day.
The previous Better Regulation Executive led a simplification programme between 2005 and 2010 that reported more than £3.5 billion in net annual savings for businesses and voluntary organisations.
The announcement comes as the Chancellor brings together business groups including the Federation of Small Businesses, the CBI and the TUC alongside the First Secretary of State and Business Secretary.
They will hear first-hand where pointless bureaucracy is still getting in the way, as government steps up its drive to restore stronger public leadership and grip from the heart of government to give British firms more breathing space to invest, innovate and grow.
This includes giving 25% off electricity bills to eligible industrial businesses, and increasing the Small Business Rate Relief threshold for retail, hospitality and leisure businesses from £12,000 to £18,000, which means 70,000 businesses will pay no business rates at all.
Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds said:
“Pen-pushing isn’t what people get into business to do. They want to be spending their time innovating, growing and becoming a thriving part of their local community.
“The more unnecessary barriers we can remove the better, and I look forward to working with William to clear a pathway to growth in every postcode of this country.”
Rain Newton-Smith, Chief Executive, Confederation of British Industry (CBI) said:
“Businesses welcome the Government’s continued commitment to regulatory reform. Firms need a modern regulatory system that is proportionate and allows them to invest, innovate and grow. The Better Regulation Executive’s re-establishment is therefore an important signal in delivering the Government’s Regulatory Action Plan. The test now becomes delivery of the 25% reduction in regulatory burden and taking steps to ease the cost of tax administration.”
Tina Mckenzie, Policy Chair, Federation of Small Businesses (FSB) said:
“I’m pleased to welcome William Sargent to this post and to talk this morning with the Chancellor, First Secretary and the Business Secretary about the crucial job of remove paperwork, time costs and sheer risk from small business on behalf of FSB. ”Cutting the regulatory burden for SMEs by a quarter is so important and I am pleased to see the strength of commitment for this today.”
Kate Bell, Assistant General Secretary, Trade Union Congress (TUC) said:
“Good regulation means high standards, safe working and protection from exploitation. It is vital to ensure a level playing field for businesses and to prevent a race to the bottom.
“And it’s important to prevent unnecessarily burdensome admin and ensure efficient processes and paperwork for firms “The latest UK-EU agreement is a good example of this government working to cut bureaucracy and get a better deal for British workers and businesses.”
