Landmark Study into UK Government policy during the Troubles launched as Thousands More Archival Records Made Available Online
Five prominent academics have been appointed following an open and transparent competition to author an Official History of UK Government policy during the Northern Ireland conflict.
The historians will be granted privileged access to state archives, including closed and redacted files, with total freedom to publish independent conclusions.
This comes as a further batch of nearly 5,000 UK Government records are digitised and published online free of charge by The National Archives.
The UK Government has today delivered a major transparency milestone, announcing the formal appointment of five independent Official Historians to lead a landmark study into UK Government policy during the Troubles, alongside the online publication of thousands of additional archival records.
In a first for the 110-year history of the UK Official History series, five leading historians have been appointed following an open and transparent process to conduct an independent, authoritative examination of UK Government policy during the conflict.
Backed by a £3.6 million, five-year commitment, the team will receive full, unfettered access to relevant state archives - including closed and redacted files - with independence over their research, findings, and final publications.
The appointed historians are:
Dr Stuart Aveyard: Senior Lecturer in Modern British History, Manchester Metropolitan University
Dr Edward Burke: Assistant Professor in the History of War since 1945, University College Dublin
Dr Tony Craig: Associate Professor in Modern History, University of Staffordshire
Professor Martyn Frampton: Professor of Modern History, Queen Mary University of London
Professor Samantha Newbery: Professor of Intelligence Studies, University of Warwick
Complementing the official history project, The National Archives has published a second tranche of nearly 5,000 digitised UK Government records relating to the early stages of the Troubles, security responses, the Sunningdale Agreement, and the 1974 Executive collapse. All files are already open in hard-copy at Kew; this release removes physical barriers by making them instantly accessible online. Further biannual batches are scheduled over the next four years.
The Secretary of State for Northern Ireland, Sir Chris Bryant MP, said:
We cannot meaningfully deal with the legacy of the Troubles, nor build a stable foundation for the future, without a rigorous understanding of the past. As part of that work it is essential that we have the fullest account possible of the UK Government’s policy and decision-making during the conflict in Northern Ireland and that is why it’s so important that these historians will have unfettered archive access.
As a published historian, it is really important to me that the historians we have appointed are totally independent and can carry out their work without fear or favour. Their job will not be to produce a comprehensive history of the Troubles but rather a specific official history of the UK Government’s decision-making during that period.
At the same time unlocking thousands more records online will mean that students, academics, and the public at large, will have access to important information.
The Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Northern Ireland, Baroness Anderson, added:
True reconciliation is built on understanding. Empowering independent historians to examine the historical record alongside wider digital access will provide crucial context to help us all reflect on the past.
Joint Chair of the Official History’s expert Advisory Panel, Professor Nic Dháibhéid said:
I am delighted that through a competitive and rigorous process we have appointed five historians of the highest calibre to carry out this important project. The expert advisory panel is looking forward to supporting them in their work, in ensuring that they have access to all relevant materials, and in sharing their findings with public and academic audiences alike.
Joint Chair of the Official History’s expert Advisory Panel, Lord Bew said:
These five historians come from very different backgrounds but all have shown an unflinching determination to reveal historical truth to be found in the archives.
Notes to Editors:
The Government’s Official History series began in 1908, and involves independent historians appointed by the Prime Minister granted privileged access to closed files beyond the normal provisions of the Public Records Act. Previous examples of official histories include the Official History of the Falklands Campaign (published in 2007) and the Official History of the Royal Navy in World War II (published in volumes between 1954 and 1961).
In 2008, the previous Labour Government commissioned Sir Joseph Pilling to review the Official History series. Sir Joseph made a number of recommendations to increase the transparency and relevance of the programme, and the report is published here.
The Government has appointed an independent expert advisory panel to provide independent advice on the direction and progress of the project. The panel consists of Lord Paul Bew (Emeritus Professor of Politics at Queen’s University Belfast); Professor Caoimhe Nic Dháibhéid (Senior Lecturer in Modern History at University of Sheffield); Professor Henry Patterson (Emeritus Professor of Politics at the University of Ulster); Professor Richard Bourke (Professor of the History of Political Thought, University of Cambridge); Professor Helen Parr (Full Professor of History, University of Limerick); Professor Ian McBride (Foster Professor of Irish History, Oxford University).
A website with further information about the project is available at www.niofficialhistory.org.uk.
