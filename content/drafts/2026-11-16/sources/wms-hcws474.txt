My Hon Friend the Minister of State for Trade (Lord Sarwar) has today made the following statement.
Today, I am announcing that the Government will introduce a melt and pour reporting requirement for steel products within the scope of the UK’s steel trade measure. The requirement will begin on a voluntary basis from 1 April 2027 and become mandatory from 1 July 2027.
Steel is a strategically important sector, supporting communities across the country and providing essential materials for our critical national infrastructure, manufacturing and defence. However, global steel overcapacity continues to distort international markets, depress prices and threaten the long-term viability of domestic steelmaking. This requirement will improve transparency in steel supply chains by identifying where steel was originally melted and poured. It will provide evidence to inform future policy decisions aimed at addressing global steel overcapacity and supporting UK steelmaking.
Melt and pour refers to the process in which raw steel or iron is initially produced in liquid form within a steel or iron-making furnace and subsequently cast into its first solid state following melting. The country in which steel was melted and poured can differ from its declared country of origin. Steel initially produced in one country may subsequently be processed in another country before being imported into the UK. Our existing trade data does not provide sufficient visibility of these supply chains to help inform decision making to identify the sources of overcapacity.
Collecting melt and pour information is increasingly becoming a standard international practice among key partners concerned about global steel overcapacity. The United States and Canada already operate reporting requirements, and the European Union introduced a requirement on 1 October 2026. As a leading country within the Global Forum on Steel Excess Capacity, in September 2026 the UK signed up to the ‘Milwaukee Framework’, a comprehensive framework for joint action to address steel excess capacity, which included a commitment to developing and initiating procedures to collect “country of melt and pour” data.
The new requirement will ask importers to declare the country in which relevant steel was originally melted and poured and retain evidence supporting their declaration, to be provided if requested by HM Revenue and Customs for verification. This is a reporting requirement for data gathering purposes. It will not itself alter tariff treatment, access to quotas, rules of origin arrangements under the UK’s steel trade measure or the United Kingdom’s free trade agreements.
The Government has engaged closely with steel producers, downstream users, stockholders and importers to ensure that the requirement is proportionate and workable. The design of the UK requirement closely matches the European Union’s approach, in order to simplify arrangements for businesses trading across both markets and reduce complexity for traders in Northern Ireland. It nevertheless includes additional facilitations intended to reflect the needs of UK businesses. For example, small consignments under £2000 in value will be exempted from the requirement.
Following feedback from steel importers, there will be a voluntary period from April to June. This will allow businesses to familiarise themselves with the new requirement, reducing the risk of disruption and unintentional non-compliance. Mandatory reporting will then begin on 1 July 2027.
Supporting evidence should clearly state the country of melt and pour and the heat test number. Mill Test Certificates are the primary form of supporting evidence, with a specified list of alternative documents to be accepted in place of or alongside Mill Test Certificates, where these are not available or do not state all the required information. We recognise that businesses have different supply chains and may not always receive the same documentation from their suppliers and this is intended to address those concerns.
Detailed guidance will be published to help importers understand the products covered, the declaration process and the evidence they should retain. My officials and HM Revenue and Customs will continue to engage with and support industry ahead of implementation to prepare for the requirement.
This Government is committed to securing a sustainable future for UK steelmaking. Improving our understanding of international steel supply chains is a key part of our continued efforts, alongside partners, to tackle global steel overcapacity.
