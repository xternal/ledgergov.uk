Crackdown on child sexual abuse material as new laws force porn sites to use tech to clean up their sites
Porn sites also told to use technology called ‘hash-matching’ to hunt down child sexual abuse material and clean up their platforms
Same technology will also have to be used to spot and remove terrorist material from platforms
Children and families will be better protected online as laws introduced by Ofcom today will mean porn sites must identify and remove child sexual abuse material with technology designed to spot it before it spreads.
The technology, called ‘hash matching’, compares online content against a database of known illegal material.
When there is a match, the platform can remove the content and stop it from spreading, without anyone having to see it first.
The same ‘hash-matching’ technology will also have to be used to stop terrorist material from all other platforms.
In addition, services in scope of the Online Safety Act should boot off predators who share child sexual abuse material, and stop them from creeping back.
Online Safety Minister Ian Murray said:
The online world has been left to police itself for too long and it is children and families who have paid the price. This is changing.
More platforms will now have to hunt down child sexual abuse material and terrorist content, not wait for it to be reported. The vile predators who share child sexual abuse material will be thrown off and kept off.
Our online spaces are as much a part of public life as any high street. We are moving fast to make sure the law keeps pace with new technology, because people have a right to be safe online, whatever technology they use.
The measures will be laid in Parliament today. They build on the Government’s plans to protect children from the harms of social media by banning platforms from providing their services to under-16s, as well as implementing restrictions on harmful features including gaming platforms.
Hannah Swirsky, Head of Policy at the Internet Watch Foundation, said:
We are proud the IWF’s evidence and insights helped shape these new codes and are pleased there will be stronger detection duties and wider hash matching requirements, particularly for pornography services.
Hash matching is a tried and trusted form of child sexual abuse material detection and the more sites deploying this protection the better. The harm done by the global spread of this material can’t be understated. And for victims, knowing the sharing of their sexual abuse can finally be stopped on more platforms is a lifeline.
We are also pleased services will now be responsible for taking steps to stop the spread of unknown child sexual abuse material, and acting on available information when it comes to stopping the sharing of illegal material in end-to-end encrypted environments.
There is more work to be done, but these measures signal that the net is tightening and there will be fewer places to hide.
ENDS
Notes to editors:
The updated codes set out the steps that online platforms and search services must take to keep people safe and cover illegal content and the protection of children. They also reflect that cyberflashing, and encouraging or assisting serious self-harm, are now priority offences under the Online Safety Act, meaning platforms will have to take stronger action to stop this type of content.
