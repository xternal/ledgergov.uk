The Government is committed to ensuring that people and businesses across the UK can continue to access the banking services they need. The Government commissioned an independent Review chaired by Richard Lloyd OBE to assess whether declining access to in-person banking services is causing consumer detriment, the scale and materiality of any detriment, and which customers are most affected.
Further to my statement on 14 May [HCWS14] the Government has today published the Access to Banking Services Review, Report of the Independent Review. This statement sets out the Government’s response to the Review.
The Review has found that, while most customers can bank effectively through remote channels, a minority will continue to need access to in-person banking services for the foreseeable future. This extends from day-to-day banking to more complex services that consumers and businesses may need to access less frequently.
The Review recommends a new statutory framework to ensure reasonable access to essential in-person banking services, reflecting changing customer needs. It also recognises the role that well designed, tailored telephone and digital services can play in meeting customers’ needs, and that there are multiple ways in which consumers can access banking services. It therefore recommends that industry, the Government and the regulator should redouble their efforts to promote accessible banking across all channels.
The Government welcomes the Review and accepts the Review’s central conclusion that further action is needed.
The Government intends to legislate to establish a new statutory framework that ensures reasonable access to essential in-person banking services for customers who need them, while recognising the important role of accessible digital and telephone banking.
In taking forward legislation, the Government will consider how the statutory framework should best secure that objective, reflecting the different ways in which customers access banking services, including in-person, digital and telephone channels. The Government will amend the Financial Services and Markets Bill to enable the creation of this framework.
In developing the detailed design of the framework, the Government will work with the Financial Conduct Authority, industry, consumer groups and other stakeholders. This will include determining how reasonable access should be secured across different types of essential banking services, the appropriate role of in-person, digital and telephone channels in meeting customers’ needs, and which firms should be in scope.
The framework will be carefully targeted and proportionate, whilst remaining responsive to changes in how people are banking, including the increasing use of digital channels, and support competition and innovation.
The framework will seek to minimise unnecessary costs and the Government will work in partnership with industry to ensure the framework can be delivered effectively and sustainably, including considering how Government and local authorities can remove practical barriers to delivering in-person banking solutions.
Alongside the development of the framework, the Government will work with the banking industry to deliver improvements to access to in-person banking services, so that consumers can benefit from progress ahead of the framework coming into effect. In this context, I welcome the sector’s ongoing work with consumer groups to embed inclusive design across channels.
I would like to extend my thanks to Richard Lloyd OBE for the considerable work he has undertaken in leading this independent Review, and to all those who contributed evidence and shared their experiences.
