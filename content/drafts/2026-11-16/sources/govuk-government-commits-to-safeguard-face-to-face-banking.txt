Government commits to safeguard face-to-face banking
People who rely on in-person banking to have access protected by new government commitment
Government welcomes independent review published today and accepts central recommendation that further action is needed
New statutory framework to be developed alongside FCA, industry, consumer groups and other stakeholders
People, communities and small businesses who need face-to-face banking will have access to essential services protected, under a new government commitment announced by HM Treasury today.
The government has accepted the central recommendation of an independent review, published today (16 November 2026), which found that whilst millions of people choose to bank online, there is a minority of customers who need access to in-person banking services for the foreseeable future.
The review, chaired by Richard Lloyd OBE, was commissioned by HM Treasury to assess whether declining access to in-person banking services is causing consumers harm.
This was informed by more than 18,000 public responses alongside evidence from businesses, charities, consumer groups and industry.
The Review recommends new legal protections to ensure people can continue to access the essential face to face banking services they need. The government has accepted this recommendation and will legislate to protect access.
More than three million people currently regularly rely on banking in person, according to evidence provided to the Review, rising to more than four million when less frequent users are included. Face to face banking is particularly important for older people, disabled people, people on low incomes, those facing negative life events, and those who find digital services harder to use, as they may face difficulties accessing banking online.
Economic Secretary to the Treasury, Lucy Rigby, said:
Millions now rely on banking digitally but for people and businesses who rely on face-to-face services, being able to speak to someone in person really matters. We will act to protect the access they need.
We thank Richard and the thousands who engaged with this review, and we will work alongside FCA, industry and consumer groups to ensure we get this right. Both people’s needs and technology are changing rapidly and it is vital that the new framework is agile and adaptable so that it can respond to our changing world.
In developing the detailed design of the framework, the government will work with the Financial Conduct Authority, industry, consumer groups and other stakeholders. This will include determining what constitutes reasonable access for different types of essential banking services.
The framework will be carefully targeted and proportionate, whilst remaining responsive to changes in how people are increasingly banking digitally, and supporting competition and innovation.
The Government will work in partnership with industry to support the delivery of the framework.

The independent Review into Access to Banking Services found that:
Evidence suggests more than three million adults in the UK are regularly relying on banking services being provided to them in-person, rising to more than four million if those who use in-person services less frequently are included.
Consumers need in-person access to two types of essential banking services: day-to-day banking (centred on opening and managing personal current accounts) and more complex services (linked to unplanned life events or circumstances requiring tailored support).
Overall, UK personal current account holders report high levels of satisfaction with banking services across branches and banking hubs, mobile apps and digital channels, but there are sizeable cohorts which experience access barriers across all these channels.
Declining access to in-person banking has caused widespread, material and recurring harm. Poor consumer outcomes attributable to an inability to access banking services include: a range of financial costs (or positive financial gains foregone), stress, disempowerment and risk-taking, reduced confidence and lost time.
Barriers to access particularly affect older and disabled people, those with mental health conditions, low incomes, low financial resilience or limited capabilities or qualifications, and those facing digital exclusion or negative life events.
Further statistics:
According to Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) data, in 2025, 79.6% of the UK’s population overall were living within three miles of a physical bank branch provided by one of the larger banks and building societies offering personal current accounts (compared to 87% in 2022). Rural coverage was much patchier than urban, and overall distances varied greatly between nations.
The use by consumers of mobile banking has increased significantly in recent years and according to the FCA’s Financial Lives Survey 2024, in the 12 months to 2024, 75% of day-to-day account holders used a mobile banking app.
According to the FCA’s Financial Lives Survey 2024, quarter (26%) of consumers find it difficult or not possible to reach the right person to resolve their query across all channels.
In the FCA’s Financial Lives Survey 2024, 17% of all UK adults indicated they found getting to a bank, building society or credit union difficult using their normal forms of transport.
In responses from members of the public to the Call for Evidence survey, 34% of consumers identified physical access as a difficulty in accessing banking services.
In responses from members of the public to the Call for Evidence survey, older people were the leading demographic mentioned as needing access to in-person banking services at 57.9%, and disabled and vulnerable consumers at 33.7%. Digitally excluded groups were also mentioned by 15.7% of respondents and some form of universal or situational access was mentioned by 42.5% of respondents.
