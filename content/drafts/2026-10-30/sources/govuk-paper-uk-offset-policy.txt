UK Offset Policy
1. General principles
1.1 Legal basis
This policy applies only where the Ministry of Defence (MOD) is lawfully able to require offset as part of a defence or security acquisition from overseas, as well as exempt procurements (“in-scope acquisition”). It shall be applied in a manner consistent with the Procurement Act 2023, other applicable domestic law, and the United Kingdom’s (UK) international obligations. It is not to be applied where a treaty, binding international arrangement or other legal constraint prevents the use of offset requirements.
Nothing in this policy removes the requirement to secure capability, value for money and lawful procurement outcomes. Offset is a separate but linked contractual mechanism intended to secure additional strategic, industrial and economic benefits for the UK from selected acquisitions from abroad.
1.2 Policy objective
Offset related to defence and security acquisitions from abroad shall contribute to maintaining and further developing an innovative, resilient and competitive UK defence industrial base that supports national security and economic growth. The policy is intended to help use selected defence capability imports to produce additional enduring benefits for the UK, including greater sovereign capability, supply chain resilience, skills, innovation, exports and UK small or medium-sized enterprise (SME) growth.
The Policy will use valuation multipliers to prioritise activity that delivers the greatest strategic value. Eligible projects aligned with published capability, industrial, economic and place-based priorities may receive enhanced credit, directing supplier effort towards outcomes of greatest importance to the UK.
1.3 Policy outcomes
The intended outcomes of this policy are to:
maintain and develop strategically important industrial capability, capacity and resilience in the UK
increase inward investment, technology transfer, research and development and innovation aligned to Defence Industrial Strategy priorities
support skills, apprenticeships, know-how transfer and the long-term competitiveness of the UK defence sector
expand opportunities for UK SMEs, mid-tier companies, primes, research organisations and academic institutions
support enduring industrial partnerships and, where appropriate, improve export competitiveness and access to international markets
do so in a way that is targeted, proportionate, accountable, measurable, transparent and consistent with value for money, ensuring no substantial increase in procurement costs and protecting competitive procurement
encourage overseas suppliers to invest and grow their business in the UK
1.4 Procurement authorities
This offset policy is established by the Ministry of Defence and applies to MOD when entering into in-scope acquisition of equipment, services, upgrades, sustainment or other defence and security procurements from abroad.
1.5 Responsible authority
MOD has overall responsibility for this policy, including its management, application, negotiation and any future policy updates. MOD shall designate a suitably qualified and resourced team with delegated authority to manage the implementation, supervision and administration of offset. This team will be known as the Offset Authority, and more detail will be published prior to applying an offset to defence and security procurements in 2027.
The Authority shall:
set and maintain implementation guidance and standard operating procedures
advise acquisition teams on whether and how to apply offset requirements where direct offset is proposed by the supplier
negotiate, approve, administer and enforce offset agreements (OA)
evaluate and approve offset projects, valuations, multipliers and credits
maintain the policy’s strategic priorities and value framework
coordinate with relevant MOD, Whitehall and Arm’s Length Bodies such as Ploughshare, the Office for Investments (OFI) and the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA), as well as stakeholders, including commercial, legal, export, economic and industrial policy functions
monitor delivery, assure compliance and report annually on implementation and results
advise DG International on international offset regimes and the use of reciprocal arrangements to support UK industrial and export interests
The Authority may establish reference groups or advisory mechanisms involving the armed forces, industry, academia and other stakeholders to inform strategic priorities, valuation methodologies or specific projects. The Authority shall retain decision-making independence in relation to approvals, valuation, crediting, milestone management and enforcement.
1.6 Principles of application
This policy shall be applied in accordance with the following principles:
Targeted: it shall be applied selectively to acquisitions where expected strategic value justifies the cost and administrative burden.
Flexible: it shall allow suppliers to develop economically viable and sustainable solutions within clear strategic boundaries.
Measurable: approved activity shall be capable of verification, valuation and crediting against documented evidence.
Additional: credited activity must be additional to normal business and clearly causally linked to the offset requirement.
Transparent: requirements, processes, priorities and expectations shall be made clear to suppliers early in the acquisition process.
Proportionate: policy interventions, milestones and remedies shall reflect delivery risk, strategic value and value for money.
Collaborative: the policy shall encourage enduring industrial partnerships rather than token or short-term transactional activity.
Well governed: implementation shall be led by an appropriately resourced, suitably qualified and empowered Authority.
1.7 Ethical standards and probity
The defence sector and its suppliers are expected to operate to high ethical, professional and compliance standards. The Authority may decline to approve, or may withdraw approval for, projects involving a supplier, third party or UK partner where there are material concerns relating to legal compliance, corruption, fraud, sanctions, national security, conflicts of interest or other serious integrity issues.
1.8 Exemptions and variations
MOD may approve exemptions or variations from this policy in justified circumstances. Such circumstances may include, but are not limited to, acquisitions conducted under binding treaty obligations, multinational programmes in which workshare is predetermined, urgent operational requirements, national security sensitivities, market conditions that would make offset disproportionate, or cases where the expected value of offset would not justify the cost or risk. Suppliers cannot request an exemption.
At the time of publication of this offset policy, transitional arrangements will be in place to manage the initial operation of this policy as it evolves into full operation. Suppliers entering into 10-year OAs will have their obligations agreed consistent with the policy that is in place at the time. Any future changes to the offset policy will not apply retrospectively, such as changing priority areas and associated multipliers, unless negotiated and agreed between the supplier and the authority. The offset policy to which suppliers will be bound will be the agreement signed and cannot be changed, by the supplier or by the authority, without negotiation and agreement.
1.9 Review and amendment
This policy shall be reviewed within 12 months of the effective date and thereafter at intervals of not more than 24 months, or earlier where required to reflect lessons learned, legal developments, changes to strategic priorities or ministerial direction.
2. Scope of application and procedural requirements
2.1 Scope of application
2.1.1 Acquisitions and suppliers subject to this policy
This policy shall normally apply to selected acquisitions from non-UK suppliers of defence or national security materiel, systems, services, support, upgrades or life-cycle activity where the contract value meets the trigger threshold and the Authority determines that application of offset is lawful, proportionate and strategically justified.
The policy may also apply where a supplier is registered in the UK but a significant part of the contract value, production or value creation is located or sourced from abroad.
2.1.2 Definition of a UK-based company
For the purposes of this policy, a UK-based company is a company that has a substantial and enduring UK footprint, undertakes a significant proportion of its relevant work in the UK, and demonstrates meaningful UK offset through its workforce, facilities, intellectual property, supply chain activity, tax and wider economic impact. Further guidance on this definition shall be issued by the Authority to reduce ambiguity and prevent avoidance.
2.1.3 Trigger threshold
The default trigger threshold for application of this policy shall be acquisitions with a contract value of £50 million or more. MOD may apply a higher or lower threshold where justified by market conditions, acquisition profile or implementation capacity.
2.1.4 Application
Offset will be reserved for large, strategically important procurements where the expected industrial, capability or economic value is likely to justify the additional administrative burden and potential cost premium. In deciding whether to apply offset, the Authority shall consider:
the strategic significance of the acquisition to defence and national security
the degree of overseas content and likely displacement of UK capability, skills or spend
the potential to generate enduring industrial, technological, skills, export or resilience benefits
the likely impact on cost, schedule, competition and procurement complexity
priority areas projects will target, the likely routes to value generation, indicative projects or project pipelines, expected delivery timelines, key partners, risks and proposed evidence for valuation and crediting
2.1.5 Multinational programmes, treaties and collaborative arrangements
This Policy shall not normally apply where workshare or industrial outcomes are already governed by an international treaty, memorandum of understanding, collaborative programme framework or other government-to-government arrangement. In exceptional cases, the Authority may require a tailored offset arrangement where lawful and necessary to protect UK interests, but such cases shall be considered individually and handled with particular care.
2.1.6 Additional procurements, options and aggregation
Where an OA is already in force and a further acquisition, option or follow-on contract concerns the same or a related capability, the Authority may increase the supplier’s commitment by a value proportionate to the additional acquisition. The Authority may also aggregate related acquisitions across a period of up to five years where the cumulative value exceeds the trigger threshold.
2.2 Procedural requirements for entering in an offset agreement
2.2.1 Early notice to industry
Where MOD intends to apply the offset policy, this shall be signaled as early as possible in the acquisition process, normally in relevant notices, pre-procurement documentation and tender documentation. Clear and consistent early notice is required to reduce uncertainty, limit unnecessary risk pricing and support fair competition.
2.2.2 Requirement for supplier acceptance
All bidders for an in-scope acquisition shall confirm in writing that, if selected, they will enter into an OA in accordance with this Policy. A selected supplier may not refuse to sign such an agreement as a condition of contract award where the requirement has been properly notified and lawfully applied.
2.2.3 Plan for offset
For acquisitions that meet the trigger threshold, and notice has been given that offsets will apply to the procurement, suppliers shall meet with the Authority no later than three months after signing the OA. The purpose of this meeting is to understand the supplier’s non-binding offset strategy and fulfilment plan. This should include the priority areas projects will target, the likely routes to value generation, indicative projects or project pipelines, expected delivery timelines, key partners, risks and proposed evidence for valuation and crediting.
2.2.4 Negotiation and contracting model
Offset shall normally be managed through a separate OA that is cross-referenced in the main Procurement Contract for enforceability. The acquisition authority shall not sign the main contract until an OA, or an approved alternative arrangement under this policy, has been concluded with the supplier. Separate offset contracting is intended to preserve procurement integrity and reduce distortion to capability contracting while maintaining enforceability.
Separating OAs from the Procurement Contract is intended to avoid delays and adding additional complexities to defence procurement. Managing offset obligations via a separate agreement also enables procurements to focus on tendering for technical requirements, whilst preserving the department’s ability to monitor, evaluate and enforce offset delivery.
The OA will obligate the supplier to deliver 100% of the Procurement Contract value into the UK through projects that are agreed between the supplier and Authority after the OA has been signed by the supplier. No activity is required to be agreed up front between the supplier and Authority prior to signing the agreement.
The standard OA value will be set at 100% of the procurement contract value, including in cases where the cost of the original procurement contract increases. The Authority may set obligations at less than 100% of the procurement contract value, after taking into account the strategic importance of the acquisition, the degree of overseas content, the expected routes to value generation, market conditions, value for money or any other relevant criteria. The policy includes multipliers to incentivise and direct activity towards published priority areas that offer the greatest strategic and enduring value to the UK.
In cases where direct offsets are required, these will be included within the procurement competition and weighted accordingly and a lower contract value obligation will be communicated early in the procurement process.
2.2.5 Standard agreements
The Authority shall maintain standard forms of agreement. These may include: an Offset Agreement (OA), a Conditional Offset Agreement (COA) and a Banking Offset Agreement (BOA).
2.2.6 Conditional and banking arrangements
A COA may be used where a supplier wishes to begin developing offset solutions prior to contract award, or where a series of lower-value contracts may cumulatively exceed the trigger threshold. A BOA may be used to recognise approved credits generated in advance of a future commitment, or to preserve excess credits for use against future commitments, subject to the limits and time periods set by the Authority.
Such limitations include, but are not limited to, activity being additional and causal to the procurement contract, timely in relation to the OA (delivery expected within a reasonable period of no more than five years) and credited for a period of five years from approval.
3. Requirements for offset
3.1 The offset commitment
For each in-scope acquisition, the Authority shall determine the offset commitment as a percentage of the eligible foreign contract value. The standard commitment range shall normally be 100 per cent of the eligible Procurement Contract value. The Authority shall set the commitment after taking account of the strategic importance of the acquisition, the degree of overseas content, the expected routes to value generation, market conditions, value for money, likely cost premium and relevant legal or international considerations.
Suppliers shall provide written disclosure of foreign content and key subcontracting arrangements where required by the Authority.
3.2 Fulfilment of the commitment
The supplier remains fully responsible for fulfilling the commitment. The supplier may use approved parent companies, subsidiaries, associated companies, joint venture partners, subcontractors or other approved third parties to deliver parts of the commitment, provided that the supplier remains legally responsible for performance. All such third parties are subject to prior written approval by the Authority.
Suppliers are expected to clarify any intention to flow down offset obligations to subsidiaries or organisations in their supply chains.
3.3 Types of offset activity
Recognised offset activity shall fall within one or more of the following categories:
Direct offset: activity specifically associated with the product or service being acquired, such as:
additional onshore support
sustainment
upgrades
technology transfer
workshare linked to the procured capability
Direct offset shall be used selectively because it can introduce additional procurement risk, delay or cost if applied inflexibly.
Direct activity will, where applicable, be carried out in conjunction with FLCs via subordinate frameworks such as the Land Industrial Strategy Objectives Framework (LISOF) and the Army Critical Industrial Sectors List.
Indirect offset (defence and security): activity not directly tied to the procured capability but supporting the UK defence and security sector, including:
supply chain integration
innovation
research and development
test and evaluation
manufacturing capability
skills and exports
Hybrid approaches: a supplier may be permitted to use a combination of direct and indirect activity where this offers the best balance of:
strategic value
practicality
value for money
3.4 Priority areas and eligible routes to value generation
Priority areas for value generation will be set by a cross Government Strategic Outcomes Board, led by the MOD. The Authority shall maintain and publish implementation guidance setting out current strategic priorities and valuation approaches to translate strategic priorities into eligible routes for value generation to support suppliers in scoping activities and developing proposals.
Suppliers may discharge their offset obligations through a range of activities that generate measurable strategic, industrial and economic value for the UK. This could include placing contracts with UK businesses for goods and services that create or sustain strategically important defence capabilities, strengthen the resilience and functioning of the UK economy, or integrate UK firms, including SMEs and mid-tier companies, into domestic and global supply chains.
Value may also be delivered through technology and know-how transfer, licensing arrangements, access to intellectual property, research and development, experimentation, prototyping and innovation activities undertaken with UK industry, academia or research organisations. In addition, suppliers may invest in UK facilities, expand enduring support capabilities, improve security of supply, production capacity, infrastructure, skills and supply chain resilience, support exports and international market access for UK partners, establish additional maintenance, repair, overhaul, sustainment or upgrade capability in the UK, and deliver training, apprenticeships and technical education aligned to defence and national security priorities.
The Authority may also recognise other activities that are demonstrably additional, assessable, strategically valuable and consistent with the objectives of the policy.
3.5 Strategic priority areas
Offset solutions shall, where possible, support current Defence Industrial Strategy and national security priorities. Without limiting future updates, present priority areas may include:
sovereign industrial capability, resilience, enduring UK support capabilities, and security of supply
Combat Air, Next Generation Land and Maritime Capabilities, Directed Energy Weapons and Complex Weapons
Quantum technologies, Drones and autonomous systems, Space, AI, Cyber, Engineering Biology, Advanced Connectivity Technologies, and Semiconductors
advanced manufacturing, digital engineering and production scale-up
uncrewed systems, counter-uncrewed systems and missile technologies
space, cyber, electronic warfare, big data, artificial intelligence and machine learning
advanced materials, energetics, propulsion, sensing, autonomy and other strategic technologies
skills, apprenticeships and workforce development
defence exports, international supply chain integration and regional industrial growth
innovation, dual-use technology and non-traditional suppliers, including SMEs
The Authority may issue more detailed annual or programme-specific priority guidance, including where priorities are linked to implementation plans, growth sectors, export objectives, the Strategic Defence Review or ministerial direction.
3.6 UK partners
Approved UK partners may include UK-based primes, mid-tier companies, SMEs, research and technology organisations, universities, Catapult centres, public laboratories, innovation bodies and, where justified, armed forces or other public sector entities involved in sustainment, engineering competence or testing. A UK partner must be able to demonstrate the UK location and value of the relevant activity.
3.7 Additionality and causality
Offset activity will be additional to ordinary business and causally linked to the MOD policy. Pre-existing business, routine recurring activity or commitments already underway shall not normally receive credit unless the supplier can demonstrate a material increase in value, scope, technological content, market access or UK benefit directly attributable to the offset requirement. All creditable activity shall require prior written approval by the Authority.
3.8 Project approval and solution development
All offset projects require written approval by the Authority before implementation if they are to be eligible for credit. The supplier shall present a project proposal in the format specified by the Authority, including the project description, participating entities, category of activity, strategic fit, expected UK value added, proposed valuation, requested multipliers, delivery milestones, evidence requirements, risk assessment and any dependencies.
The Authority will publish proportionate service standards as part of its implementation guidance and seek to provide timely decisions reflecting the complexity of each proposal. It shall focus on evaluating whether a proposed solution is strategically relevant, additional, economically viable, measurable and likely to deliver enduring benefit. The Authority may engage with suppliers pragmatically to improve, substitute or restructure proposals, provided that consistency, fairness and auditability are maintained.
3.9 Alignment with social value and other policies.
Offset is separate from Social Value and other procurement policies such as the LISOF. However, where an approved activity clearly contributes to both offset objectives and Social Value outcomes, the Authority may allow the same activity to be recognised under both regimes, if this is permitted by relevant guidance, is transparently documented and does not create duplication, double counting or inconsistent incentives. The Authority shall issue implementation guidance on how this interaction is to be managed.
Procurements in scope of offset that also have a Social Value commitment will be assumed to treat Social Value in the standard way through the tendering process, with offset obligations arranged separately after contract award. The department will issue future guidance on both policies in due course.
3.10 Export support and abatement
The Authority may recognise activity that improves UK export competitiveness or helps UK businesses enter new markets, including support to global supply chain integration, market access and collaborative export campaigns. The Authority may also advise DG International on the use of abatements or reciprocal arrangements to reduce or remove offset requirements imposed by foreign governments on UK suppliers where this supports UK industrial and export interests.
3.11 Valuation
3.11.1 General valuation principles
The Authority shall use a transparent value framework that is consistent with the HMT Green Book principles to determine the creditable value of approved projects. Valuation shall take account of the nature of the activity, defence benefits, other expected UK value added, strategic significance, durability of the benefit, risks of non-delivery and the evidence available to support verification. The Authority may issue more detailed valuation guidance and worked examples.
Valuation may be based, as appropriate, on one or more of the following:
the value of contracts placed with UK entities, adjusted to reflect likely supply side benefits to the UK economy
the value of investment made in UK facilities, capabilities, infrastructure, skills or technology
the value of technology, know-how or intellectual property transferred to a UK entity
the value of research and development, prototype or innovation activity undertaken in the UK
the value of export sales, market access or other commercial outcomes that can be attributed to the approved activity
the value of training and skills development where this creates enduring capability and is evidenced appropriately
other agreed indicators of tangible or intangible benefit, including resilience, capability development or cluster effects, where the Authority is satisfied that these can clearly be evidenced and valued credibly
3.11.2 Multipliers
The Authority will apply valuation multipliers to incentivise offset activity that delivers exceptional or enduring value against the Government’s defence, security, industrial and economic priorities, as set out below:
Location
Enhanced credit may be applied to activity that:
strengthens regional industrial clusters and supply chains, particularly where activity builds on existing strengths, supports Defence Growth Deals, or generates sustainable economic benefit in priority places
contributes to the Government’s reindustrialisation objectives through the establishment, expansion or modernisation of industrial capacity in the UK
supports levelling up, place-based growth and the development of resilient local and regional defence ecosystems
creates wider system-level benefits by connecting investment across firms, institutions, supply chains and places
Activity
Enhanced credit may be applied to activity that:
advances the Defence Growth Framework, including productivity, innovation, research and development, technology and know-how transfer, inward investment, exports and the creation of high-value employment
develops critical skills and workforce pipelines through apprenticeships, technical education, specialist training and the transfer of expertise
enables the development, commercialisation, scaling or adoption of innovative and frontier technologies
improves the export competitiveness and international market access of UK businesses or secures enduring participation in global programmes and supply chains
increases opportunities for UK SMEs, mid-tier firms, scale-ups and non-traditional suppliers, including their integration into domestic and international supply chains
Sector
Enhanced credit may be applied to activity that:
develops or sustains sovereign and strategically important industrial capabilities, addresses critical supply-chain vulnerabilities or improves security of supply
supports priority capabilities, technologies and sectors identified through the Defence Industrial Strategy, Defence Investment Plan, Strategic Defence Review, Modern Industrial Strategy and associated sector frameworks
contributes to the establishment, expansion or modernisation of strategically important manufacturing, production, maintenance, repair, overhaul, sustainment, test and evaluation capabilities within the UK
generates system-level benefits across strategically important capabilities, technologies and industrial ecosystems
The level of multiplier applied shall reflect the activity’s strategic importance, additionality, durability, UK value added, delivery risk and alignment with published priorities. Multipliers may be combined where an activity delivers materially distinct benefits across more than one priority, but shall not reward the same benefit more than once.
The Authority shall maintain, publish and periodically update detailed guidance setting out targeted priority areas, eligibility criteria and multiplier treatment, allowing these to respond to ministerial direction, industrial and capability needs, changes in the Growth Framework and emerging opportunities. Ministers must publish details of the Offset policy’s multipliers to Parliament once every five years.
3.11.4 Offset credits and credit claims process
An offset credit is the formal recognition by the Authority that an approved offset activity has delivered a defined amount of value for the purposes of discharging a supplier’s commitment. Credits shall normally be awarded in arrears following delivery and verification. The supplier shall submit a credit claim in the format prescribed by the Authority together with supporting evidence. The Authority may approve the claim in full, approve it in part, defer a decision pending further evidence, or reject it while stating the reasons for rejection.
3.12 Fulfilment period, milestones and achievement period
OAs shall specify the fulfilment period. The standard fulfilment period shall normally be up to ten years from signature of the agreement, though the Authority may set a shorter or longer period where justified by the nature of the acquisition or the approved project portfolio.
Unless otherwise agreed, the following standard milestone profile shall apply:
by the end of Year 3: 30 per cent of the commitment
by the end of Year 5: 50 per cent of the commitment
by the end of Year 10: 100 per cent of the commitment
The Authority may vary milestones where this would better support strategic value, early investment, long-lead innovation or export outcomes, provided that any variation remains documented, justified and enforceable. The Authority may show flexibility where a supplier is proactively engaged and the evidence indicates that a strategically valuable solution remains viable but requires adjustment.
3.13 Reporting and monitoring
By 31 March each year, the supplier shall submit an annual report setting out activity delivered in the previous calendar year, progress against milestones, risks, updated project pipelines and any associated claims for credit. The Authority shall respond by 30 September of the same year or within such other period as may be specified in the agreement. The supplier may not normally claim credit for activity that is more than two years old unless the Authority approves otherwise.
The supplier, approved third parties and UK partners must provide access to records and information reasonably required by the Authority for assurance, audit, valuation and compliance purposes.
3.14 Penalties and remedies
The UK Offset Policy is designed to enable defence industry to be strategic partners in investing in the UK defence industrial base. As such, the UK’s model will prioritise incentives and meaningful partnerships to enter into OAs and will work with suppliers to the greatest extent possible to ensure offset obligations can be delivered in a flexible way to reasonable timeframes. Penalties are a measure of last resort for instances where suppliers are not engaging with the policy or demonstrate persistent failure to meet their obligations.
Where a supplier fails to meet a milestone or otherwise breaches its OA, the Authority may apply one or more contractual remedies.
These may include:
financial compensation
a requirement to provide an irrevocable bank guarantee
withholding or adjustment of credits
reputational consequences
or exclusion from future relevant competitions until the breach is remedied, where lawful and proportionate.
Unless otherwise agreed, financial compensation for a missed milestone shall normally be not less than 10 per cent of the unfulfilled value at that milestone. Payment of compensation will extinguish a proportionate amount of the underlying commitment. Where the supplier remains actively engaged and the Authority is satisfied that extension with revised terms will better secure strategic value than immediate enforcement, the Authority may agree a pragmatic remedy, including additional time. This flexibility shall not weaken the Authority’s right to apply stronger remedies where a supplier fails to engage or persistently underperforms.
4. Transfer of credit, banking and abatement
4.1 Banking of credit
A supplier may apply to enter into a BOA under which approved credits generated in advance of a future commitment, or credits generated in excess of an existing commitment, may be preserved for future use. Each banking arrangement shall specify the categories of eligible activity, the maximum amount of credit that can be banked and the period for which the credit remains valid. Unless otherwise agreed, banked credit shall normally expire after five years.
4.2 Transfer of credit
Credit held under a banking arrangement may, with the Authority’s prior written approval, be transferred within the supplier’s corporate group. In exceptional circumstances, the Authority may approve transfer to a third party where this is in the interests of the policy. Unless otherwise agreed, transferred banked credit shall not constitute more than 50 per cent of the commitment under the agreement to which it is transferred.
4.3 Abatements and reciprocal arrangements
The Authority may advise MOD and wider government on the use of abatements, reciprocal undertakings or other mechanisms to reduce or remove offset obligations applied by foreign governments to UK suppliers. Any such arrangement must support UK strategic, industrial or export interests and shall be considered separately from ordinary crediting decisions.
5. Reporting, follow-up and transparency
5.1 Annual reporting by the Authority
The Authority shall prepare an annual internal report on implementation of this policy. The report shall cover the number and value of agreements in force, progress against commitments, value generated, strategic outcomes, use of multipliers, emerging risks, lessons learned, resource implications and any recommendations for policy or implementation changes.
5.2 Public disclosure
OAs, COAs and BOAs and project-level details shall normally be handled as commercially sensitive and confidential and may also be sensitive for national security reasons. However, MOD may disclose the existence of agreements, the supplier name, the total commitment value, the extent of its fulfilment and high-level examples of value generated where this is lawful and does not create commercial, diplomatic or security harm.
The Authority will maintain a list of obligors with extant OAs, COAs and BOAs and the remaining value of obligation to be discharged to enable greater partnerships with UK industry. This list will be publicly available via gov.uk and through the Defence Office for Small Business Growth.
5.3 Evaluation
Implementation of this policy shall be subject to periodic evaluation. The Authority shall ensure that the evidence base captures both tangible and intangible benefits, including jobs, investment, exports, technology, skills, innovation, resilience and wider spillover effects where practicable. Evaluation will inform future policy calibration, including thresholds, multipliers, staffing, processes and the relationship with other policy levers such as Social Value, export support and other frameworks that deliver industrial benefit through procurements (LISOF).
Evaluation will take place via standard data collection processes as part of the management of OAs, COAs and BOAs by the offset authority. These activities will take place throughout the obligation period, primarily through formal evaluation milestones at years 3, 5 and 10 in addition to informal data collection that may be required by the Authority throughout the obligation period.
6. Breach, disputes and final provisions
6.1 Governing law and disputes
OAs, COAs and BOAs shall be governed by the laws of England and Wales or, where appropriate, the laws of Scotland or Northern Ireland unless otherwise agreed. Disputes shall be handled in accordance with the dispute resolution provisions set out in the relevant agreement, which may provide for the courts of England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, as appropriate, or for arbitration.
6.2 Consequences of breach
Failure to fulfil an OA, COA or BOA, including failure to meet milestones, reporting obligations, audit requirements or other material commitments, may be taken into account by MOD in relevant future procurements in accordance with applicable law. Serious or persistent breach may lead to exclusion from future competitions for in-scope acquisition until the breach is remedied, subject to specific legal advice and the terms of the relevant agreement.
6.3 Entry into force
This policy shall come into effect on the date approved by the Secretary of State or other competent authority.
Key design features and operational definitions
Key design features
Trigger threshold: the default threshold for mandatory consideration of offset is £50 million, subject to review and adjustment as implementation matures.
Contract commitment: the Authority shall set an obligation at 100 per cent of eligible foreign contract value.
Value generation routes: recognised routes include contracts with UK entities, technology transfer, know-how transfer, investment, R&D, innovation, skills, exports, supply chain integration, MRO and sustainment capability.
Multipliers: strategic multipliers may be applied cumulatively to reward activity in priority sectors, technologies, SME growth, innovation, exports, resilience and regional development.
Offset credits: credits are the formal mechanism by which delivered and evidenced value is recognised against a supplier’s commitment and will be granted upon project delivery.
Credit claims process: the supplier must submit evidence in the prescribed form and credits are awarded only after Authority review and verification.
Strategic priorities: the Authority shall maintain and periodically update priority areas aligned to the Defence Industrial Strategy and wider national security objectives.
Performance period: the standard fulfilment period is ten years unless varied by the Authority.
Milestones: the standard milestone profile is 30 per cent by Year 3, 50 per cent by Year 5 and 100 per cent by Year 10, with flexibility where strategically justified.
Penalty and remedy framework: remedies may include financial compensation, bank guarantees, increased commitments in return for additional time, reputational consequences and exclusion from future competitions where lawful and proportionate.
Offset solution development: suppliers shall work with the Authority through a defined project approval process to negotiate, approve and manage viable delivery solutions.
Additionality and causality: activity must be new or materially enhanced, and causally linked to the offset requirement, to be eligible for credit.
Banking agreements: approved credits may be banked in advance or preserved on over-delivery for future use.
Abatements: the Authority may advise on reciprocal or bilateral arrangements to reduce offset burdens on UK exporters.
Separate contracting: offset shall normally be agreed and managed via an OA separately from the procurement contract, while remaining linked to it for enforcement.
Authority model: the policy relies on a suitably qualified, experienced and empowered Authority with the capacity to negotiate value, manage risk and enforce delivery.
Operational definitions
Offset Obligation: the quantified commitment accepted by an obligor to deliver agreed offset value within a specified fulfilment period.
Obligor: An overseas supplier that has entered into an OA and is therefore responsible for delivering an offset obligation.
Direct offset: activity clearly connected to the product or service being acquired.
Indirect offset (defence and security): activity not directly connected to the acquired product or service but benefiting the UK defence and security sector.
Indirect offset (wider economy): activity outside the defence and security sector that delivers an approved strategic benefit to the UK.
SME: a small or medium-sized enterprise as defined in current UK guidance, unless the Authority specifies otherwise for policy purposes.
UK value added: the estimated UK value arising from the additional activity, spend, capability, IP, jobs or investment that is genuinely generated in the UK and can be evidenced to the satisfaction of the Authority.
Offset Agreement: the binding agreement entered into between an obligor and the Offset Authority that sets out the offset obligation, delivery arrangements, milestones and reporting requirements, remedies and other applicable terms. It is separate from, but cross-referenced in, the underlying Procurement Contract.
Procurement Contract: the underlying MOD contract for the acquisition of defence or security equipment, services, upgrades, sustainment or other capability to which an offset requirement relates.
