Mayors and local leaders work with No10 North to drive search for 'top 10' biggest strategic investment sites across country
Mayors and local leaders will help identify opportunities as government works to unlock up to 10 major industrial and commercial sites across the UK.
The Strategic Sites Accelerator will deploy around £700 million, alongside expert support to tackle barriers including planning, grid connections and access to finance.
Programme aims to crowd in up to £2 billion in private investment and could support tens of thousands of jobs over time.
Tens of thousands of jobs and up to £2 billion in private investment over time will be targeted across the UK, as our regions are backed with a £700 million package of support to help drive reindustrialisation.
Starting today [Monday 2 November], the Strategic Sites Accelerator will deploy around £700 million alongside expert support to help unlock up to 10 major industrial and commercial sites across the country, helping some of the UK’s most promising investment opportunities get off the ground.
The Accelerator will focus on commercially viable sites with strong investor demand, where government backing can help overcome the barriers stopping development from moving ahead.
It will tackle the key barriers holding back high-potential sites that could support Britain’s reindustrialisation. That includes everything from access to finance, grid connections, and planning constraints, removing the blockers that can cool would-be investors.
The Accelerator will support sites in a range of ways, from loans and equity and targeted grant funding to technical expertise, bringing government, local partners and developers together to tackle specific barriers.

The Accelerator is already engaging on potential sites in Glasgow, Burgess Hill and Sheffield with technical support and expertise, and will back our Mayors and local authorities with blended finance across the country.
This includes exploring investment opportunities in Greater Manchester, West Yorkshire, West Midlands, South Yorkshire, Liverpool City Region, the North East, Glasgow City Region and Cardiff Capital Region – building on the National Wealth Fund’s strategic partnerships in these regions.
The Accelerator will also be open to sites across every nation and region of the UK, from science parks and manufacturing sites to innovation districts held back by viability constraints.
Bringing together the Office for Investment and the National Wealth Fund, with CBRE as its National Delivery Partner, the Accelerator will work with investors, developers and regional partners on commercially credible sites where there is genuine market demand, using finance and technical support to tackle the specific barriers preventing investment or development from going ahead.
No10 North will work alongside the programme and local leaders to support the government’s wider drive to identify investment opportunities across the country. This includes working closely with the Accelerator to exchange pipelines of priority projects in support of the National Economic Council.
Marking a major step forward for the Government’s Industrial Strategy, the scheme aims to unlock up to 10 strategic sites across the UK’s high-growth sectors like advanced manufacturing, digital and tech, defence, and life sciences.

Background
The Strategic Sites Accelerator is designed to unlock strategically important commercial and industrial sites where credible market demand exists but barriers are preventing development.
Around £700 million will be available through equity, debt and targeted grant funding, alongside technical and advisory support. The ambition is to crowd in up to £2 billion of private investment.
The Government’s public ambition is to support six to 10 sites. Individual investments will be subject to the necessary commercial assessment, due diligence and approvals.
Employment will depend on the sites and investments ultimately brought forward. The agreed formulation is that, if sites address their viability constraints, tens of thousands of jobs could be created directly and through the wider economy over time.
Government is also giving mayors a greater stake in the growth they generate, with a share of business rates from 2027 and an assignment of income tax from 2028.
A further £285 million will support areas moving towards devolution deals or Strategic Authority status, including £120 million of capital funding to help places develop and deliver investment opportunities.
