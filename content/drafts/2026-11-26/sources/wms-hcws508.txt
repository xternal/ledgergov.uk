This Government is committed to halving violence against women and girls (VAWG) in a decade. A key part of that mission is transforming the response to stalking, which is a uniquely intrusive and devastating crime. Around 1.9 million people aged 16 and over experienced stalking in the year ending March 2026. For victims, the impact can be constant and life-changing, stripping away their sense of safety, freedom and control.
While important progress has been made since stalking became a specific criminal offence in 2012, too many victims continue to face inconsistent experiences across the criminal justice system. Concerns about how stalking is understood, identified and investigated were highlighted by the super-complaint on the police response to stalking cases, which also raised questions about whether the law is sufficiently clear and effective. In response, the Government committed to an independent review of stalking legislation.
In October 2025, the Home Office commissioned Richard Wright KC to lead that review. Drawing on extensive engagement with victims, practitioners and criminal justice partners, it examined whether the current legislative framework provides a clear, coherent and effective response to stalking, including its interaction with related offences such as harassment and controlling or coercive behaviour. While Stalking Protection Orders (SPOs) were outside the formal scope of the review, it also considered the implications that any legislative reform might have for this important safeguarding tool.
Today, the Government has published Richard Wright KC's final report alongside its response, and both will be placed in the Libraries of both Houses. The Government agrees with the review's central conclusion that the legal framework should be clearer, more coherent and better able to distinguish stalking from related offending. We have therefore committed to introducing a new standalone stalking offence, replacing the current two offences. In designing the new offence, we will consider the range of features recommended by the review to ensure the law better reflects the reality of stalking and provides stronger protection for victims.
Our response takes a balanced approach. We have carefully considered where legislative reform is needed and where improvements can instead be delivered through policy, operational practice or guidance. Reforming the law is important, but legislation alone will not transform outcomes for victims. The Government intends to legislate as soon as Parliamentary time allows. At the same time, we will continue to drive improvements across the criminal justice system, making better use of existing powers, strengthening guidance and improving consistency in the response to stalking. Together, these measures will strengthen protection for victims, improve accountability for perpetrators and support our wider mission to halve violence against women and girls within a decade.
