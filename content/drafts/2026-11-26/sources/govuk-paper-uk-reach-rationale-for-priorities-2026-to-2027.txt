UK REACH: rationale for priorities 2026 to 2027
Introduction
The UK’s registration, evaluation, authorisation and restriction of chemicals (UK REACH) regulations are part of the chemicals regulatory framework for Great Britain (England, Scotland and Wales). A key aim of UK REACH is to provide a high level of protection of human health and the environment from the use of chemicals.
The UK REACH work programme covers a range of activities and sets out how the Health and Safety Executive (HSE), with the support of the Environment Agency, will deliver its regulatory activities in the financial year 2026 to 2027. HSE is the regulatory agency for UK REACH.
The work programme includes restrictions and time-limited authorisations of substances (subject to specific conditions) which are important risk management tools under UK REACH. The Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra) and the Scottish and Welsh ministers are the ‘appropriate authorities’ for UK REACH. We (the appropriate authorities) have worked with HSE and the Environment Agency to identify priorities for the UK REACH work programme in financial year 2026 to 2027.
This document explains Defra and the Scottish and Welsh Governments’ agreed overall policy priorities for UK REACH in financial year 2026 to 2027.
Strategic approach
In December 2025, Defra published the Environmental Improvement Plan (EIP), which sets out the government’s commitments for restoring nature and improving the environment. Commitment 40 states that we will: “Reform UK Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation, and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) to enable protections that address chemical pollution to be applied more quickly, efficiently and in a way that is more aligned with our closest trading partners, especially the EU, by December 2028”.
We will continue to make regulatory decisions in the best interest of Great Britain, drawing on the best available evidence and, where appropriate, on decisions made by trusted jurisdictions, especially the EU. By doing this we can reduce business complexity and trade barriers and provide industry with the certainty they need to grow and invest, while continuing to protect people and the environment in the right way. HSE and the Environment Agency are contributing operational advice to help Defra and the devolved governments as they are developing policy in line with this commitment.
Priorities for the financial year 2026 to 2027 UK REACH work programme
1. Restrictions
By the end of 2026, HSE aims to finalise its opinion on proposals for a UK REACH restriction on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in fire-fighting foams. A decision on whether to implement a restriction will be taken by the Defra Secretary of State, with the consent of the Scottish and Welsh ministers, in due course.
In line with commitment 40 of the EIP, we intend to continue policy development work to introduce an additional restriction regulatory process via legislation by December 2028. With this new process, we would take assessments and decisions made by trusted jurisdictions, especially the EU, as the starting point for UK regulatory decisions, unless there are compelling reasons to diverge. This approach aims to enable protections to be applied more quickly, efficiently and in a way that is more aligned with our closest trading partners, especially the EU. By doing this, we aim to establish a stable, predictable and consistent approach to chemicals regulation, which provides industry with the certainty they need to grow and invest in the UK.
While the financial year 2026 to 2027 work programme does not initiate any new restriction workstreams, the appropriate authorities, HSE and the Environment Agency continue to monitor and identify emerging chemical risks to ensure they are addressed.
2. Authorisation
The primary objective of authorisation is to control the risks from substances of very high concern (SVHCs) on the authorisation list (Annex 14) while driving the substitution of these substances where this is technically and economically feasible. This work programme will see the continuation of the reviewing and granting of specific authorisations where appropriate, with a view to ensuring the risks of substances are properly controlled, and encouraging substitution efforts by industry in the review periods granted. Since 2021, the appropriate authorities have considered and completed over 60 applications.
3. Additions to the candidate list and authorisation list
On 24 February 2026, the government published a new approach to the UK REACH candidate list of substances of very high concern. This replaced the interim principles that had applied since 2021 and gives practical effect to commitment 40 of the EIP. Under the new approach, substances added to the EU REACH candidate list since 1 January 2021 are being reviewed and, where appropriate, added to the UK REACH candidate list. This enables new protections to be applied in a way which is more aligned with the EU. HSE, as the Agency for UK REACH, retains the ability to identify further SVHCs where UK circumstances warrant it.
Following publication of the new approach, HSE began reviewing the additions the EU has made to its candidate list since 1 January 2021. Where substances have been assessed to meet the SVHC criteria set out in the regulation, HSE has started consulting on their proposed inclusion and, to date, 24 substances have been added to the UK REACH candidate list following this policy change. This work forms a key strand of the 2026 to 2027 work programme, and HSE will take the necessary steps to consult on further batches of substances during the year.
During the financial year 2025 to 2026, HSE formulated its recommendation for SVHCs to be moved from the candidate list to the authorisation list (Annex 14), in line with its statutory obligation to make such a recommendation at least every 2 years.
Defra, Scottish and Welsh ministers have taken HSE’s recommendation into account and are minded to add 3 substances to the UK REACH authorisation list:
tetraethyl lead (TEL)
DOTE
the reaction mass of DOTE and MOTE
A decision to add a substance to the authorisation list (Annex 14) is implemented by statutory instrument. Defra is currently aiming for the decision-making process and any applicable implementation process to be completed in 2027. Any new authorisation list (Annex 14) entries will include the latest application date and sunset date for each substance.
The 3 substances are also on the EU authorisation list.
The 2026 to 2027 work programme also includes a review of the document setting out HSE’s approach to recommending priority substances for inclusion in the authorisation list (Annex 14). The document was first produced in 2021. It will be assessed for its potential to support the government’s position on decisions taken in the EU and elsewhere, in accordance with the recommendations in the authorisation list (Annex 14).
