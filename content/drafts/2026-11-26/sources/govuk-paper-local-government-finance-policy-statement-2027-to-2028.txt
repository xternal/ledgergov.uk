Local government finance policy statement 2027 to 2028
Introduction
1. Local government is at the heart of this government’s ambition to rewire the state, empowering local leaders to deliver good growth in every postcode. Local authorities provide the foundations on which successful devolution depends, delivering the services communities rely on and shaping local economies. Strategic authorities can only be as strong as their constituent local authorities, making local authorities’ financial sustainability central to successful devolution.
2. Last year via the Fair Funding Review 2.0 and the first multi-year Local Government Finance Settlement (‘the Settlement’) in a decade, we committed to fixing the foundations of local government and took an important first step in delivering a new funding system. The 2026-27 Settlement made available around £78 billion in Core Spending Power for local authorities in England, a 6.1% increase compared to 2025-26. We are investing in local government, and provided over £5.6 billion of new grant funding towards local government services over the next 3 years.
3. For the first time since 2013-14, the government updated the relative needs formulas that form a key part of how local authorities’ funding allocations are calculated. By using more up-to-date data, we are able to assess local authorities’ relative demand for services more effectively. We consulted extensively on these reforms and are grateful for the engagement from the sector throughout that process. As a result, we are now targeting money where it is needed most , meaning 9 in 10 councils will receive funding that broadly matches their assessed relative need by the end of the multi-year Settlement, up from around a third before our reforms. That means public money is being more efficiently spent on local services. We also kept our promise to end complex and wasteful, competitive bidding and siloed grant pots, and simplified an unprecedented 38 revenue funding streams, worth almost £57 billion over 3 years. This will provide greater stability, certainty, and flexibility to local authorities.
4. We know that longer-term certainty is vital for local authorities to plan effectively. That is why we delivered the first multi-year Settlement in a decade, and why we do not intend to make substantial changes to its methodology and distribution this year. The 2027-28 Settlement builds on the multi-year foundations, making available over £80 billion in Core Spending Power, an increase of over £3 billion compared to last year.
5. This Policy Statement therefore sets out the government’s current intentions for the 2027-28 Settlement, providing local authorities with early certainty on key aspects of their funding to support their budget-setting. It confirms our intention to maintain the multi-year framework, alongside the updates and further reforms set out below. Further proposals and local authority allocations will be set out at the provisional Settlement in December.
6. This Settlement also takes a crucial step in delivering measures set out in the Fiscal Devolution Roadmap by confirming the incorporation of mayoral strategic authorities into the business rates retention system. The Roadmap confirmed the government’s intention for local and mayoral strategic authorities collectively to retain 100% of business rates by the end of the decade, in place of central government grant, with local authorities retaining at least 70%. Moving towards a larger, long-term share of locally retained revenues will give councils greater flexibility and certainty over their resources and allow them to benefit from the growth they help create, supporting more effective long-term planning and a more sustainable funding system.
7. From 2027-28, we are taking the first step towards this ambition by introducing a 20% mayoral share of business rates, with further detail on the path towards 100% retention, including increasing the share retained by local authorities, to be set out in due course. In practice, this means swapping several existing mayoral grants for a share of retained business rates of the same value, alongside the ability for mayoral strategic authorities to retain 20% of future growth in business rates until the system is next reset.
8. These changes sit alongside wider reforms to tackle the high and rising costs of services including adult and children’s social care, homelessness and SEND, helping to put the sector on a more sustainable footing. This government is under no illusion about the scale of the issues facing local government. We are committed to working with the sector to shape these reforms and build on the certainty we are providing now to support its long-term sustainability.
9. We are tackling the service pressures facing councils. We are investing nearly £3 billion over 4 years in the biggest transformation of children’s social care in a generation, including a historic £2.4 billion through this multi-year Settlement. This will help shift the system from expensive statutory provision towards early-intervention and preventative support. In addition, we are investing £4 billion over this year and the next 2 years to reform SEND, putting more expertise in communities and funding into early intervention so families get support sooner.
10. The Prime Minister has set out his vision for a National Care Service providing free personal care for older people, focused on prevention and supporting people to live independent and fulfilling lives; we are already laying the foundations, with the multi-year Settlement making over £4.6 billion additional funding available for adult social care in 2028-29 compared with 2025-26. Further investment in rough sleeping and social and council housing also supports our ambition to reduce homelessness and provide more secure homes.
Local Government Finance Settlement 2027-28
Summary
The 2027-28 Settlement builds on the first multi-year Settlement in a decade, continuing to invest in local government whilst providing greater certainty over local authorities’ funding. Overall, the Settlement will make available over £80 billion in Core Spending Power, an increase of over £3 billion compared with 2026-27.
We will continue to deliver the Fair Funding Review 2.0 reforms, targeting funding where it is needed most whilst maintaining the multi-year framework and transitional protections that give local authorities greater certainty over their funding.
Within that framework, the 2027-28 Settlement will also:
Take the first step towards greater fiscal devolution, introducing a 20% mayoral share of business rates from April 2027 in place of existing central government grants, alongside the ability for mayoral strategic authorities to retain 20% of future business rates growth.
Support the government’s commitment to tackle rough sleeping, following the Autumn Budget announcement of a further £432 million for the Rough Sleeping Programme. We expect revenue funding to be added to the existing Homelessness, Rough Sleeping and Domestic Abuse Grant consolidated grant within the Settlement, and included within Core Spending Power, for 2027-28 and 2028-29.
Increase Baseline Funding Levels in line with inflation as outlined in the multi-year framework methodology last year.
Build on the significant funding simplification delivered last year, with proposals to simplify further funding streams to give local authorities greater control over their resources.
Update council tax levels and council tax base growth forecasts using the latest available data, providing a more accurate picture of resources available to local authorities in line with the multi-year methodology.
Continue to support local authorities to manage their updated funding positions through the transitional arrangements announced last year, protecting authorities that would otherwise see their income significantly fall as a result of the reforms.
As set out last year, the Adjustment Support Grant provided one-off support in 2026-27. In line with the multi-year arrangements previously announced, it is not proposed to form part of the 2027-28 Settlement, subject to consultation.
Further detail, including provisional local authority allocations, will be set out as part of the provisional Settlement in December.
Core Settlement
11. We are committed to the multi-year framework and methodology set out in the Fair Funding Review 2.0 and the 2026-2027 Settlement. This gives stability and certainty and includes maintaining the methodology and level of transitional protections already announced, rather than recalculating them.
12. Local authorities will see an increase in baseline funding levels (BFLs) next year. The BFLs for 2027-28 will be updated to reflect the September 2025 to September 2026 change in the Consumer Price Index. The BFLs for 2028-29 will be updated to reflect the latest Office for Budget Responsibility forecast of the Consumer Price Index, published at Autumn Budget 2026.
13. Recognising their unique circumstances, bespoke arrangements for the Isles of Scilly and City of London, as set out at the 2026-27 Settlement, will remain.
14. For the purposes of calculating Core Spending Power at the multi-year Settlement, we necessarily made assumptions around council tax levels for 2026-27, which we are now updating in line with the methodology we set out last year. We will update the forecast 2026-27 council tax levels data with actual 2026-27 data, rebasing the 2027-28 and 2028-29 forecasts from this updated position.
15. Similarly, we necessarily made assumptions around council tax base growth in the calculation of Core Spending Power for 2027-28 and 2028-29 using the long-standing methodology using the most recent 5 years of historic data. We will continue with this approach but update the historic period to include 2026-27. This will not impact Fair Funding Assessment allocations.
Council tax referendum principles
16. Referendum principles strike a fair balance between protecting taxpayers and giving councils flexibility to make decisions locally. The principles are not a cap, nor do they force local authorities to set taxes at the threshold level.
17. The government intends to maintain the following proposed referendum principles as set out at the multi-year settlement:
a core council tax referendum limit for local authorities of up to 3%
a council tax referendum principle of up to 3% or £5, whichever is higher, for shire district councils
an adult social care precept of 2% for all authorities responsible for adult social care services
a council tax principle of £5 for Fire and Rescue Authorities
no council tax referendum principles for mayoral combined authorities, mayoral county combined authorities or town and parish councils, but the government will review the decisions taken by these authorities when considering referendum principles in future years
18. The council tax referendum principles for Police and Crime Commissioners will be set out at the Police Funding Settlement.
19. Further detail on referendum principles will be set out at the provisional Settlement.
Household Energy Payment Support
20. Energy costs have risen around the world because of the conflict in Iran. While this is a global shock and not unique to the UK, the government knows that these price increases are worrying for families across the country. To help households with the rising cost of energy, the government will provide a £100 Household Energy Payment for households in council tax bands A to D for this year. This will be paid directly into bank accounts where possible from January, giving up to 20 million households in England some breathing space from rising energy prices.
21. The government is also providing £100 million for local authorities in England to provide additional support to households most acutely impacted by rising energy bills. Local authorities are best placed to identify and support vulnerable households in their area, which is why they have been given this flexibility.
Bringing Mayors into the Business Rates Retention system
22. As announced in the Rewiring the State Cabinet Statement, our intention is that local areas should be rewarded for taking action to grow their taxbase, and local leaders should be supported to deliver good growth in every postcode. Therefore, from 2027-28 Mayors will receive a share of business rates income instead of Investment Funds, Mayoral Capacity Funding and Local Growth Funds (where relevant).
23. This includes a share of the £150 million uplift to capacity and capability funding for Strategic Authorities announced at the Budget, intended to support local leaders to continue building the resource and expertise required to manage greater powers, and Good Growth Funds.
24. Mayors will also retain a 20% share of growth in their areas, which will include any additional rates built up since the start of the current reset period in 2026-27, subject to future resets. More grants will be exchanged for increased locally retained rates from 2028-29, with the objective of moving Mayors’ core business rates funding position closer to the value of the 20% share of regional rates. Mayoral strategic authorities’ (MSAs) core business rates funding position (baseline funding level) will increase annually in line with the Consumer Price Index, although where existing grant profiles exceed inflation we will still honour the already published allocations.
25. The new mayoral share of business rates will not affect constituent councils’ core Settlement funding, including the allocations set out through the multi-year Local Government Finance Settlement. Where areas already have arrangements in place, those arrangements will be taken into account and will not affect baseline funding levels.
26. To support the transition to their new funding arrangements, 100% of MSAs’ core business rates funding will be protected through a safety net in 2027-28. For 2028-29, the safety net will protect 97% of MSAs’ core business rates funding at a minimum. To support the delivery of changes to funding arrangements, MSAs will not be subject to a levy on growth for 2027-28 and 2028-29. The levy charged on local authorities in 2027-28 and 2028-29 will not be used to fund MSA safety net protection. From 2029-30, a levy will be applied to MSAs’ business rates growth. This will be designed to balance providing meaningful reward for business rates growth and funding protection.
Homelessness funding uplift
27. The announcement of a further £432 million investment in the Rough Sleeping Programme reflects the Prime Minister’s determination to make tackling rough sleeping a national priority. It extends support beyond March 2027 and through to the end of this Parliament, taking total investment behind his commitment to £874 million.
28. Mayoral strategic authorities’ and councils’ allocations of the uplift will be set out in due course. We expect revenue funding to be added to the existing Homelessness, Rough Sleeping and Domestic Abuse Grant within the Settlement, and included within Core Spending Power, for 2027-28 and 2028-29.
Reforming social care and children’s services
Adult social care
29. The Prime Minister has set out his vision for a National Care Service providing free personal care for older people, to be delivered in the next parliament. It will be person-centred, highly integrated with health services, and preventative, helping people lead as independent and fulfilling lives as possible, and providing dignity and peace of mind. We are laying the foundations for this reform now.
30. The upcoming Better Care Fund Policy Framework will set out arrangements for 2027-28, supporting more integrated health and care services and reduced bureaucracy for local systems. DHSC and MHCLG will continue to work with the NHS and local government on further Better Care Fund reform beyond 2027-28, to support adult social care reform and neighbourhood health. Local Authority Better Care Grant allocations for 2027-28 will be published at the Local Government Finance Settlement later this year. Authorities’ allocations of this grant in 2027-28 will be the same as their 2026-27 allocations. We are already laying the foundations, and at the time of the multi-year Settlement we made over £4.6 billion additional funding available for adult social care in 2028-29 compared with 2025-26. This also enables local authorities to voluntarily pool additional funding into the Better Care Fund where this supports local priorities.
31. We would like to see local authorities move towards neighbourhood-based approaches to commissioning homecare, helping to build the foundations of the future National Care Service by supporting sustainable local care markets. Neighbourhood approaches can support local care markets to deliver wider workforce reforms, including the Fair Pay Agreement and Employment Rights Act, by creating more sustainable market conditions and supporting improved employment practices. DHSC is working with commissioners and providers to understand what works in practice and will publish practical models and guidance to support local authorities in adopting these approaches, while retaining flexibility to respond to local circumstances.
National Minimum Allowance
32. The National Minimum Allowance for foster carers is routinely uplifted each year, taking into account changes in inflation and earnings forecasts produced by the Office for Budget Responsibility, and what is affordable for local government. At the multi-year Settlement, we confirmed that the National Minimum Allowance will increase by at least 2.5% each year, providing greater certainty for local authorities. The National Minimum Allowance uplift for 2027-28 will be 2.92%.
Families First Partnership programme
33. The government is delivering the biggest transformation of children’s social care in a generation with the Families First Partnership programme, backed by £2.4 billion over the multi-year Settlement. As set out at the multi-year Settlement, £853 million will be provided for the Families First Partnership in 2027-28 through the Children, Families and Youth Grant, distributed using the Children and Young People’s Services (CYPS) formula. This continued investment will support the shift from costly statutory intervention towards earlier, preventative help.
Dedicated Schools Grant deficits
34. The government recognises the continued and significant impact that Dedicated Schools Grant (DSG) deficits are having on local authority financial sustainability and we have taken action. Through the first phase of the SEND deficit support programme, the government is providing substantial support by resolving 90% of local authorities’ historic DSG deficits accrued to the end of 2025-26, representing support worth around £6 billion nationally and linked to the delivery of ambitious local SEND reform plans. With the vast majority of plans now approved, councils and their local partners are expected to drive forward reform and improve outcomes for children and young people with SEND. Local authorities with an approved plan will receive their High Needs Stability Grant payment in November 2026, with those approved following resubmission receiving payment by February 2027.
35. The government also recognises the significant and growing financial pressures on local authorities from deficits accruing in 2026-27 and 2027-28. The Statutory Override remains in place until the end of March 2028 and will continue to apply to deficits accruing in 2026-27 and 2027-28. The government has already committed to providing further proportionate, though not unlimited, support to assist local authorities with budget planning over these 2 years. Details of this will be set out before the end of the Statutory Override in March 2028. From 2028-29, SEND spending will be covered within the government’s DEL budget, so local authorities are not expected to fund future SEND costs from general funds.
Delivering further funding simplification
36. The government is committed to simplifying the local government funding landscape, giving local authorities greater certainty and flexibility over their funding and reducing unnecessary administrative burdens. A fragmented funding landscape, with multiple separate grants, conditions and reporting requirements, can make it harder for councils to plan effectively and focus resources on local priorities. Our approach is intended to support longer-term planning and a more strategic, outcomes-focused approach to funding.
37. Through the 2026-27 Settlement we simplified an unprecedented 38 funding streams, worth nearly £57 billion over the 3 years of the multi-year Settlement. This represented a significant step towards a simpler and more coherent funding landscape.
38. For 2027-28 we plan to build on the progress made through the multi-year Settlement by simplifying further funding streams, subject to the necessary consultation and final decisions.
39. Our plans for further funding simplification in 2027-28 include proposals to:
create a £602.7 million Local Transport Resource Grant consolidated grant, including the majority of the Department for Transport’s revenue transport grants for local authorities and strategic authorities, from 2027-28, with funding continuing into 2028-29
provide a £17.8 million uplift to the existing Holiday Activities and Food programme to support local authority capacity on school-age childcare during school term-time and holidays in the Children, Youth and Families consolidated grant across 2027-28 and 2028-29
roll 3 further funding streams into the Revenue Support Grant across 2027-28 and 2028-29: the LGF Data Review Final New Burdens Payment, around £50,000; the LGF Debt Costs Reporting New Burdens Payment, around £20,000; and Offensive Weapons Act 2019 funding for Trading Standards, around £3.14 million. This will reduce the number of separate grants and associated administration burdens
40. In addition, we propose distributing the £60 million uplift to the English National Concessionary Travel Scheme, announced August 2026, through the Local Government Finance Settlement. This funding extends the statutory entitlements for disabled passholders.
41. Further detail on the funding streams being simplified in 2027-28 will be set out in the Funding Simplification Explanatory Note published alongside the Settlement, including their value, how they will be consolidated, their distribution arrangements and whether they are included in Core Spending Power.
42. Alongside funding simplification, the government is continuing to develop the Local Outcomes Framework, supporting greater transparency around the outcomes local government delivers and a more proportionate approach to accountability. The framework will support a greater focus on outcomes, making better use of existing and publicly available evidence rather than creating unnecessary new reporting requirements.
Technical adjustment to the Business Rates Retention system
43. The government delivered the first reset of the Business Rates Retention system at the beginning of 2026-27. In line with our published approach, we are updating local authorities’ 2026-27 Business Rates Baselines (BRBs) through the 2027-28 Local Government Finance Settlement. This will ensure that BRBs are based on up-to-date data that was previously unavailable when the reset was initially delivered. To help local authorities understand what their new BRB positions will be, we have published a BRB update calculator.
44. To provide local authorities with certainty as distributional reforms are being implemented, the government committed to temporarily increasing the safety net threshold. In line with this, the safety net was set at 100% of local authorities’ baseline funding levels (BFLs) in 2026-27, with a view to it being reduced to 97% of BFLs in 2027-28. We are now confirming that the safety net threshold will be 97% of BFLs in 2027-28.
45. Where requested, we will proceed with business rates pooling for 2027 to 2028.
Internal drainage boards
46. The government recognises the impact of rising energy costs and climate change on internal drainage boards (IDBs), and the resulting increased costs on local authorities and farmers.
47. Therefore, we propose maintaining the £5 million IDB Special Levy Support Grant for 2027-28 to support local authorities who face additional pressures due to IDB special levies. As usual, the allocations of this funding will be announced after the final Local Government Finance Settlement, following the receipt of special levy data.
48. Defra and MHCLG jointly commissioned a research project on IDBs Funding and Costs, which has recently been completed. The research will be used to explore potential approaches that could support IDBs and their infrastructure in the future and we will continue to work with the sector.
Transition funding beyond 2028-29
49. Last year, we delivered the first multi-year Settlement in a decade, enabling local authorities to better plan their finances over 3 years and deliver services more efficiently. As a result of income protection arrangements some councils will remain above their fair funding shares in 2028-29 at the end of the multi-year Settlement.
50. Through the Fair Funding Review, the government has been clear that the future Settlements should be kept up to date rather than remaining frozen. The current Fair Funding Assessment uses updated data and evidence, and future updates to the system should continue to reflect the latest available data, alongside reviews of formula methodology and drivers where appropriate.
51. The Spending Review next year will provide the opportunity to determine arrangements for 2029-30 and beyond. The government recognises that, as part of this, some degree of future transitional protection is likely to be necessary to support local service delivery. However, there remains a balance to be struck between providing transitional arrangements that enable local authorities to respond to changes and moving decisively towards improved and up-to-date allocations.
52. The government will continue to work closely with local government and other government departments to understand the specific pressures facing the sector ahead of the next Spending Review and other fiscal events. The level of any future transitional protection will be consulted on in the normal way through future provisional settlements.
Exceptional Financial Support
53. The government recognises the continued challenging financial context for local authorities as we deliver the transition to the reformed system and manage the legacy of the previous flawed system. We also know that local authorities are continuing to face significant pressures in high-cost service areas such as Adult and Children’s Social Care. As set out above we are taking steps to address these pressures – but also recognise that in some cases they are leading well-run councils to request Exceptional Financial Support to enable them to set and maintain balanced budgets.
54. We recognise that tackling ongoing financial vulnerability in parts of the sector will take time and that some local authorities may continue to request support to manage pressures as they move towards financial sustainability. The government will therefore continue to have a framework through Exceptional Financial Support in place to support those in the most difficult positions. Any authority that has concerns about its ability to set or maintain a balanced budget should approach the department in the first instance, where we will treat all discussions in confidence.
55. The government has been clear on its commitment to break the cycle of Exceptional Financial Support. Alongside delivering much needed wider funding reforms to fix the foundations, as set out earlier this year we are committed to supporting local authorities differently and working in partnership with councils that receive Exceptional Financial Support to support local plans for their financial recovery.
56. This government’s position remains clear that any support provided should be time-limited and temporary, and local authorities should have clear plans to deliver the improvements and service transformation required to help them to return to financial stability.
57. Local authorities should only be seeking support where it is critical to their financial position and where they are taking all necessary and reasonable local steps to manage their finances. As part of the support process, the department will seek external assurance on local authorities’ financial positions. The government will also not hesitate to take action where there is any evidence of failure, including in the management of public money.
Council Tax flexibility
58. This government recognises that some local authorities in very challenging financial positions consider that additional council tax income would support their long-term financial sustainability. To balance our objectives of supporting local authorities and protecting taxpayers, the government will consider requests for flexibility where a local authority considers additional council tax increases as critical to managing financial risk over the medium-term.
59. As set out last year, the interests of taxpayers and our commitments to tackle cost of living pressures are central to government’s decision making and we will only consider requests from local authorities who have below average council tax levels for their authority type. Given the impact on taxpayers we are clear that councils should only be making requests where this is essential to managing their financial risk and they are taking all other reasonable steps locally to manage their financial position. We expect also any authority requesting an additional increase to ensure that appropriate support is put in place for vulnerable households, including reviewing the provision in their council tax reduction scheme and their discretionary discount policy for taxpayers in hardship.
60. Authorities who wish to request flexibility should approach MHCLG and the government will consider requests on a case-by-case basis. As part of mitigating the potential impact of sharp increases on taxpayers, the government will consider requests to spread additional increases over multiple years. All queries and requests from councils and Fire and Rescue Authorities in respect of Exceptional Financial Support or council tax flexibility should be directed to the Local Authority Financial Risk Team: lafinancialrisk@communities.gov.uk. All requests from police forces should be directed to the Home Office.
Next steps
61. We will publish further information on these proposals, including local authority allocations, at the upcoming provisional Settlement in December 2026. These proposals are subject to consultation, followed by debate and a vote in the House of Commons, in the usual way.
