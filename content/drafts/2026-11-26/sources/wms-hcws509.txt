I am pleased to announce reforms to the Disability Confident scheme, the Government's flagship employer initiative to promote disability inclusion in the workplace.
Since its launch in 2016, Disability Confident has supported thousands of employers across Great Britain to recruit, retain and develop disabled people and people with health conditions. The scheme has played an important role in encouraging employers to consider how they can remove barriers to employment and create more inclusive workplaces.
As the scheme has matured, employers, disabled people, and representative bodies have identified opportunities to strengthen its impact, consistency and credibility. Through extensive engagement, stakeholders have highlighted the importance of ensuring the scheme continues to drive meaningful action by employers while remaining accessible to organisations of all sizes.
The Government has therefore undertaken a comprehensive review of Disability Confident. Our review has been informed by broad engagement and testing, ensuring that both lived experience of disabled people and practical employer insight have shaped the reforms announced today.
The reforms will strengthen expectations of participating employers and create a clearer pathway for progression through the scheme. As previously announced, the Government will reduce Level 1 membership from three years to two years and remove the option to renew at Level 1. These changes will encourage employers to progress through the scheme and continue improving their disability inclusion practices.
To strengthen the scheme further, the Government will introduce stronger criteria across all levels of Disability Confident and enhanced verification arrangements at Level 3. These changes will provide greater assurance that employers are translating their commitments into meaningful action and outcomes.
The reformed scheme will introduce a clearer progression journey for employers between levels. This will be supported by a new interactive readiness tool, enhanced guidance and practical examples. Additional guidance will be provided for micro-businesses and small and medium-sized enterprises, helping organisations of all sizes identify the most appropriate route for strengthening their inclusive employment practice and progressing through the scheme.
The current "Offer of an Interview" commitment will be replaced with broader inclusive recruitment expectations. These will place greater emphasis on identifying and removing barriers throughout the recruitment process, helping employers focus on fair and inclusive recruitment outcomes.
Alongside this at level 3 in place of the Voluntary Reporting Framework, there will be a greater focus on employee voice and lived experience, recognising the important of understanding how workplace policies and practices are experienced by disabled employees themselves.
To increase opportunities for employers to learn from one another and share effective practice, Level 2 employers will be asked to create annual public statements setting out their progress, while summary findings from Level 3 verification activity will be published by DWP to improve transparency and promote learning across the scheme.
To ensure Disability Confident remains responsive and continues to evolve, new feedback mechanisms will be introduced. Refreshed stakeholder groups will also be established to help us shape the ongoing development of the scheme.
We will also publish an annual Disability Confident performance report. This report will provide greater transparency on overall scheme performance, participation levels, themes from verification findings, emerging good practice and opportunities for further improvement.
Existing members will be supported through a managed transition to the reformed scheme. Further information on implementation arrangements and timescales will be published alongside supporting guidance on GOV.UK.
A stronger Disability Confident scheme will help employers realise the benefits of a diverse workforce while supporting more disabled people and people with health conditions to enter, remain in and progress in work. These reforms are an important part of the Government's broader strategy to remove barriers to opportunity and improve employment outcomes. By encouraging employers to adopt more inclusive employment practices, the reformed scheme will support the ambitions of the Government's Keep Britain Working agenda and help ensure that disabled people can participate fully and thrive in the labour market. This will contribute to the Government's wider aim of creating a more inclusive, productive and prosperous economy for everyone.
