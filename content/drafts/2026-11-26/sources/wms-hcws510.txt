Local government is at the heart of this Government’s ambition to rewire the state, empowering local leaders to deliver good growth in every postcode. Local authorities provide the foundations on which successful devolution depends, delivering the services communities rely on and shaping their local economies. Strategic authorities can only be as strong as their constituent local authorities, making local authorities’ financial sustainability central to successful devolution.
Today, the Government has published a Policy Statement setting out its current intentions for the 2027-28 Local Government Finance Settlement for local authorities across England. It provides early indication on key aspects of local authorities’ funding ahead of the provisional Settlement in December, supporting authorities as they plan their budgets for next year.
Commitment to our Funding Reforms
Last year, through the Fair Funding Review 2.0 and the first multi-year Settlement in a decade, we took an important step towards fixing the foundations of local government and delivering a fairer and more sustainable funding system. Government consulted extensively on these reforms and we are grateful for the engagement from the sector throughout that process. As a result, we are now targeting money where it is needed most, meaning nine in ten councils will receive funding that broadly matches their assessed relative need by the end of the multi-year Settlement, up from around a third before our reforms.
We know that longer-term certainty is vital for local authorities to plan effectively. That is why we remain committed to the multi-year framework set out last year, and we do not intend to make substantial changes to its methodology and distribution. The government intends to maintain the core referendum principles set out and consulted on at the multi-year Settlement. Referendum principles are intended to strike a fair balance between protecting taxpayers and giving councils flexibility to make decisions locally. Further details will be set out at the provisional Settlement; the police precept will be set out at the police funding Settlement.
We were clear that fixing fundamental problems would take time and that despite these reforms, we know local government is still under immense pressure. This includes from the increasing cost of delivering services to vulnerable residents across areas like adult social care, children's services, SEND, and temporary accommodation. We are taking action by reforming high cost public services, but this will take time. We won’t shy away from the challenge as we take the next step in delivering greater long-term certainty to empower good growth in every postcode.
The 2027-28 Settlement
The 2027-28 Settlement builds on these multi-year foundations. It will make available over £80 billion in Core Spending Power across England and will continue with our commitment to better align funding with deprivation and need.
The Settlement will support the Government’s commitment to tackling rough sleeping. Following the Autumn Budget announcement of a further £432 million for the Rough Sleeping Programme, we expect revenue funding to be added to the existing Homelessness, Rough Sleeping and Domestic Abuse Grant consolidated grant within the Settlement.
Earlier this summer, the Prime Minister committed to take forward a programme of reform to support the financial sustainability of local authorities. As set out in the Fiscal Devolution Roadmap, a core part of this is the government’s commitment for local and mayoral strategic authorities to collectively retain all business rates that are generated locally by the end of the decade. This will be in place of central government grant, with local authorities retaining at least 70%. Moving towards a larger, long-term share of locally retained revenues will give councils greater flexibility and certainty over their resources and allow them to benefit from the growth they help create, supporting more effective long-term planning and a more sustainable funding system.
This Settlement takes a first crucial step in delivering measures set out in the Roadmap by confirming the incorporation of mayoral strategic authorities into the business rates retention system, whilst protecting the core funding position of constituent local authorities.
We are also building on the significant progress made on Funding Simplification through the multi-year Settlement, which simplified 38 funding streams worth almost £57 billion over three years. This year, we will go further, with additional funding simplification proposals set out in the Policy Statement to give local authorities greater flexibility over their resources and reduce administrative burdens.
Supporting the financial sustainability of local government
We know there is still more to do. There are no quick fixes to the financial pressures that have built up across local government, and it will take time to deliver the change this country needs. Our reforms are getting money to where it is needed most but we cannot undo a decade of damage overnight.
The Policy Statement therefore also confirms that there will continue to be a robust framework in place to consider requests for Exceptional Financial Support from authorities facing acute financial difficulty - alongside a clear ongoing expectation that councils are taking the right steps locally to minimise the need for that support.
The government has been clear on our aim to break the cycle of councils needing additional support to manage their financial pressures and we will continue to work closely with councils in the Exceptional Financial Support process to provide support and challenge to drive their financial recovery.
Proposals in the policy statement for the 2027-28 Settlement will be subject to the usual consultation process within the provisional Local Government Finance Settlement.
This Written Ministerial Statement covers England only. The policy statement will be deposited in the House libraries, and has been published on Gov.uk: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/local-government-finance-policy-statement-2027-to-2028.
