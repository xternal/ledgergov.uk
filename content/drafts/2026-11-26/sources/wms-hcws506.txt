Today I am informing the House of the award of the Gateway Agreement with Babcock to provide essential submarine maintenance and naval base support services that help sustain the United Kingdom’s Continuous at Sea Deterrence. The contract, awarded to Babcock International Group PLC subsidiary Devonport Royal Dockyard Limited, covers maintenance services for the Royal Navy’s nuclear submarine fleet and support services at His Majesty’s Naval Bases Devonport and Clyde as part of the Naval Support Integrated Global Network (NSIGN) Programme.
The contract represents a significant investment in skilled employment and the United Kingdom’s sovereign defence industrial capability. It will create 3,000 new British jobs – 1,600 in Devonport, 900 in Clyde and 500 in Bristol and other locations, and create around 2,500 apprenticeship and graduate opportunities, boosting growth and supporting government drive to get young people into work. It will also invest £2.4 billion across the wider UK supply chain, including £1 billion targeted at small and medium-sized enterprises. These opportunities will help sustain the specialist skills needed by our submarine enterprise while supporting businesses and communities across the United Kingdom.
Improving readiness and strengthening our ability to generate maritime forces at pace are essential so that the Royal Navy is ready to operate whenever and wherever it is needed, protecting the nation and maintaining our warfighting edge in an increasingly uncertain world. The Gateway Agreement complements the Government’s wider investment in naval infrastructure and sovereign defence industrial capability, including Project Royal Oak, supporting the readiness and resilience of the Royal Navy.
The Gateway Agreement will provide the commercial and operational framework through which the Ministry of Defence and Babcock will deliver in-service submarine support, improving submarine availability and directly supporting the First Sea Lord’s Submarine Maintenance Recovery Plan. By increasing maintenance capacity and capability, improving productivity and accelerating maintenance throughput, it will help strengthen the Royal Navy’s warfighting readiness and support our NATO commitments and wider global security responsibilities. This will help sustain Continuous at Sea Deterrence – the bedrock of our national security.
A strengthened performance framework will place greater emphasis on outputs, productivity and accountability. It is designed to improve maintenance delivery and value for money, with the Ministry of Defence holding Babcock to account for delivery against its contractual commitments. This approach will support the transformation needed to address historic maintenance backlogs, restore resilience and prepare for future requirements.
The Gateway Agreement and Project Royal Oak represent a once in a generation uplift in defence investment supporting the Royal Navy’s nuclear submarines and naval base and dockyard facilities. This investment will help us get submarines to sea faster, improve refit timelines and increase our warfighting readiness. It will also reinforce that an increasing defence budget, spent with British companies, is an engine for growth and is an example of reindustrialisation in action.
