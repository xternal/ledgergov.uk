School tours, workshops and £10 tickets to open up the magic of theatre to 100,000 young people
Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy joined stars of stage and screen to announce a new programme giving young people access to theatre and encourage careers in the creative industries
Nationwide, a new four-year partnership involving theatres from every English region, will combine world-class National Theatre productions and schools tours with new participation, enrichment and skills programmes
Children in underserved areas will be given access to play sport, explore the arts, enjoy outdoor activities and access safe, welcoming youth clubs beyond the school gates, backed by £688 million, as part of the Prime Minister’s plan to improve the experience of growing up across the country
Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy joined stars of stage and screen including Jim Carter, Adjoa Andoh, Adrian Lester, Imelda Staunton and Bella Maclean alongside young performers at the National Theatre to announce a government-backed programme that will open up opportunities for young people in theatre, sports, arts and the outdoors.
At the star studded launch event, the Culture Secretary announced up to £15 million from the Every Child Can programme will support the National Theatre to work with The National Lottery Community Fund and regional theatres across England. Together they will reach young people who may never have experienced theatre because of cost or where they live.
Through the new Nationwide programme, announced today, the National Theatre will work with 12 partners across every English region to bring world-class theatre to more than 100,000 young people. They will be able to explore acting, directing, playwriting and backstage roles, training and workshops will help them develop skills for careers in the creative industries, both on stage and behind the screen.
These enrichment activities will wrap around National Theatre touring productions in each location, helping young people to make the connection between what they see in themselves and what they see on stage. The partners will offer thousands of subsidised £10 tickets to make performances affordable for young people, and productions specifically designed for young audiences will tour to 400 schools across the country.
In addition, a new music theatre programme will create original community productions, inspired, devised, produced and performed by local young people in their own areas, while a Skills Roadshow will bring opportunities directly to young people in areas without theatres. Young people will also have a say in shaping the programme, with a new Nationwide Youth Council giving them a voice in how activities are designed, developed and delivered.
The government has also announced an additional £106 million from the Dormant Assets Scheme for the Every Child Can programme, bringing total funding to £238.5 million, to help more young people access activities beyond the school gates, from arts and culture to sport and nature.
The remaining £173 million will open to organisations across England in 2027, supporting more opportunities for young people to develop new skills, discover their interests and build real-life connections.

Today’s announcement forms part of the Prime Minister’s programme to improve the experience of young people growing up across the country. The government is rebuilding opportunities for young people, and working with local communities to help the next generation build successful careers from the start.
This investment will ensure they are given the opportunities to develop critical life skills and access training opportunities for adult life. It also provides young people with the offline opportunities they have told us they want to experience through the major ‘State of the Nation’ survey published as part of the National Youth Strategy, and ahead of the government taking the vital step in protecting them from the harms of social media, with the upcoming restrictions launching in 2027.
It comes ahead of Alan Milburn’s final report into youth unemployment due this month, commissioned by the Government to better understand the drivers behind the almost one million young people neither earning or learning.
On top of Every Child Can, the Government is investing in places, people and opportunities that will help young people thrive through its £450 million Youth Matters Fund programme. Driven by the priorities of young people, the Fund will support improvements to hundreds of youth facilities and provide meaningful, in-person activities and opportunities for young people, including sport, music and volunteering.
Funding will support 60 locations across England, selected because they are the most in need of investment. These areas have experienced longstanding underinvestment in youth provision, with young people reporting a lack of access to sufficient opportunities. The 60 local authorities will receive an initial share of £20.7 million in funding from the Youth Matters Fund programme this year. From Middlesbrough and Oldham to coastal communities such as Skegness and King’s Lynn, the funding will help address the imbalance in access to youth services and ensure a young person’s opportunities are not determined by where they live or their background.
The Government is also strengthening the youth services workforce, funding the delivery and recruitment of 1,000 new youth workers and trusted adults, and working with the sector and the National Lottery Community Fund to help youth workers develop their skills and impact.
The government’s National Youth Strategy, Youth Matters, was launched by Lisa Nandy in December 2025 and co-produced with 14,000 young people through a landmark ‘State of the Nation’ survey.

Notes to editors:
The Every Child Can programme is already helping to break down barriers to opportunity by funding activities and facilities that young people might otherwise struggle to access. The programme is providing £12.5 million to make sure every primary school has a library, where children can access books and discover the joy of reading. It is providing £12.5 million to turn libraries into creative spaces where young people can make music, record their own songs and get hands on with professional equipment for free and £10 million will help young people who have experienced care discover their creative talents and explore opportunities in the creative industries through one to one mentoring with creative professionals.
Ministers have asked The National Lottery Community Fund to work with the National Theatre to scrutinise and develop its plans. These will be subject to The National Lottery Community Fund’s formal solicitation process. Funding being granted and how the programme is delivered will be strictly subject to the outcome of that process.
The National Theatre 12 regional partners involved in the scheme are the Lowry in Salford, Newcastle Theatre Royal, Leeds Heritage Theatres, Sheffield Theatres, Nottingham Theatre Royal, the Curve in Leicester, Birmingham Hippodrome, the Belgrade in Coventry, Norwich Theatre, Theatre Royal Plymouth, Mayflower Theatre in Southampton and Marlowe Theatre in Canterbury.
The Every Child Can Fund will be designed by The National Lottery Community Fund in partnership with the youth and enrichment sector and young people themselves. It will directly fund opportunities across the five enrichment categories - civic engagement, sports, arts & culture, nature, outdoor & adventure and wider life & future skills - across the whole of England. The Fund will launch in Spring 2027.
In addition to the funding for young people announced today, further Dormant Assets funding for programmes around social investment and financial inclusion will be announced shortly.
The £450 million Youth Matters Fund programme includes funding for the construction and renovation of youth facilities and the purchase of capital equipment, and funding for youth work and enriching activities, to support the ambition to halve the participation gap in enriching activities between disadvantaged young people and their peers, as well as funding for central programme management and support.
60 local authorities are receiving funding this year as the first phase of the £450 million Youth Matters Fund programme. The wider Youth Matters Fund will open for applications in early 2027 to local authorities and organisations from the same 60 areas. It will be delivered by Social Investment Business, in partnership with UK Youth, National Youth Agency, Young People’s Foundation Trust, UK Community Foundations and Sharpe Pritchard. Further details will be announced in due course.
The 60 areas were chosen with a formula based on levels of deprivation and low access to social and community infrastructure; how satisfied young people are with local activities and services; and the proportion of 16-17 year olds who are not in education, employment or training.

About the Dormant Assets Scheme
The UK Dormant Assets Scheme redirects money from dormant bank accounts, pensions, insurance policies and other financial products to good causes across the UK. Funds are released only after financial institutions have made every effort to reunite assets with their owners, who retain the right to reclaim their money at any time.
Since its launch, the Scheme has unlocked more than £1.6 billion to support social and environmental initiatives, helping to create opportunities for young people, strengthen financial inclusion, grow social investment and empower communities to thrive.
In England, funding from the Dormant Assets Scheme is distributed by The National Lottery Community Fund. As the UK’s largest community funder, the Fund works with communities, charities, social enterprises and public sector partners to ensure dormant assets are invested in ways that create lasting social impact and deliver against Government priorities.
