Today, the government has published its one-year-on report of England’s Men’s Health Strategy, setting out progress and our next phase of action to improve the health and wellbeing of men and boys in England.
One year ago, we published England’s first-ever Men’s Health Strategy, with three broad aims: ensuring health services engage men and boys and respond to their needs; empowering men and boys to maximise their own health and wellbeing; and creating the conditions in which men and boys can thrive.
The positive response has been fantastic.
Over the past year, we have worked across government and with the NHS, local authorities, employers, academia, industry and the voluntary sector to turn its ambitions into action.
Highlights include launch of the £6.3 million Men’s Health Community Fund, delivered in partnership with Movember and People’s Health Trust, supporting community-led initiatives, particularly for men underserved by traditional services. The Premier League's Together Against Suicide initiative with Samaritans has expanded to provide bespoke environments to engage fans on home matchdays at all 20 Premier League clubs this season, supported by a partnership with the government.
Despite this progress, too many men experience preventable ill health and die too young, with particularly stark inequalities for men living in deprived communities. Men in the most deprived areas of England can expect to die over 10 years earlier and experience more than 19 fewer years of healthy life than men in the least deprived areas. Men also accounted for three-quarters of deaths by suicide in 2024.
That is why the one-year-on report identifies where we’ll go further, including embedding men’s health across the life course and better understanding the impact of digital environments and societal norms.
Success requires sustained action from national and local government, the NHS, employers, voluntary and community organisations and others.
That is why we have also published new voluntary, non-statutory guidance to help local authorities, integrated care boards, NHS organisations and voluntary and community organisations embed men’s health within existing work to improve health and tackle inequalities.
The guidance invites local leaders to consider how they can champion men’s health, establish senior ownership, use local data and insight to understand inequalities, engage men and their communities, and focus action where it can make the greatest difference.
We will publish a measurement framework in 2027 to monitor progress and identify where further action is needed and continue to strengthen the evidence base for men’s health.
Alongside the report, I am delighted to announce that we will shortly open recruitment for a Men’s Health Ambassador. The Ambassador will provide visible national leadership, galvanise action across sectors and help drive better outcomes for men and boys.
The Men’s Health Strategy represents a long-term commitment to improving the health and wellbeing of all men and boys. Through stronger national leadership, greater support for local delivery, sustained partnership and clearer accountability, we will continue building on this progress so that men and boys can live longer, healthier lives.
