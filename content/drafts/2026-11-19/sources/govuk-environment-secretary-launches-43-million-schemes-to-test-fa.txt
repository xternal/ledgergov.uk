Environment Secretary launches £43 million schemes to test farming tech, boost climate resilience and build reservoirs
Farmers will get £43 million to help them slash costs, protect food production and deal with the impacts of drought, Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle will announce today (Thursday 19 November).
The Environment Secretary will launch packages of support to fix some of the most difficult issues facing rural communities, after a summer of dry weather and wildfire left many growers struggling.
The measures include opening up to £15 million in grants for water storage projects and a further £28 million for innovation in projects focused on improving soil health, water quality and environmental management, aimed at helping businesses become more resilient and profitable.
Speaking at the CLA Rural Business Conference at the QEII Centre in Westminster, Dame Angela is expected to pledge that ministers will stand by rural Britain rather than leave farmers to shoulder the burden of extreme weather, rising costs and global uncertainty.
She is expected to say:
Our goal is rural communities that have power over their future, public services they can rely on, the tools they need to adapt to our changing world, and the support and clarity to be able to invest, innovate and grow their businesses.
Working together, we can ensure the 9.7 million people who call rural England home benefit from the prosperity they help create, protect our precious natural environment for future generations, and unlock growth in every rural postcode.
Commenting on the opening of the Water Management Grant, CLA President Gavin Lane said:
We welcome this fund and the focused work Defra is doing to address the wider barriers to constructing farm reservoirs, including planning requirements and abstraction licensing.
Greater water security is crucial to food resilience, environmental protection and rural economic growth.
This fund is an important first step, but the scale of the challenge should not be underestimated. It must be part of an ambitious, longer-term government commitment to supporting investment in on-farm water storage and the construction of farm reservoirs.
Applications for the Water Management Grant, announced by the Prime Minister in the summer, will open on November 30, helping farmers invest in reservoirs and storage to secure supplies during dry periods, with the latest Farming Innovation Programme competition aiming to protect soil health and improve water quality.
The application window for the Water Management Grant will remain open for 12 weeks to 23 February 2027 and will not operate on a first-come, first-served basis, meaning an application submitted on the closing day has the same chance of receiving funding as if it were submitted on the opening day.
In a move welcomed by the industry, growers will be able to apply before planning and licensing permissions are fully in place, cutting red tape and speeding up access to funding.
At the same time, applications will open for the biggest ever Farming Innovation Programme competition, worth up to £28 million.
The cash will back practical projects focused on improving soil health, water quality and environmental management to support long term resilience. Previous funding rounds have supported companies such as ElentecBioAg in developing technology to treat dairy cow slurry, turning it into fertiliser nutrients that farmers can use on their own farms or sell on.
Ministers have also launched a new Fertiliser Sustainability and Resilience Plan, inviting experts to engage in its development, aimed at boosting UK production and shielding farmers from volatile global markets and supply chain shocks. The announcement comes as farmers face soaring costs: data shows prices of some fertilisers in the UK rose by 30% in just two months this year (February to April 2026).
The Environment Secretary is also expected to promise changes to the Sustainable Farming Incentive scheme, moving away from the current first-come, first-served approach.
A new trial is also beginning from November, allowing farmers on common land such as Dartmoor, who were previously not eligible, to join Countryside Stewardship Higher Tier schemes.
