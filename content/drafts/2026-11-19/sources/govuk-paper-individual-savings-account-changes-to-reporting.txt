Individual Savings Account — changes to reporting
Who is likely to be affected
Financial institutions who provide or manage ISA accounts, and individuals investing in ISA accounts.
General description of the measure
This measure introduces monthly reporting requirements for ISA managers. The measure requires ISA managers to report prescribed subscription events to HMRC on a monthly basis, provide additional information relating to ISA investors and accounts, and report transfers. A subscription event is any action that changes an investor’s current-year ISA subscription position, such as paying money into an ISA or making another adjustment that affects how much of their annual ISA allowance has been used.
It also requires a National Insurance number to be obtained before subscriptions can be accepted to adult ISAs and introduces timescales for correcting oversubscriptions outside relevant ISA limits. These changes will allow HMRC to identify and resolve annual ISA subscription limit breaches closer to the point at which they occur, improving compliance with ISA rules and reducing administrative burdens associated with retrospective interventions by HMRC.
This measure also maintains the annual cash ISA subscription limit at £20,000 for individuals under 65 from 6 April 2027.
Policy objective
This measure supports the government’s objective of modernising the administration of ISAs through digital reporting. It will improve the accuracy and timeliness of information available to HMRC and ISA managers, enabling subscription breaches to be identified and resolved sooner, helping to maintain a simple, efficient and trusted tax-advantaged savings regime.
Background to the measure
At autumn statement 2023, the government announced a package of ISA simplification reforms. To support these reforms, HMRC began developing a new digital reporting regime to modernise ISA reporting and compliance.
During 2024, HMRC engaged extensively with ISA managers and representative bodies to inform the design of the regime. Following this engagement, the government announced at Autumn Budget 2024 that it would proceed with the reforms and legislate for the required changes. HMRC continues to work closely with industry stakeholders on implementation.
These regulations introduce the reporting framework required to support the digital reporting programme from April 2028.
At Budget 2025, the government announced that, from 6 April 2027, the annual cash ISA subscription limit would reduce from £20,000 to £12,000 for individuals under 65, while the overall ISA allowance would remain £20,000. Savers reaching 65 would continue to be able to subscribe the full £20,000 to cash ISAs.
At Budget 2026, it was announced that the reduction in the cash ISA limit would not be implemented and that the legislation would be repealed.
Detailed proposal
Operative date
This measure will have effect from 6 April 2028. The revocation of The Individual Savings Account (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2026 (2026/1018) will have effect from 6 April 2027.
Current law
The ISA rules are contained in the Individual Savings Account Regulations 1998 (SI 1998/1870) (“the ISA Regulations”) which are made under powers in Chapter 3 of Part 6 of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005 and section 151 of the Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992.
The ISA regulations currently require ISA managers to provide HMRC with annual returns of information relating to ISA accounts.
The ISA Regulations also provide the annual subscription limits. The existing legislation provides that no more than £20,000 can be subscribed annually to ISAs. Amending legislation (effective 6 April 2027) has been made which would have provided that no more than £12,000 could be subscribed annually to cash ISAs where the individual was below 65.
Proposed revisions
The ISA Regulations will be amended to:
introduce a new requirement for ISA managers to submit monthly reports of subscription events to HMRC
prescribe the information to be included in monthly subscription event reports and annual returns
require ISA managers to report transfers between account managers
require ISA managers to obtain specified National Insurance number or confirmation that the investor does not hold one, before accepting subscriptions to adult ISAs
introduce deadlines for compliance with HMRC directions to correct subscription errors
define new reporting periods and associated reporting obligations
revoke the legislation reducing the annual subscription limit for cash ISAs

Economic impact

Impact on individuals, households and families
This measure will impact individual investors who wish to make payments into their ISA (known as a subscription). Under the new digital reporting framework, HMRC will identify subscriptions that exceed the £20,000 annual ISA limit more quickly through monthly reporting, rather than after the end of the tax year through annual reporting. As a result, affected investors will be required to correct excess subscriptions as advised by their ISA managers at an earlier stage.
Investors will also be required to provide to their ISA manager their National Insurance number or confirmation that they do not hold one.
This measure is not expected to impact on family formation, stability, or breakdown.
This measure will not impact individuals’ experience of dealing with HMRC as the changes do not impact how they interact with HMRC.
Equalities impact
The amendment will require ISA managers to obtain an investor’s National Insurance number or confirmation that they do not hold one, before accepting subscriptions to an ISA. This measure will apply to individuals who invest in an ISA and those who are eligible to invest in an ISA account regardless of their protected characteristics.
As HMRC does not hold data on the protected characteristics of all individuals who may be affected by this measure, the assessment is based on available information about ISA holders. Where particular groups are overrepresented among ISA holders, they may also be overrepresented among those affected by the measure. However, this analysis is subject to uncertainty.
Individuals aged over 55 are estimated to be overrepresented in the ISA holder population 57% compared to their prevalence in the UK adult population 45%. In addition, people from a White English, Welsh, Scottish, Northern Irish, or British ethnic background are estimated to be overrepresented 89% in the ISA holders population compared to their prevalence in the UK adult population 82%.
Where data is available no other protected groups were estimated to be overrepresented in the population of ISA holders.
Almost all individuals over the age of 16 and in work have a National Insurance number. Individuals who do not have a National Insurance number but are eligible to invest in an ISA account will be expected to apply for one and provide it to their ISA manager. Individuals not eligible for a National Insurance number must confirm their non eligibility to their ISA manager.
Administrative impact on business including civil society organisations
This measure is expected to have a significant impact on approximately 500 ISA managers. The measure will require ISA managers to provide additional information to HMRC and to submit returns more frequently and digitally under the digital reporting reforms.
ISA managers will need to be aware of the change and obtain additional information from customers, which will include obtaining National Insurance numbers for people who already hold ISAs but have not previously provided one.
ISA managers will be required to report subscription events to HMRC on a monthly basis, provide additional information relating to ISA investors, and submit annual returns in a revised digital format.
One-off costs are estimated to be £20 million across affected businesses. These costs are expected to arise primarily from familiarisation with the new requirements and changes to systems, processes and reporting arrangements needed to comply with the new obligations.
Ongoing administrative costs are estimated to be £1.7 million per year across affected businesses. These net costs arise from the ongoing collection, maintenance and submission of information to HMRC under the new reporting framework, with some savings expected from automation and digitisation of working systems.
The scale of impact will vary depending on the size and complexity of the ISA manager and the extent to which existing systems and processes require modification. The measure is expected to improve the quality and timeliness of information provided to HMRC, supporting the effective operation of the ISA regime and reducing the need for corrective action arising from reporting errors.
The revocation of the legislation reducing the annual subscription limit for cash ISAs may impact ISA managers who began drafting communications and making systems changes in preparation for the change.
The measure is not expected to impact civil society organisations.
This measure is expected overall to impact businesses’ experience of dealing with HMRC since the process change will require them to submit more information and in a digital format to HMRC. HMRC will provide detailed guidance to support the changes.
Operational impact (£ million) (HMRC or other)
Implementing this amendment carries no additional operational cost to HMRC and forms part of the wider digital ISA reporting programme.
Other impacts
Other impacts have been considered and none have been identified.
Monitoring and evaluation
The measure will be monitored through information provided by ISA managers, existing ISA reporting returns and ongoing stakeholder engagement.
