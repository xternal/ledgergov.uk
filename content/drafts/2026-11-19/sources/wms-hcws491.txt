Today I can confirm the publication of the provisional allocations for mainstream schools and local authorities through the schools and central school services national funding formulae (NFFs) as well as the high needs funding allocations for 2027-28. Our consultation response on upfront SEND funding for mainstream schools has also been published.
Overall, the core schools budget in 2027-28 will total £66.7 billion. To simplify the funding system, allocations for mainstream schools, high needs and central school services will incorporate the additional funding provided in 2026-27 through the schools budget support grant 2026 (SBSG 26).
As announced in July, these allocations will also incorporate a funding adjustment to reflect the reduction in employer contribution rates, for the teachers’ pension scheme (TPS) from April 2027. The reduction in pension contributions does not lead to a change in the value of teacher pensions or to a saving for the government. Funding for schools will be reduced to reflect the decreased cost incurred by schools at national level and thus be cost neutral for public sector employers as a whole. After excluding the impact of TPS changes, the core schools budget will continue to increase by more than real-terms per-pupil between 2026-27 and 2027-28, based on the GDP deflator measure of inflation.
For mainstream schools, factor value increases have been applied to all NFF factors after adjusting for the SBSG 26 and the TPS employer contributions funding changes. Most core factor values will increase by 3.0%. The lump sum and sparsity factor values will increase by 5%, providing additional support for small and remote schools in light of falling pupil numbers. The FSM6 deprivation factor will also increase by 5%, directing more funding towards schools serving disadvantaged pupils. The schools NFF will continue to provide stability to schools through a 1% increase to the funding floor, which protects schools from year-on-year decreases in funding. The minimum funding levels will increase by 1.5%.
Local authorities will continue to determine individual schools’ and academies’ funding through their local formulae, so the actual funding schools receive may differ from those published today. To support a more consistent funding system, authorities not already mirroring the NFF will continue to be required to move their formulae closer to it.
Total funding for high needs will amount to £12.4 billion in 2027-28, and of that £12.1 billion is going to local authorities through the provisional allocations published today. High needs funding is increasing by £460 million from 2026-27 to 2027-28, after accounting for the TPS employer contributions funding adjustment. £245 million of this underlying increase is being allocated per-head of the local 2-18 year old population, which will particularly benefit some low-funded local authorities. Other elements of the underlying increase will support additional places and pupils in special schools, alternative provision and specialist post-16 settings.
Updated allocations of schools, high needs and central school services funding for 2027-28 will be published through the Dedicated Schools Grant in December, taking account of the latest pupil data.
Alongside the allocations, we have published our response to the consultation ‘Upfront funding for mainstream schools: creating a ‘local SEND inclusion formula’. The majority of respondents supported giving schools more flexibility to plan resources around pupils’ needs. Upfront funding means mainstream schools receive a share of high needs funding that would otherwise be distributed through top-up funding, helping them to plan early, flexible and responsive support for their pupils with special educational needs (SEN). By taking a test and learn approach, we can build evidence in partnership with local authorities who see this as a natural next step in developing their SEND system. Together, we can understand the experience of schools, how to manage risks and share effective practice ahead of wider funding reform.
