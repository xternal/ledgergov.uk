New Men’s Health Ambassador to help improve men’s health
Search begins for England’s first Men’s Health Ambassador on International Men’s Day
New guidance to help local leaders provide more support for men’s physical and mental health at home, work and in the community
Suicide prevention grants for areas where men are most at risk open for applications soon
Men across England will have a national champion for their physical and mental health. The government today, on International Men’s Day, has launched the search for England’s first Men’s Health Ambassador, to help men live longer, healthier and happier lives.
The ambassador will be a national voice for men’s physical and mental health, championing services that make it easier for men to get help close to home, at work and in their communities.
Alongside this, new guidance published today helps local leaders fit services around men’s lives, for example health appointments that work around shifts, family or caring duties.
The need for more action on men’s health remains. In 2025 35% of all deaths among men in England were before the age of 75. Men in England’s most deprived areas can expect to die more than 10 years earlier, with more than 19 fewer healthy years, than those in the least deprived areas.
Health Minister James Frith said:
Men’s health is suffering and further action is needed to improve the physical health and mental wellbeing of those who may not always seek the help they need.
That is why today - on International Men’s Day - I can confirm plans to appoint a new Men’s Health Ambassador who will help men live healthier and happier lives.
Minister for Men and Boys Jake Richards said:
For too long, many men have suffered in silence. Whether it’s poor mental health, loneliness, or a lack of support when they need it most, the consequences aren’t just felt by men themselves but by their families and communities.
As the country’s first Minister for Men and Boys, I’m determined to drive work across government so men’s health gets the attention it needs. That’s why we’re launching the search for a Men’s Health Ambassador today, and I look forward to working with them as we take this work forward. Because improving the lives of men and boys will make our country stronger.
This goes hand in hand with our work to support women and girls. I’m unequivocal that better relationships, stronger families and safer communities depend on giving boys and young men the support, role models and opportunities they need to thrive.
Around 3 in 4 deaths by suicide are among men, and suicide remains one of the leading causes of death among men under 50, which is why we are taking action on suicide prevention.
We will shortly be welcoming applications for grants as part of our Suicide Prevention Pathfinders programme, which will provide up to £3.6 million to strengthen the connections between support services in areas where middle-aged men face the highest risk.
The Department of Health and Social Care is partnered with the Premier League on their Together Against Suicide initiative with Samaritans.
This season the programme expanded to all 20 Premier League Clubs with more than 3,000 league and club staff receiving suicide prevention training and millions of fans witnessing the alerts and support advertised during 2 weeks in September.
Other suicide prevention initiatives include the Staying Safe from Suicide training, which 25,000 health practitioners had passed by September.
Today also marks one year since the launch of the Men’s Health Strategy, leading to a number of improvements including:
the launch of the Men’s Health Community Fund - in partnership with Movember and People’s Health Trust - which is designed to test new ways to reach men too often missed by traditional services backed by up to £6.3 million
a 3-year £93.3 million gambling-harm prevention programme. Almost £40 million in grants over 2 years has been allocated to support 40 voluntary organisations, which have received their first payments and started work
support for dads with guidance provided to all 153 local authorities on how to develop father-inclusive practices, particularly as part of the Healthy Babies services
the development of the HomeTest programme so men already on prostate cancer active monitoring pathways can order and complete prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood tests at home
NHS England, working with mining charities, community hubs and other trusted local groups have helped locate patients across 6 former coalfield communities with 1,322 referrals resulting in 267 confirmed chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and 143 confirmed asthma diagnoses being made sooner
in 2025 to 2026, the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), the research arm of DHSC, committed £18.3 million directly for new research projects into men’s health. This supports studies across a range of priorities including cancer, mental health and better support for fathers and men missed by existing services. A further £8.5 million was invested through NIHR infrastructure to support the delivery of active men’s health research projects across England
Julie Bentley, Chief Executive of Samaritans, said:
Men have been at the highest risk of suicide for decades, and tackling this must be a national priority. We’re pleased to be working with government to drive change and hope suicide prevention will be high on the agenda of the future ambassador. There’s a misconception that men simply need to talk, but men tell us that when they do open up, they can be met with stigma, shame and feel unheard. It’s not enough to encourage men to speak out - we must also make sure they’re listened to and receive the support they need. Initiatives like Together Against Suicide, which reaches men where they are, alongside funding for support services in communities at greatest risk, are vital steps towards preventing suicide and saving lives. Every suicide is one too many.
People’s Health Trust’s Chief Executive, John Hume, said:
Too many men are missing out on the support they need to live healthy lives, particularly in communities facing the greatest challenges. We welcome the government’s commitment to improve men’s health through the appointment of a Men’s Health Ambassador and a stronger focus on reaching men where they are.
People’s Health Trust is proud to be a partner in the Men’s Health Community Fund, with the government and Movember, investing in community-led projects that help men get support before problems reach crisis point. Key to this work is supporting men in industries like construction, who experience higher rates of stress, anxiety and suicide, and connecting them with trusted local support when they need it.
Greg Fell OBE, President of the Association of Directors of Public Health and Director of Public Health for Sheffield City Council, said:
Improving men’s health and wellbeing must start with ensuring the right support is available at every stage of their lives. That means schools, the NHS, housing associations, voluntary and community organisations, and other partners all working together with the boys and men in our communities to make sure that support is easier to access and genuinely responsive to their needs.
The government’s commitment to taking this approach and recognising the important role that local ambassadors play in connecting men to trusted early support is incredibly welcome.
Directors of Public Health are already working hard with local partners to improve and protect men’s health and wellbeing, and are ready to help the government build on these relationships to ensure today’s strategy can make a difference for generations to come.
