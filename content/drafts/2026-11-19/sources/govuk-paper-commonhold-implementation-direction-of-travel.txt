Commonhold implementation: Direction of Travel
Introduction
The establishment of commonhold as the new default tenure for flats will be one of the most transformational reforms to homeownership in England and Wales in a generation. It will drive forward the government’s long-term commitment to abolishing the leasehold system by providing a modern, fit-for-purpose alternative which gives homeowners genuine and lasting control over their homes.
Commonhold enables individuals to own their homes outright on a freehold basis while collectively managing and maintaining the shared areas through a Commonhold Association. It is specifically designed to manage homes without a landlord, and provides greater transparency around how buildings are looked after and the associated costs. It also ensures that those who pay for the upkeep of a building have a meaningful say in how it is run. Making commonhold the default tenure for flatted developments will put fairness and autonomy at the heart of residential ownership.
Whilst the scale of this change is significant, the principle behind it is not new. From condominium ownership in North America to strata title in Australia and New Zealand, forms of collective ownership are an embedded and successful part of housing markets across the world. We will build on these international examples and establish a commonhold system in England and Wales that is viable and effective at scale. Not just an alternative to leasehold, but a radical improvement on it.
These changes reflect the direction of the market. Ground rents have been prohibited in new homes since 2022, and many new developments have subsequently been provided without a landlord. The reformed commonhold model will now provide homeowners with the full range of tools to manage homes effectively, with flexibility to meet the changing needs of buildings and their owners, while ensuring all homeowners have the opportunity for a say on the key decisions affecting their homes.
The Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill will provide the legal framework needed to realise the government’s ambitions to reinvigorate commonhold ahead of a future ban on the sale of new leasehold flats. The Bill will update and strengthen the existing commonhold model so that it can function in a much wider range of settings. It will achieve this by introducing measures such as commonhold ‘sections’ and separate heads of cost, and permitting certain leases within a commonhold (such as shared ownership housing). New flexibilities and safeguards for commonhold owners will make the model easier to use. The Bill will also make it more accessible and straightforward for existing leaseholders to convert their buildings to commonhold should they wish- both for the many occupants of buildings that have already enfranchised (or do not have a landlord from the outset), and those yet to do so, who will be able to enfranchise and convert to commonhold at the same time.
We know that legislation alone is not enough. Alongside the introduction of the Bill, the government has committed to a comprehensive programme of implementation to enable a smooth transition to commonhold. This means working closely with consumers and across industry, including with developers, lenders, conveyancers, property agents, and social housing providers as well as other property professionals and associated services, to ensure readiness for greater use of commonhold.
This Direction of Travel document has been prepared to provide an early indication of the government’s approach and outline the key areas of work that will support commonhold implementation. We are publishing it now so that consumers, industry and other stakeholders can begin preparing for the changes ahead. More detailed implementation plans, guidance, timetables and supporting measures will follow as legislation progresses and reform moves towards delivery. We will publish a full commonhold Implementation Roadmap closer to Royal Assent of the Bill.
What has the government already done, and what do we intend to do?
We made a clear manifesto commitment to make commonhold the default tenure for flats. Last year, we responded to the Law Commission’s ‘Reinvigorating commonhold: the alternative to leasehold ownership’ report through the publication of the Commonhold White Paper and in January 2026 published the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill in draft for pre-legislative scrutiny.
We have now introduced the Commonhold and Leasehold Reform Bill to Parliament, and have published a comprehensive Guide to the Bill. We have also responded to the Housing, Communities and Local Government Select Committees pre-legislative scrutiny of the draft Bill, and both issued and responded to the Moving to Commonhold consultation.
We are working with the Leasehold Advisory Service (LEASE) to scope and produce comprehensive consumer guidance on the new commonhold model. We will also provide detailed information, advice and support for current and prospective commonhold owners, directors, and buyers and sellers. This will include a dedicated ‘How to Commonhold’ guide, which we intend to publish in the new year.
We are working with representatives from the conveyancing sector including the Conveyancing Association to develop new and bespoke commonhold sales information forms including a new Commonhold Property Enquiry (CPE1) Form, to support a smooth home buying and selling process for commonhold flats.
We are working closely with delivery partners to support their preparations for the widespread adoption of commonhold. This includes work with HM Land Registry, who are currently undertaking service design work to make new commonhold registrations ‘digital by default’ and to support a better, more efficient customer experience. We are also working with Companies House to support registrations of commonhold associations and HM Courts and Tribunals Service on supporting the tribunals service to develop necessary guidance for stakeholders, and to prepare their systems and processes for greater uptake of commonhold.
We have asked the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS) to develop a statutory Code of Practice on commonhold for Secretary of State approval, implementing a key recommendation of the Law Commission on the allocation of proportionate financial contributions in residential, mixed-use and purely commercial commonholds. This will ensure consistency, fairness and transparency around cost-setting in the new commonhold regime.
We will consider how government can support the development of sector-led training, advice and information for industry practitioners across the housing market. We welcome initiatives that have already been rolled out, such as new training modules on commonhold for managing agents developed by The Property Institute.
We will publish a detailed Implementation Roadmap closer to the Bill’s Royal Assent which will provide a clear timetable for the implementation of measures in the Bill and plans for a programme of secondary legislation, including the revised Commonhold Community Statement and Articles of Association, so that consumers and industry know when the full detail of the new commonhold model can be expected.
Development of the Implementation Roadmap will be informed and supported by the establishment of a new Commonhold Implementation Steering Group, comprised of consumer and industry representatives and chaired by the Housing Minister.

What will this mean for you?
For homeowners and tenants living in commonhold, it is our intention that:
you are supported to understand your rights and obligations, participate in your Commonhold Association, and exercise your voting rights on matters that affect you and your home. This includes, for example, setting and approving commonhold budgets, or appointing a managing agent to help with the management or governance of the building.
you can access dispute resolution where things go wrong. Should matters escalate, you will have clear guidance enabling you to understand how to access the tribunal and make use of the minority protections and new enforcement rules available to unit-holders.
you have access to guidance on the duties and responsibilities associated with serving as a Commonhold Association director, should you choose to do so. You will also have clear information on how to hold directors to account, including appointing a director if a vacancy cannot be filled or replacing a director failing in their duties to effectively maintain, repair, and insure the commonhold.
you are supported by clear and consistent processes when buying or selling a commonhold property, with information for prospective buyers provided in a timely and transparent way through standardised forms
For consumers, who are interested in exploring commonhold ownership, it is our intention that:
you are able to access clear information about key aspects of owning and living in a commonhold property, including the role of the Commonhold Association and the opportunities to participate in its management and governance
you are provided with timely and standardised sales information material when buying a commonhold property, and are supported through your purchasing journey by property professionals who understand the commonhold model
if you are currently a leasehold flat owner, you have access to the information needed to understand the new conversion process and, together with fellow residents, can make an informed decision about whether conversion to commonhold is appropriate for you and your building
For developers, it is our intention that:
you have the information needed to plan for and deliver commonhold developments, including on how sections can be used to manage or delegate decision-making in large, complex, or mixed-use sites. You will also have access to clear information about the operation and governance of commonhold developments to provide to prospective purchasers.
you can access information about the requirements for establishing Commonhold Associations and transferring responsibilities to unit-holders, as well as new development rights and consumer protections available to support phased development, site completion, and ongoing marketing and sales activity
you have the information needed to provide new commonhold homes when the new commonhold model is available for use. You will also have a transitional period to familiarise yourself with the new commonhold regime and adapt business models, contractual arrangements, sales processes, and financing structures in advance of the ban on the use of leasehold coming into force.
you can use transitional arrangements to safeguard the delivery of new homes where it is not possible to immediately switch your projects to commonhold. Where a qualifying planning application has been submitted, or planning permission granted, before ban commences, you will be able to continue to sell leasehold flats on these sites after the ban has commenced if you wish to do so. This is intended to avoid disruption or delay to the development pipeline.
For lenders, it is our intention that:
you will benefit from measures in the new commonhold regime that will protect a lender’s stake in the building (such as lenders’ ability to apply to the tribunal for the appointment of professional directors where no unit owners are willing to serve, or to replace a director failing in their duties)
you can be assured of new rules designed to protect the solvency of the Commonhold Association, including: compulsory public liability insurance and reserve funds, keeping bills affordable through unit-holders’ approval of annual budgets, new mechanisms for Commonhold Associations to enforce financial breaches or raise emergency funding, and new rules around voluntary termination
you have information necessary for lending policies, risk models, documentation and operational processes for commonhold properties, and supporting guidance on the new commonhold regime regarding governance, financial, and management arrangements that underpin commonhold developments and how these may affect lending decisions
you are able to assess commonhold properties using timely, consistent and reliable information about the Commonhold Association, building finances and long-term maintenance obligations, and have access to standardised information and transaction processes that support efficient mortgage underwriting and conveyancing
For insurers, it is our intention that:
you have clear and timely information on the operation of the commonhold framework and its implications for underwriting and claims management
you have access to the information needed to review policies, processes, and risk models in advance of implementation
you are able to assess commonhold developments, including large and complex buildings, using clear and reliable information about governance, maintenance, and financial arrangements
you are equipped to develop insurance products that meet the needs of commonhold unit-holders, Commonhold Associations, and directors
For legal professionals, it is our intention that:
you have the information you need to advise clients on commonhold ownership, governance, and transactions, as well as on conversions of existing leasehold buildings
you have access to legislation all in one place, clear guidance and standard documentation to support commonhold-related legal work and help clients navigate and resolve disputes arising within the commonhold framework
you are able to advise Commonhold Associations, directors, lenders, developers and unit owners on their rights, responsibilities, and obligations
you are supported to adopt consistent and efficient processes for commonhold transactions and conversions, benefitting from standard governance documentation including the Commonhold Community Statement (or CCS) and Articles of Association
For property professionals, it is our intention that:
you have the information and guidance needed to effectively advise clients on the practical implications of buying, owning, managing and selling commonhold properties, as well as converting existing leasehold properties to commonhold
you have access to clear guidance and industry standards to support the management of commonhold developments, including the RICS-developed Commonhold Code of Practice
you are supported to develop consistent approaches to the valuation of commonhold properties and assessment of commonhold-related risks
you have information on how budgets, reserve funds and long-term maintenance planning operate within a commonhold structure, and are equipped to support Commonhold Associations, directors and unit-holders in meeting their respective responsibilities regarding the management and governance of the building, including any additional obligations such as those around building safety
For registered providers of social housing (RPs), it is our intention that:
you have access to clear information about how commonhold will operate within affordable and mixed-tenure developments, and how providers’ wider obligations can be upheld within the commonhold framework
you have the information you need about the roles, rights and responsibilities of registered providers within a commonhold structure, including where you retain an interest in units through shared ownership arrangements, and can adapt policies, systems, legal documentation, and operational processes where necessary
you are equipped to support shared owners and other residents in understanding their rights and responsibilities within a commonhold
you are able to participate effectively in Commonhold Associations and fulfil any associated duties and obligations
