Brothers sentenced for illegal waste wood fire risk site
CLC Utilities Limited and its operators, brothers Gary Paul Cook, 46, of Hurworth Place in Darlington, and John Philip Cook, 40, of Heron Crescent, Newcastle-Upon-Tyne, appeared at Newcastle Crown Court on 18 November for sentencing.
The brothers and the company had previously pleaded guilty in May 2025 to operating the site without an environmental permit.
It followed an investigation by the Environment Agency.
After the brothers contested the facts of the offence, a hearing was needed to determine both how much waste had been stored, and how culpable the brothers had been in accumulating the waste.
Court ruling
The court ruled that the company had illegally stored an estimated 8,000 tonnes of waste wood at its site at Davy Bank in Wallsend and continued operating even after the Environment Agency told them to stop.
The brothers were found to have deliberately allowed this accumulation to happen.
They were each sentenced to a 12-month community order requiring the completion of 160 hours unpaid work by Gary Cook, and 140 hours by John Cook.
As part of the government’s commitment to tackling waste crime, the Environment Agency is stepping up its action as part of its 10 point plan
Gary Wallace, Area Environment Manager for the Environment Agency in the North East, said:
The company and Cook brothers operated with excessive volumes of waste over a prolonged period, creating a significant fire risk and putting other businesses in the area in jeopardy.
They persistently flouted the law, which is there to protect people and the environment.
As part of our 10 point plan we’re taking faster, more targeted action on waste criminals and this case shows where there are breaches we will take action.
Images shows the wood waste stored at the site.
Estimated 8,000 tonnes of waste stored
The court heard the company was incorporated on 8 March, 2018. It accepted waste wood, which would then be pulverised and processed to create wood fines ready to supply chip board manufacturers and the biomass industry.
The company had registered a waste exemption with the Environment Agency, which allowed for low risk waste activity.
When Environment Agency officers attended the site in June 2021 to check compliance with the exemption it found an estimated 8,000 tonnes of waste wood being stored illegally in a way that posed a significant fire risk.
Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service warned that if the waste wood was ignited, the resulting fire would have affected neighbouring homes and businesses close to Davy Bank.
The company was told to respond to a list of remedial actions by the end of August 2021, but no response was received, and the waste exemption was deregistered.
Company failed to stop operating
When continuously told they were not in compliance and to stop operating, the company continued to operate, also failing to provide any formal account for their offending after refusing to attend an interview under caution.
To tackle waste crime at its source, the Government is increasing the use of drones, giving Mayors and the police a bigger role, and introducing a dramatic rise in fines to deter those who dump illegally.
The Government is also giving the Environment Agency stronger powers to punish those who break the law. Those who dump waste illegally or fail to treat it correctly are set to face fines of up to £5,000, up from the current maximum of £300, while those responsible for serious offences can already face much tougher sanctions, including prosecution and imprisonment.
Full charges
CLC Utilities
Between 09 June 2021 and 07 October 2022 at Davy Bank, Wallsend, CLC Utilities Ltd operated a regulated facility, namely a waste operation for the recovery and disposal of waste wood, otherwise than in accordance with an environmental permit or other authorisation for the facility.
Contrary to Regulations 12(1)(a) and 38(1)(a) Environmental Permitting (England & Wales) Regulations 2016
Gary Cook and John Cook
Between 09 June 2021 and 07 October 2022 at Davy Bank, Wallsend, CLC Utilities Ltd operated a regulated facility, namely a waste operation for the recovery and disposal of waste wood, otherwise than in accordance with an environmental permit or other authorisation for the facility and as an officer of that body corporate or a person purporting to act in that capacity, the offence occurred as a consequence of your consent, connivance or neglect.
Contrary to Regulations 12(1)(a), 38(1)(a) and 41(1) Environmental Permitting (England & Wales) Regulations 2016
