Army buys hundreds more drones and electronic warfare kit in £25 million boost to strengthen Britain’s defences on NATO’s eastern flank
£25 million investment in drones and cutting-edge battlefield technology will help keep Britain secure as threats evolve and boost deterrence
Hundreds more attack and reconnaissance drones bought to bolster UK forces protecting NATO’s eastern flank, stepping up warfighting readiness
Lessons from Ukraine are rapidly transforming into new capabilities, ensuring British forces have the kit needed to deter and defeat future threats
Britain’s Armed Forces will be better equipped to counter the rapidly changing threats facing the UK and its allies, following a £25 million boost in drones and cutting-edge battlefield technology.
The funding from the Defence Investment Plan will equip British forces protecting NATO’s eastern flank in Estonia with hundreds of additional one-way attack and first-person view drones, advanced electronic warfare systems and modern battlefield protection. It will also double the number of intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance drones available to troops, helping them identify threats and locate enemy forces more quickly and effectively.
The investment will also support the Army’s developing ‘Recce-Strike’ approach, bringing together reconnaissance, intelligence and precision attack capabilities so British forces can detect threats, make decisions and strike at greater range and speed.
Defence Secretary Wes Streeting MP said:
This investment will increase our deterrence on NATO’s front line, with our troops in Estonia being kitted out with the latest drones and capabilities. It will mean our Army can move faster, be more agile and strike the enemy harder. And it shows how we’re investing today to step up our deterrence and readiness for tomorrow.
The war in Ukraine has shown how quickly the battlefield is changing, with comparatively low-cost drones, electronic warfare and the ability to detect and strike enemy forces rapidly transforming modern combat. Investing now in the technology and skills needed to meet these threats will help strengthen Britain’s security, deter aggression and ensure our Armed Forces stay ahead of our adversaries.
The new equipment will also help protect British troops from the growing threat posed by enemy drones. New electronic warfare technology, carried by soldiers and fitted to vehicles, will help troops detect hostile drones and disrupt them before they can pose a threat.
General Sir Roly Walker, Chief of the General Staff, said:
Ukraine has shown that the side which can see first, strike first and protect itself best has a significant advantage in modern war. We are acting on those lessons now.
This investment delivers more drones, more electronic warfare capability and better protection for our soldiers, making the British Army harder to find, harder to defeat and more lethal when it matters most. That’s what it takes to be ready to fight and win alongside our NATO Allies.
The investment includes:
Hundreds of additional one-way attack and First-Person View drones which can be used to can be used to strike vehicles, positions or equipment, alongside a doubling of Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance drones to find and track threats.
Counter-drone capabilities include body-worn electronic warfare systems and vehicle-mounted equipment capable of scanning for electronic signals and additional shotguns. This will give troops several layers of protection against drones and help British forces continue to move and fight in an increasingly contested battlefield.
Multi-spectral camouflage designed to reduce the detectability of personnel. This will make troops more difficult for an enemy to detect using modern surveillance technology including thermal sensors. This will help British forces remain concealed for longer and reduce their vulnerability to enemy surveillance and attack.
Additional lightweight 4x4 utility vehicles to give troops greater freedom to move quickly around the battlefield and operate in smaller, more dispersed groups. This will make British forces less predictable and more difficult for an enemy to locate and target.
Taken together, the new equipment will allow troops to find threats more quickly, remain harder to detect and strike an enemy faster when required.
By April 2027, 4th Light Brigade will be equipped with the new capabilities, strengthening the lethality and protection of British forces committed to NATO’s eastern flank and ensuring troops are equipped to respond to the threats they are most likely to face.
The investment reflects a faster approach to defence procurement, turning technology and lessons from current conflicts into equipment for frontline troops more quickly.
The programme will help ensure British forces can fight effectively alongside NATO allies, protect themselves against emerging threats and deliver decisive combat power when required, strengthening the UK’s contribution to the defence of the Alliance.
Notes to editors
The £25 million funding forms part of a £50 million Defence Investment Plan allocation for rapid investment in transformative Army lethality, under Project RAPSTONE.
The Army’s recce-strike approach integrates reconnaissance, surveillance, decision-making and precision strike capabilities to reduce the time between detecting and engaging an enemy.
By April 2027, 4th Light Brigade is planned to be equipped with the new capabilities.
British forces deployed to Estonia contribute to NATO’s deterrence and defence posture on the Alliance’s eastern flank.
